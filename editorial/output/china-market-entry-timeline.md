# China market entry timeline: the realistic six months on social

Six months is a realistic China market entry timeline for a social-first launch.
The accounts are not the slow part. The trademark is, so it starts the day the
board says yes.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

It's tempting to draw a China market entry timeline with "open accounts" in
month one and "launch" in month two. The accounts part is right. Tencent reviews
an overseas company's WeChat (微信) file in 7 to 15 working days. What that plan
leaves out is the name.

China's trademark office, CNIPA (国家知识产权局), takes about four months just
to examine a filing. Putting your brand name on a verified account, and later on
your ads, depends on that filing. So the real plan runs three tracks side by
side, and the legal one sets the date.

| Month | Track 1: legal and trademark                | Track 2: accounts                  | Track 3: localization and content |
|-------|---------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| 0     | Name search, filings in English and Chinese | Documents gathered, region checked | Chinese name chosen               |
| 1     | Examination running                         | WeChat and Xiaohongshu filed       | Profiles and pillars drafted      |
| 2     | Examination running                         | Accounts verified on company name  | First content built               |
| 3     | Examination running                         | Ad account invitation requested    | A month of content banked         |
| 4     | Examination ends on average                 | Full calendar live                 | Soft launch                       |
| 5     | Publication and opposition window           | Paid tests, first seeding          | Content adjusted to search data   |
| 6     | Registration still pending in most cases    | Board read-out                     | Second content cycle              |

Every wait below comes from a regulator or a platform, and each one is cited
where it appears. All sources were checked twice in October 2026, and the plan
assumes a board decision in month 0.

<!-- SECTION: The three tracks -->

## The three tracks behind a China market entry timeline

A China launch on social is three projects with different clocks.

The legal track runs on statute and moves slowly, in months, with deadlines
written into the Trademark Law. Accounts move in working days, as long as the
paperwork is exactly what each platform asks for (and each one asks for
something slightly different). Localization is the track your own team controls,
which is why it's usually the one that slips.

But they're linked. An account can go live in weeks on your registered company
name. Putting the brand name on it, and running ads under it, waits on the
trademark.

That's why the trademark moves first, even though nothing public happens on it
for months.

<!-- SECTION: Track 1 -->

## Track 1: legal and trademark, months 0 to 4

It needs a name search and a filing plan. It also needs a Chinese name, and
foreign companies can't file alone.

> A foreign person or foreign company applying to register a trademark in China
> must go through a lawfully established trademark agency.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration (国家知识产权局), Trademark Law, 2019 amendment, July 2019. https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2019/7/30/art_95_28179.html

China registers the first filer, not the first user. Under the law, when two
companies file similar marks for similar goods, the earlier filing wins.

Squatters know it.

> China struck down more than 200,000 bad-faith trademark filings in the first
> half of 2023, and again in the first half of 2024.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration (国家知识产权局), reposting Xinhua (新华网), July 2026. https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2026/7/2/art_55_207044.html

Then the clock. The law gives the office nine months to examine, and it has been
running well under that.

> China's average trademark examination period held steady at four months.
> Source: People's Daily (人民日报), report on the State Council Information Office press conference, July 2026. https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202607/30/content_30171963.html

Four months is examination only. Publication and an opposition window follow.
Today the window is three months, and the revised law cuts it to two.

> The revised law gives opponents two months from publication and keeps the
> nine-month examination limit. It refuses filings made with no intent to use
> that clearly exceed normal business needs. It takes effect January 1, 2027.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration (国家知识产权局), Trademark Law, 2026 revision, June 2026. https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2026/6/26/art_95_206942.html

Which window applies depends on the publication date. On the four-month average,
a mark filed in October 2026 would be published early in 2027. CNIPA's draft
transition rules would give it the shorter window.

> Under the draft, marks published before January 1, 2027 keep the old
> three-month opposition period, and later ones follow the revised law. The
> draft is open for comment until November 12.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration (国家知识产权局), draft transition measures for the revised Trademark Law, September 2026. https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2026/9/28/art_75_208337.html

The whole track, step by step:

| Step                        | Time limit in law                            | What it takes in practice               |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Filing through an agency    | No limit                                     | Days, once the name is cleared          |
| Examination                 | 9 months                                     | About 4 months on average               |
| Publication and opposition  | 3 months, 2 if published from 2027           | Fixed                                   |
| Registration, no opposition | After the window closes                      | About 7 months in a typical case (2023) |
| Refusal review, if refused  | Request within 15 days, decision in 9 months | About 7 months (2023)                   |
| Opposition, if filed        | Decision within 12 months                    | About 10 months (2023)                  |

> In 2023, a typical registration took seven months. Oppositions averaged 10
> months, and refusal reviews seven.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration Trademark Office (国家知识产权局商标局), 2023 work results, January 2024. https://sbj.cnipa.gov.cn/gzdt/2026/0828/50967.html

The 2023 figures are the latest CNIPA has published for those steps.

Some brands extend an existing international registration to China through the
Madrid system instead. That route has its own clock.

> When China is designated through the Madrid system, the office has 18 months
> to refuse if the home office is party to the Protocol only. The limit is 12
> months if it is party to both the Agreement and the Protocol.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration Trademark Office (国家知识产权局商标局), Madrid FAQ, October 2022. https://sbj.cnipa.gov.cn/gjzn/2026/0830/53720.html

So what does the trademark actually hold up? Each platform gives a different
answer.

> Naming a verified WeChat account after a trademark requires an R mark
> registered with China's trademark office. Marks registered abroad cannot be
> used. A mark still under application can only go through the platform's
> self-chosen naming route.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), WeChat verification trademark naming rules, platform documentation, confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/touch/sappfaq/150722EVNrui150722zUVVNR.html

> A foreign trademark on a Douyin enterprise account needs a Chinese
> registration certificate or a Madrid registration certificate.
> Source: Douyin Enterprise Account Help Center (抖音企业号帮助中心), verification information rules, July 2024. https://renzheng.douyin.com/support/content/141500?graphId=412&mappingType=2&spaceId=77

Xiaohongshu (小红书) is more flexible. Its verification rules, quoted in the
next section, accept a trademark certificate or an acceptance notice that meets
platform requirements. A foreign mark can use its foreign registration
certificate. Its ad platform, Juguang (聚光), wants the filing to be at least
three months old.

> On Xiaohongshu's Juguang ad platform, a mark still under application needs the
> acceptance notice. The application must be at least three months past its
> acceptance date.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书), Juguang registration and account opening guide, May 2022. https://fe-video-qc.xhscdn.com/fe-platform/cc8a5f81ddb6d91d6be9dfb34e62b49b999f234b/%E8%81%9A%E5%85%89%E5%B9%B3%E5%8F%B0%E6%B3%A8%E5%86%8C%E4%B8%8E%E5%BC%80%E6%88%B7%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B.pdf

So if the ad account carries the brand name, timing matters. A filing made in
month 0 passes the three-month mark in month 3. Wait until month 2 to file and
the first branded ads can't run before month 5.

## Track 2: platform accounts and verification, months 1 to 3

The accounts are quick once the papers are right.

WeChat (微信) is the most predictable.

> An overseas company's WeChat verification is reviewed within 7 to 15 working
> days of a complete file. The fee is 99 US dollars per application, charged
> whether or not it passes.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), overseas entity verification FAQ, platform documentation, confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/190723aQvMR31907233IF7FV.html

> An overseas entity can register one WeChat Official Account, and only as a
> service account. It can be used once verification succeeds.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), overseas entity service account registration, platform documentation, confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/180312zQjeYV180312FNNbmA.html



Xiaohongshu (小红书) updated its verification rules in October 2026. Check your
home country before you plan around them.

> Xiaohongshu verifies overseas companies according to the regions and
> industries it has opened. Its rules list mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore,
> and Malaysia as opened regions. Review generally takes three working days,
> longer for complex files, extra documents, or holidays.
> Source: Xiaohongshu Juguang help center (小红书聚光帮助中心), enterprise professional account verification rules, updated October 2026. https://ad.xiaohongshu.com/next_help/docs/195c5fe505c71b4b0335a2fe0d61d8e0

> Xiaohongshu enterprise verification costs 600 yuan per application, and each
> verification order expires after 30 days. Overseas companies apply on the
> desktop site.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书), enterprise account verification guide, platform document, confirmed October 2026. https://fe-video-qc.xhscdn.com/picasso-editor/7072d509ee7370545a056b6f9030a926e8879dca.pdf

A company registered in another country should confirm its region is open before
the plan is set. If your group owns a Hong Kong company, that company is in a
named region. Settle which entity applies in month 0, not month 2.

Douyin (抖音) has its own route now.

> Douyin's enterprise help center lists a self-service onboarding guide for Hong
> Kong, Macau, Taiwan, and overseas companies, updated October 13, 2026.
> Source: Douyin Enterprise Account Help Center (抖音企业号帮助中心), October 2026. https://renzheng.douyin.com/support/content/227771?graphId=412&mappingType=2&spaceId=77

Its documents and fee sit behind a login. The service agreement does say when
review starts.

> Douyin starts reviewing an enterprise verification within 15 working days of a
> complete submission.
> Source: Douyin Enterprise Account Help Center (抖音企业号帮助中心), enterprise verification service agreement, January 2026. https://renzheng.douyin.com/support/content/123631?graphId=412&mappingType=2&spaceId=77

Weibo (微博) is the odd one out. It works with overseas companies through an
overseas agent.

> Weibo directs overseas enterprise accounts to the overseas agent that served
> them at verification.
> Source: Sina Weibo customer service (新浪微博客服), enterprise entity change rules, platform documentation, confirmed October 2026. https://kefu.weibo.com/faqdetail?id=21369

Our own published timelines sit inside these limits.

> Most clients go from signed contract to a live, publishing WeChat account in
> about four weeks.
> Source: TheRedScroll, WeChat platform page, confirmed October 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/platforms/wechat/

> China Market Entry: live in 30 days, with accounts verified and first content
> published.
> Source: TheRedScroll, China Market Entry service page, confirmed October 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/services/market-entry/

Those are planning figures, not measured averages, and they rest on the platform
clocks above.

## Track 3: brand localization and content, months 1 to 4

Nobody outside your company sets this clock.

It starts with the Chinese name, which has to exist in month 0 because the
trademark filing needs it. Our advice is to file the Chinese name as its own
mark alongside the Latin one. In our experience, choosing it is the step most
likely to stall. The law does not require it, but first-to-file applies to
Chinese names too.

Then come the profiles, the content pillars, and the first month of posts. Our
first 90 days guide covers that work week by week. One rule matters most here.
Build a month of content before the soft launch, so the calendar doesn't stall
once it starts.

Some categories add a regulator to this track.

> An ordinary cosmetic is filed once the documents are submitted. A special
> cosmetic needs registration, with a 90-working-day technical review that
> restarts when extra documents are requested.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (国家市场监督管理总局), cosmetics registration and filing measures, Order 35, January 2021. https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-01/12/content_5579239.htm

> An overseas cosmetics registrant or filer must appoint a legal entity inside
> China to handle registration and filing.
> Source: State Council (国务院), Cosmetics Supervision and Administration Regulation, June 2020. https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2020-06/29/content_5522593.htm

Food, supplements, and medical devices have their own rules. If you sell one of
those, the regulator, not the platform, can set your launch date.

That's the setup. Months 4 to 6 are where the plan meets the public.

<!-- SECTION: Month 4 -->

## Month 4: the soft launch, and what it has to prove

By month 4 the accounts are verified, a month of content is banked, and the
trademark examination is ending. The soft launch is the full calendar going live
without paid support.

What should it prove? First, that the content pillars earn saves and comments,
not just views. Then that some notes pull readers from search rather than only
from the feed, which is the sign an account has started to compound. And
messages from the profile should reach a named person and get an answer the same
day.

No platform publishes a benchmark for a brand account in its fourth month. Judge
the soft launch against its own first weeks.

<!-- SECTION: Month 5 -->

## Month 5: paid testing and the first creator seeding

Paid tests go behind the notes that already earned search traffic. Seeding
starts with small creators who post about the product in their own words, booked
through the creator marketplace of Xiaohongshu (小红书), Pugongying (蒲公英).

One catch for an overseas company: the ad account on Juguang (聚光),
Xiaohongshu's ad platform, is not self-serve.

> On Xiaohongshu's Juguang ad platform, every industry is by invitation for
> overseas companies, with no self-service entry. Applicants go through the
> platform's sales or operations team. Qualification review is expected to take
> 48 hours.
> Source: Xiaohongshu Juguang help center (小红书聚光帮助中心), cross-border business admission rules, updated February 2026. https://ad.xiaohongshu.com/next_help/docs/27223a333b21567c31a8c7c8a60fbddb

So the invitation request goes in during month 3, while the content bank is
still being built. We bill ad spend at cost, with no markup, and our pricing
page lists what the work around it costs.

## Month 6: what a realistic result looks like

Month 6 is too early to judge return on seeding. The marketing chief of
Xiaohongshu (小红书) has given examples of how long payback takes.

> Xiaohongshu's chief marketing officer said a robot vacuum brand's seeding
> return turned at about 45 to 60 days. Buyers of a body oil the brand thought
> was a seven-day decision often took 60 to 90 days.
> Source: Huxiu (虎嗅), via Sina (新浪), report on Xiaohongshu's WILL conference, December 2024. https://finance.sina.com.cn/wm/2024-12-20/doc-ineacsym5596846.shtml

On those cycles, seeding that starts in month 5 may only pay back in month 7 or
8. A realistic month 6 shows verified accounts and a steady calendar. It shows
search traffic on the best notes, a cost per read from the paid test, and the
first inbound leads. In a typical case it won't yet show a registered trademark
either.

## The five things that most often push the date

**An incomplete file.** The 7 to 15 working days on WeChat (微信) only start
when the file is complete. A verification order on Xiaohongshu (小红书) lapses
after 30 days. One missing letter, and the wait starts over.

**A name conflict.** A refusal gives you 15 days to ask for a review, and
reviews averaged seven months in 2023. An opposition averaged ten. If a squatter
already holds your name on WeChat, there is a complaint route.

> A trademark holder can complain about an account using its name. WeChat
> reviews the complaint within seven working days, the other side has seven days
> to appeal, and the name is released if the appeal fails.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), WeChat Official Account infringement complaints, platform documentation, confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/120911VrYVrA161026ammAjA.html

**A regulated category.** Cosmetics, food, and supplements can add months that
no agency can shorten.

**Platform access.** Xiaohongshu's open regions and Juguang's invitation step
both decide who can apply, not just how fast.

Then there's **the calendar**, China's and your own. Reviews run in working
days, and China's long holidays stop them.

> In 2026, China's Spring Festival holiday ran nine days, February 15 to 23, and
> National Day ran seven, October 1 to 7.
> Source: State Council General Office (国务院办公厅), 2026 public holiday notice, November 2025. https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202511/content_7047090.htm

Your own calendar does as much damage. A naming decision that sits with a global
brand team for six weeks moves every track by six weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does China market entry take?

For a social-first launch, plan on about six months from board approval to a
running launch with paid tests and the first leads. The accounts can be verified
in the first month or two. The trademark is slower: examination averages four
months, then an opposition window, and a typical registration took seven months
in 2023. Regulated categories can add more.

### Do I need a trademark before launching in China?

Not to open accounts. They can launch on your registered company name. You need
a filing to use the brand name well. Tencent's naming rules let a verified
WeChat (微信) account carry a trademark only once it's registered in China, and
Douyin (抖音) wants a registration certificate. Xiaohongshu (小红书) ads accept
a pending mark three months after acceptance. File in month 0.

### What can go wrong with a China launch timeline?

Five things, mostly. An incomplete document file. A name conflict or a squatter.
A regulated product category. Platform access rules, such as the open regions on
Xiaohongshu (小红书). And the calendar, from Spring Festival to slow
headquarters approvals. Most of these are settled, or not, in month 0.
