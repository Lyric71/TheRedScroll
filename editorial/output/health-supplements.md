# Health supplement marketing in China: start with the route

Health supplement marketing in China starts with product classification and the
sales route. Cross-border access can remove an import requirement. It doesn’t
make a health claim legal.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

An overseas supplement label is not a Chinese advertising brief. Before booking
creators, establish what the product is, how it will enter China, and which
claims its record supports.

| Decision | General-trade health food | Eligible cross-border retail import |
|---|---|---|
| Product entry | Registration or qualifying filing | Positive-list and personal-use conditions |
| Sales scope | Domestic distribution after required approvals | Consumer’s personal use; no resale |
| Claim basis | Registered or filed product record | Import relief does not approve health claims |
| First marketing check | Product record and advertising approval | Product classification and channel acceptance |

If you’re entering China, resolve those checks before setting a campaign date.
If you already sell there, compare your store listing with the claims in creator
posts. A compliant import route won’t fix a misleading ad.

Sources checked October 25, 2026. The channel plan below is editorial guidance,
not clearance for a specific product or advertisement.

## Health supplement marketing in China has separate entry checks

The Blue Hat (蓝帽子) is China’s health-food mark. “Supplement” on a foreign bottle
does not settle its Chinese classification. Ordinary food, health food, and
medicine have different rules. This guide covers food supplements, not drugs or
infant formula. General trade means importing for domestic distribution, rather
than a qualifying personal-use retail order.

> First-time imported health foods generally require registration. Qualifying
> vitamin and mineral supplements use filing instead.
> Source: Food Safety Law (食品安全法), Article 76, amended September 2025. https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=104105

Filing is a defined route with conditions, not a shortcut any brand can choose.
Check the applicable ingredient catalog and product form with a qualified
regulatory specialist. Give that person the full formula, overseas label, and
intended Chinese claim before commissioning the creative brief. Ordinary food
cannot borrow the health-function claims allowed for regulated health foods.

Don’t turn an administrative deadline into a launch promise.

> Technical review has a 60-working-day period, with a possible 20-working-day
> extension. Supplementary materials restart that review clock. Inspection,
> verification testing, and re-review time are excluded from specified limits.
> Source: China Food and Drug Administration, Health Food Registration and Filing Measures, Articles 16, 19, and 27, February 2016. https://www.samr.gov.cn/zt/ndzt/2019n/bjspjsqjxcjwljxyjsckpxc/zcfg/art/2023/art_fca01dd2bbed437282f7ffd4d056f481.html

Those are parts of the process. They exclude the work needed to assemble a
complete application. An actual project schedule also needs testing,
translations, and any overseas inspection requirements. The regulation leaves
overseas inspection and verification-test timing to the manufacturer’s
circumstances.

The other route is cross-border e-commerce retail import (跨境电商零售进口). It has its
own conditions.

> Qualifying cross-border retail imports for personal use are exempt from
> first-import licensing, registration, or filing requirements. Consumers
> may not resell the goods.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce (商务部) and five other departments, Notice 486, November 2018. https://cws.mofcom.gov.cn/gztz/art/2018/art_b7becbacb58a4d0d8bf17b91a168f044.html

## What each route lets you say

For a registered or filed health food, start with its registration or filing
documents and product instructions. An ingredient study cannot expand the
approved claims of your finished product.

> Health-food ads must stay within the registered or filed record and product
> instructions. They must prominently show the required warning, health-food
> mark, and suitable and unsuitable groups.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (国家市场监督管理总局), Order 21, Article 7, December 2019. https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_4ca076ac0eeb4a9e9204e0cee26e359b.html

The warning explains that health foods are not medicines and cannot replace
medicine to treat disease. It does not cancel out an unlawful promise above it.
Product approval and approval of an advertisement are separate steps. The
required ad review comes before publication, including online.

For cross-border products, keep the customs exemption in its proper place. It
does not give an overseas “supports immunity” line automatic permission in a
Chinese ad. Confirm the product’s classification and the applicable advertising
requirements before translating a claim.

| Proposed content | Treatment | What the editor needs |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate origin, ingredients, and pack size | Factual information, subject to context | Product documents; no implied health result |
| A registered health function | Restricted | Exact product record and required ad approval |
| “Prevents diabetes” | Prohibited for food advertising | Remove the disease claim |
| “Guaranteed to work” | Prohibited in health-food ads | Remove the guarantee |
| A creator’s personal recommendation | Prohibited as health-food ad endorsement | Replace the endorsement-led brief |
| Overseas health claim on a cross-border item | Not cleared by customs status | Product-specific regulatory review |

These are editing decisions, not preapproved copy. A factual sentence can still
become misleading through its headline, illustration, or surrounding sales
pitch.

## Claims that can stop a campaign

The problem is often the promised outcome, not one banned word. Replacing
“treats insomnia” with a wink about throwing away sleeping pills leaves the same
message.

> Health-food advertising may not promise efficacy or safety, claim disease
> prevention or treatment, imply the product is essential for health, or use
> endorsers to recommend it.
> Source: Advertising Law (广告法), Article 18, amended April 2021. https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_5474cf75173c45d6a0379730fb4e8d97.html

Read the cover image with the caption. Then play the video with subtitles on. A
host may promise something the approved script never said. Read the pinned
comments and destination page before signing off on the whole ad.

Current enforcement reaches beyond conventional ads.

> The regulator’s April 2026 enforcement notice targets false food marketing,
> including ordinary-food health claims, exaggerated health-food effects,
> fabricated reviews, and misleading recommendations.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation, online food sales enforcement notice, April 2026. https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/tssps/art/2026/art_6634fa5970974be2b469f49a269b9578.html

A platform accepting an upload is not evidence that the claim is lawful. Keep
the approved wording and the published version together so the team can identify
what changed when a complaint arrives.

## Where to study buyers’ questions

Start your research on Xiaohongshu (小红书), also called RedNote. Search the
Chinese ingredient name alongside questions about the label and format. Read
what people ask, without treating personal testimonials as clinical evidence.
Our RedNote agency page explains the platform work.

Use Douyin (抖音) to study the questions that surface during product
demonstrations. Review WeChat (微信) customer-service questions for gaps in the
information you already provide. These are proposed research roles, not a
ranking of supplement sales by platform.

Market estimates need a clear category definition.

> iiMedia Research estimates China’s broad health-products market at
> 377.5 billion yuan in 2025. Its category is 保健品, not a separately
> measured market for your supplement type.
> Source: iiMedia Research (艾媒咨询), January 2026. https://www.iimedia.cn/c1094/109207.html

Use that estimate as context. It does not tell you demand for a particular
formula, its legal route, or what an order will cost to acquire.

<!-- SECTION: Trust -->

## Build trust with information people can check

Start with the pack. A buyer should be able to read its contents and origin
without hunting through promotional images. Where a product record applies,
explain how to check it. Storage instructions belong beside the product
information, with a clear way to reach customer support.

A laboratory photo does not establish that the advertised product works.

Keep general health education separate from a disguised sales pitch.

> Internet rules prohibit disguised health-food ads presented as wellness
> education. Such education must not share a page or appear simultaneously
> with related seller contact details or shopping links.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation, Internet Advertising Measures, Article 8, February 2023. https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_d93a579afd45413e8576e4623fab348f.html

Removing the shopping link alone does not establish that a post is lawful.
Review its commercial purpose and full presentation. A doctor’s costume, patient
story, or dramatic before-and-after sequence can change what the viewer
understands.

For planning, keep a claim sheet beside the content calendar. Record the
proposed wording, evidence, product record, reviewer, and approved asset
version. When a claim is refused, remove it from every brief that repeats it.

## KOC seeding needs a different brief here

A key opinion consumer, or KOC, is a small creator whose appeal often rests on
personal experience. That appeal creates a problem for health-food ads: the law
prohibits endorsers, including ordinary people making recommendations.

Do not commission “this fixed my sleep” posts and assume a modest follower count
makes them acceptable. An advertising label does not legalize a prohibited
endorsement. Nor does gifting the product settle the issue.

Creators can produce assets without becoming product endorsers. Define that role
before agreeing on volume. Producing an approved product image differs from
recommending the product under the creator’s own name. Have the final format
checked before paying for distribution.

> Health-food internet ads require prior review and must follow the approved
> content. Linked promotional reviews must be identifiable as advertisements.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation, Internet Advertising Measures, Articles 7 and 9, February 2023. https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_d93a579afd45413e8576e4623fab348f.html

For a cross-border item, get a product-specific answer before commissioning
testimonials. The store’s ability to take an order is not that answer.

## Check the cross-border channel before buying traffic

The positive list names goods eligible for this route, subject to its
conditions. Begin with the exact product and its customs classification.
“Supplements are allowed” is too broad to approve a range.

> The 2022 positive-list adjustment changed tariff codes and product remarks
> as well as adding goods. It took effect on March 1, 2022.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce, cross-border retail import list announcement, February 2022. https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2022/art_7256ee87a5b54b8f9660b4f9dfce848c.html

Confirm the applicable list entry and restrictions, then the store’s current
category requirements. Choose a fulfillment route that can produce the required
customs information. Check the applicable purchase limits and tax conditions as
well. A social account alone does not do this.

Before sending traffic, check the customer journey yourself. Can the buyer
understand the product information, delivery route, and return process? Who
answers when the package differs from the domestic version they expected?

> The cross-border notice requires a consumer risk notice and provides for
> Chinese electronic labels where goods lack Chinese labels. Sellers retain
> quality, recall, and consumer-protection duties.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce and five other departments, Notice 486, November 2018. https://cws.mofcom.gov.cn/gztz/art/2018/art_b7becbacb58a4d0d8bf17b91a168f044.html

The customer must accept the risk notice before ordering. Include that checkout
step in the launch plan, along with the ban on resale.

## FAQ

### Do I need Blue Hat registration to sell supplements in China?

Not every product follows the same route. General-trade health foods usually
need registration or qualifying filing. Eligible cross-border retail imports for
personal use receive specific first-import exemptions. Establish the product’s
Chinese classification before choosing a route. A foreign supplement label alone
does not establish eligibility or permission to make health claims.

### What health claims are allowed in Chinese advertising?

For health foods, stay within the registered or filed product record and obtain
the required advertising approval. Disease prevention and treatment claims,
efficacy guarantees, and endorsements are prohibited. Ordinary food cannot claim
health functions. Cross-border import relief does not approve overseas marketing
language. Review the full ad, including images and links.

### Can I sell supplements in China through cross-border e-commerce?

Potentially, if the exact product meets the current positive-list conditions and
the retail-import rules. Confirm customs classification, platform admission, and
consumer disclosures before launching. The goods are for personal use and cannot
be resold. This route does not automatically permit domestic distribution or
approve health claims in Chinese marketing.
