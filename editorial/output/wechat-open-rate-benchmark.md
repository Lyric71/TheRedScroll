# WeChat open rate benchmark: what under 3% really means

For a brand's WeChat (微信) service account, 2.8% to 4.1% was an average open
rate in 2022, and four in ten posts in that study already fell below 2.8%.
Under 3% is common. It becomes a problem when your total reads fall with it.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

You publish every week. The open rate keeps sliding. Your agency says the
whole platform is down, and you suspect that's an excuse.

It's partly true. A WeChat (微信) open rate benchmark only counts reads that
come from the push to your followers. WeChat now sends a growing share of
readers through recommendation and sharing, and those reads never touch the
open rate.

Here is the largest public benchmark for brand accounts we could find, built
on about 15,000 brand posts. Foreign companies can only open a service account, so
read the right-hand column.

| How the post performed | Former subscription account | Service account |
|------------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------|
| Poor (bottom 20%)      | Under 0.7%                  | Under 1.5%      |
| Low                    | 0.7% to 1.3%                | 1.5% to 2.8%    |
| Average                | 1.3% to 2.2%                | 2.8% to 4.1%    |
| Good                   | 2.2% to 4%                  | 4.1% to 6.8%    |
| Great                  | 4% to 7.8%                  | 6.8% to 14.9%   |
| Excellent (top 5%)     | Above 7.8%                  | Above 14.9%     |

Bands from KAWO, December 2022, explained below. Every source on this page
was checked in October 2026.

Below: why the number fell, the five causes we see most often, and a 30-day
plan to fix them.

## The WeChat open rate benchmark for brand accounts

The table above comes from KAWO (科握), a Shanghai company that sells social
media software. It studied posts from brand accounts that use its tools.

> Across about 15,000 brand posts from January 2021 to July 2022, a service
> account post needed more than 4.1% to rank as good. Below 1.5% was poor.
> Averages are for April to July 2022.
> Source: KAWO (科握), December 2022. https://kawo.com/cn/%E5%8D%9A%E5%AE%A2/%E5%93%81%E7%89%8C%E5%85%AC%E4%BC%97%E5%8F%B7%E6%8E%A8%E6%96%87%E6%89%93%E5%BC%80%E7%8E%87%E5%9F%BA%E5%87%86%E7%BA%BF

Two details in that study matter more than the bands do.

First, KAWO defines open rate as reads from the push message divided by the
people the post was sent to. Reads from anywhere else are left out.

Second, follower count did not predict open rate. KAWO split accounts into
under 10,000, 10,000 to 100,000, and over 100,000 followers, and found no
reliable link.

> KAWO found no clear correlation between follower numbers and open rate,
> with too many other factors, such as posting frequency and time, in play.
> Source: KAWO, December 2022. https://kawo.com/en/blog/open-rate-benchmark-for-brands-wechat-oa

So a benchmark by follower band tells you less than one by account type.
Compare service accounts with service accounts, which is the right comparison
for a foreign brand anyway.

> Accounts registered by overseas entities can only be service accounts
> (服务号).
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/180312zQjeYV180312FNNbmA.html

Even by 2022 standards, under 3% was ordinary. The low band tops out at 2.8%
and covers the 20th to 40th percentile, so about four in ten service account
posts in the study already sat below it.

And the study's own trend line pointed down.

> Over the 18 months studied, open rates for both account types kept sliding
> slowly downward.
> Source: KAWO (科握), December 2022. https://kawo.com/cn/%E5%8D%9A%E5%AE%A2/%E5%93%81%E7%89%8C%E5%85%AC%E4%BC%97%E5%8F%B7%E6%8E%A8%E6%96%87%E6%89%93%E5%BC%80%E7%8E%87%E5%9F%BA%E5%87%86%E7%BA%BF

We found no newer public benchmark for brand accounts. Since 2022 the push
has also lost ground to recommendation (more on that below), so treat these
bands as a ceiling rather than a target you're failing. Nobody has published a
current benchmark by industry either; the only one we found dates from 2019,
names no original source, and stayed out of this page.

## Why open rates fell across WeChat

The audience did not leave WeChat (微信).

> WeChat and its international version had 1.418 billion combined monthly
> active accounts at the end of 2025, up 2% from a year earlier.
> Source: Tencent Holdings 2025 results, reported by Tencent News (腾讯新闻), March 2026. https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260318A07APT00

What changed is how articles reach people. First, the crowd: more
articles chase the same readers.

> Official Accounts published more than 444 million articles in 2024. About
> 307,800 of them passed 100,000 reads.
> Source: NewRank (新榜), January 2025. https://www.newrank.cn/article/detail/29573

Then the feed stopped showing posts in the order they went out.

> In 2021 the Official Account feed gained a recommended block and folded part
> of the content people had subscribed to. The timeline was broken up.
> Source: NewRank (新榜), March 2025. https://newrank.cn/article/detail/30626

Recommendation now rivals the push as a way in.

> On 36Kr's (36氪) consumer account, the share of reads from recommendation
> rose from 24.3% in August 2023 to 45.9% in March 2025, taking share from the
> push. The average open rate had been as high as 10% in 2017 and fell to 1% to
> 2% around 2020.
> Source: 36Kr, March 2025. https://eu.36kr.com/zh/p/3211329723614340

36Kr sums up WeChat's direction as "light on subscription, heavy on
recommendation." Think about what that does to your monthly report. A read from
recommendation, a forward or Search counts as a read. It doesn't count toward
open rate.

| Where the read comes from             | Counted in open rate | What tends to drive it            |
|---------------------------------------|----------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Push to followers (群发通知)          | Yes                  | The title, and who still follows  |
| Recommendation in the feed            | No                   | How readers engage with the piece |
| Friends' Recommend taps (推荐)        | No                   | Readers tapping Recommend         |
| Forwards to chats and Moments (朋友圈) | No                   | A reason to share                 |
| WeChat Search (搜一搜)                | No                   | Words people actually search for  |
| Your profile page                     | No                   | Articles published without a push |

So check total reads, in the account's own data panel, before you judge an
agency on open rate alone. If total reads hold while open rate falls, the
platform moved and your agency may have done nothing wrong. If both fall, read
on. The five causes below come roughly in the order we find them on accounts
we take over.

## Cause 1: you spend scarce pushes on the wrong posts

Teams love to test Tuesday against Thursday on WeChat (微信). It's rarely
worth the meeting. The weekday barely matters.

> In KAWO's study of 500,000 posts from August 2016 to July 2019, WeChat
> usage was consistent through the week and only a little lower at weekends.
> The strongest publishing windows were 5 pm and 11 pm.
> Source: KAWO (科握), October 2019. https://kawo.com/en/blog/2019-wechat-weibo-data-insights

The real timing problem for a foreign brand is scarcity.

> A service account (服务号) can switch on the follower push 4 times per
> calendar month. The former subscription account gets 1 a day.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/120911VrYVrA131025QniAfu.html

Four pushes a month means each one is a quarter of your monthly pushes.
Spend one on a routine notice and the open rate shows it.

There's a second route: publishing without the push. It has no limit,
but the article appears only on your profile page.

> Publishing with the push notice off has no limit. The article shows only on
> the account's profile page, not in followers' message lists.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/230913IjEVNN2309136nUZNZ.html

Use the four pushes for your strongest pieces and publish the rest quietly.
Followers who care will still find them on your profile.

<!-- SECTION: Cause 2, titles -->

## Cause 2: your titles are written for search, not for the feed

Search is a real source of followers on WeChat (微信).

> WeChat Search (搜一搜) reached 800 million monthly users in 2022 and
> brought in 27% of new Official Account followers.
> Source: WeChat Search team at WeChat Open Class PRO, reported by Sina Tech (新浪科技), January 2023. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2023-01-10/doc-imxztfeh2280083.shtml

But open rate is decided in the feed, where your title sits between a
friend's article and a news alert and has very little time to earn a tap. A title built from stacked keywords ("WeChat Marketing Guide 2026 China
Social Media Strategy") reads like a filing label.

Keep it short. WeChat doesn't leave much room anyway.

> A push title can run to 64 bytes, which is 32 Chinese characters.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/120911VrYVrA131025QniAfu.html

Then keep it honest. WeChat polices titles that overpromise.

> WeChat's platform team said it would act against titles that copy the tone
> of official notices, inflate urgency, or dodge review with spaces and rare
> characters.
> Source: WeChat Official Platform operations center, reported by Cailian Press (财联社), May 2024. https://www.cls.cn/detail/1690970

A good feed title names one specific thing the reader gets. Write it in
Chinese from the start. In our experience a translated English headline runs
long and reads flat.

<!-- SECTION: Cause 3, stale followers -->

## Cause 3: your follower base is stale

Open rate divides by everyone who still follows you. That includes the
visitor who scanned a QR code at a 2021 trade show and never opened a single
article.

On the old chronological feed, those followers at least saw your title.
Today WeChat (微信) folds part of what people subscribe to and slots in
recommended posts instead. A follower who never reads you is the easiest one
to lose in that shuffle.

Followers can pin you, though.

> A star (星标) on an account pins it to the top of the folded Official
> Account feed. In April 2025 WeChat added stars for single articles too.
> Source: Bianews (鞭牛士), reported by Tencent News (腾讯新闻), April 2025. https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20250404A02VOP00

Ask readers to star the account, once, in a piece they've just found useful.
Put the ask at the end of your best article, not in a banner on every post.

Then look at where followers came from. A lucky draw or a trade-show gift
builds a list of people who wanted the prize, not your articles.

You don't have to push to all of them every time.

> A push can be scheduled, or sent to one group of followers (分组群发) rather
> than the whole list.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/230913IjEVNN2309136nUZNZ.html

<!-- SECTION: Cause 4, reseeding -->

## Cause 4: you aren't reseeding through Channels or Moments

If the push reaches fewer people, bring readers in by other doors. Channels
(视频号), the short-video feed inside WeChat (微信), is one that's getting
busier.

> Channels total user time grew more than 20% year on year in 2025.
> Source: Tencent 2025 annual results, reported by China Fund News (中国基金报), March 2026. https://www.chnfund.com/article/AR1231fcdd-25ff-01cd-2836-3a2015e46b1d

A short Channels video can point to the article behind it. The video earns
reach on its own signals.

> The Channels team said share, like and comment rates carry high weight in
> how Channels recommends videos.
> Source: NewRank (新榜), March 2025. https://newrank.cn/article/detail/30626

Moments (朋友圈) ads are the paid door, and they can sell the follow
directly.

> Moments ads can promote an Official Account with a one-tap follow. Bidding
> buys start from a 1,000-yuan daily budget, in cities outside the core and
> key tiers. Opening the ad account is free.
> Source: Tencent Ads (腾讯广告), October 2026. https://e.qq.com/topic/marketing/industry/weixin/

Neither door lifts open rate directly, but both lift total reads. And the
followers they bring in chose your content, which helps the next push.

<!-- SECTION: Cause 5, forwarding -->

## Cause 5: your content has no reason to be forwarded

Forwards and Recommend taps are how an article escapes your follower list.
36Kr reports that in January 2025 WeChat (微信) turned the old "Wow" button
(在看) into "Recommend" (推荐). Friends' picks now get their own entry in the
feed.

Smaller accounts gain most from that.

> On 36Kr's main account, recommendation brought just 2.2% of reads in a week
> of March 2025. On its smaller consumer account, the share was 45.9%.
> Source: 36Kr (36氪), March 2025. https://eu.36kr.com/zh/p/3211329723614340

Don't chase the button itself, though.

> On one NewRank article that passed 100,000 reads, friends' Recommend taps
> produced only 0.02% of its reads. It had 1,010 shares and 34 Recommend taps.
> Source: NewRank (新榜), March 2025. https://newrank.cn/article/detail/30626

Shares did the work.

So what gets forwarded? Something a reader can use or pass on, like a size
guide, or a store opening with the address and the hours. Or a checklist for a
decision a buyer is stuck on. A brand story rarely travels that way.

## FAQ

### What is a good WeChat open rate in 2026?

For a brand service account, the last large public study put average at 2.8%
to 4.1% and good at 4.1% to 6.8%. That data is from 2022, and four in ten posts
already sat under 2.8%. Since then the push has lost ground to recommendation.
Judge your rate next to total reads: if reads hold steady, the platform
shifted; if both fall, the account has a problem.

### Why did my WeChat views drop?

Usually for one of five reasons: pushes spent on routine notices, a weak push
title, a follower list full of people who never read, no Channels (视频号) or
Moments (朋友圈) support, or too few reasons to forward. WeChat (微信) also
sends more readers through recommendation now. Check read sources in your
account's back end first.

### How often should a brand publish on WeChat?

A foreign brand runs a service account (服务号), which can push to followers
four times a calendar month. That makes one push a week the natural rhythm,
with extra articles published without the push, which is unlimited. Quality
beats volume here. A weak push uses up a quarter of the month's pushes.
