# China marketing hidden costs: 12 lines nobody puts in the proposal

Most China marketing hidden costs are small public fees paid to someone
other than your agency. Three are not small: locked ad balances, legal risk,
and the commission on every sale.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

A China proposal prices the work. It rarely prices the paperwork or the
platforms' own fees, and money that gets stuck in an ad account rarely
appears either.
That gap is where the surprises come from.

The table below lists twelve lines a proposal can leave out, with the
published figure for each. Every number is sourced further down, and every
source was checked twice on October 6, 2026. Where no one publishes a figure, the
table says so.

| Cost line              | Who charges it                     | Published figure                                       | When it hits             |
|------------------------|------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| 1. Verification        | WeChat, Xiaohongshu, Douyin, Weibo | 99 US dollars to 600 yuan per application              | Before launch            |
| 2. Annual renewal      | The same platforms                 | 600 yuan a year on Xiaohongshu; 120 yuan on Douyin     | Every year               |
| 3. Ad balances         | Tencent Ads, Ocean Engine          | Bonus credit never refunded; cash only after ads stop  | When you pause or leave  |
| 4. Transcreation       | Your writer or agency              | No published rate                                      | Every post and pack      |
| 5. Trademark           | CNIPA, plus a required agent       | 270 yuan per class online, plus the agent's fee        | Months before you verify |
| 6. ICP filing          | MIIT, through your host            | No filing fee; 10,000-yuan fine if you skip it         | Before a China website   |
| 7. Legal review        | A lawyer or your agency            | No published rate; fines run 200,000 to 1 million yuan | Before anything runs     |
| 8. Samples and seeding | You, plus creators                 | About 20 to 60 US dollars a post for small creators    | Every seeding wave       |
| 9. Phone and identity  | A named person on your side        | No fee; one ID covers five WeChat accounts at most     | Setup and staff changes  |
| 10. Sales commission   | WeChat Store, Xiaohongshu, Douyin  | 1% to 5% a sale, plus payment fees                     | Every sale               |
| 11. Rush fees          | Agency, creators, producers        | No published market rate                               | When timelines shrink    |
| 12. Out-of-scope work  | Your agency                        | No published market rate                               | Month two onward         |

## Why China marketing hidden costs stay out of the proposal

Most of these lines aren't the agency's to charge. They go to Tencent,
Xiaohongshu (小红书), a government office, or a creator, and a fee someone else
sets is the easiest one to leave out of a quote.

Some lines depend on choices you haven't made yet. Will you sell inside the
app? Put up a website in China? Send product to creators? Until you decide,
nobody can price them.

And a low headline number is easier to sell. Fixed pricing is still
rare in this market.

> 0 out of 37 agencies analyzed offer fixed-price packages.
> Source: TheRedScroll competitive analysis, April 2026.

China's Advertising Law (广告法) gives you a tool here. Article 30 requires a
written contract between an advertiser and the agency that runs its ads. Use
that contract to list every line in the table above, and who pays it.

**If you haven't launched yet,** read this as a checklist. Ask for each line
in writing before you sign.

**If you're already live and the bills keep growing,** read it as an audit.
Your agency may have done nothing wrong. Several of these costs simply arrive
late.

## 1 and 2. Verification fees, and paying them again every year

Every major platform charges to verify a business account. WeChat,
Xiaohongshu, and Weibo keep the fee even when the review fails.

| Platform                 | Fee per application | How long it lasts | Renewal                     |
|--------------------------|---------------------|-------------------|-----------------------------|
| WeChat, overseas company | 99 US dollars       | One year          | Annual review               |
| WeChat, mainland company | 300 yuan            | One year          | Annual review               |
| Xiaohongshu              | 600 yuan            | One year          | Annual review, 600 yuan     |
| Douyin                   | 600 yuan            | One year          | 120 yuan a year             |
| Weibo                    | 600 yuan            | One-time fee      | None stated on the fee page |

WeChat (微信) has two prices, set by where your company is registered.

> An overseas entity pays 99 US dollars per verification application, charged
> whether or not the review succeeds.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), overseas entity verification FAQ, confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/190723aQvMR31907233IF7FV.html

> A WeChat verification costs 300 yuan per application and lasts one year.
> The fee is not refunded if the review fails.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), platform documentation, confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/161219JvMNvi161219raieiY.html

Miss the annual review and you lose more than a badge. Tencent's help page
says a service account whose review lapses or fails also loses payment and
other advanced features.

Xiaohongshu (小红书) charges the same fee every year.

> Xiaohongshu charges 600 yuan a year to verify an enterprise account, with an
> annual review required to keep it.
> Source: New Rank (新榜), October 2025, confirmed against the platform's verification guide. https://a.newrank.cn/trade/news/4199

Douyin (抖音) is cheaper in year two.

> Douyin enterprise verification costs 600 yuan in the first year and 120
> yuan a year to renew.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), Douyin enterprise account FAQ, May 2022. https://www.oceanengine.com/faq/douyin-qyh-rzxydsq.html

> Weibo charges 600 yuan per application for enterprise blue V verification,
> whether or not it succeeds.
> Source: Sina Weibo customer service (新浪微博客服), confirmed October 2026. https://kefu.weibo.com/faqdetail?id=20004

The fees are small. A missed renewal costs more, so keep every date in one
shared calendar, owned by one person on your side.

## 3. Ad balances you don't get back quickly

None of the platform documents we checked prints a minimum first deposit.
Tencent advertises free account opening. The cost hides in the other
direction: getting money out.

Ad accounts are prepaid. You top up, then spend down. On Tencent Ads
(腾讯广告), which sells WeChat ads, bonus credit never comes back.

> Tencent alone sets its rebate and bonus policy. The non-cash part of an ad
> account, such as bonus inventory and credit, is not refundable.
> Source: Tencent Ads (腾讯广告), advertiser cooperation agreement, confirmed October 2026. https://e.qq.com/contract.html

Cash does come back, but only after every ad is off.

> Before a refund, all ads must be offline. Money goes back by the route it
> came in, and recharges older than 175 to 360 days may not return that way.
> Invoiced amounts need the invoices returned first.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), advertiser refund FAQ, confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/170214jyaU3y170214eEFJRb.html

Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), which sells Douyin ads, works the same way.

> Ocean Engine accounts are prepaid. A cash refund needs an account with no
> live ads, and grants, coupons, and credit lines are not refundable.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), marketing platform service agreement, in force January 2026. https://sf1-cdn-tos.douyinstatic.com/obj/ies-hotsoon-draft/account_center/ee525166-d060-401d-9d2f-83359617acf0.html

Ocean Engine's refund page adds a catch: asking for your balance back closes
the ad account, and any grant left in it is wiped.

On Xiaohongshu (小红书), the Juguang (聚光) ad balance is shared with the
account's own promotion tools, according to the platform's product manual.
Money spent boosting a post comes out of the same pot.

The practical rule: top up for the next month, not the next quarter. Ask who
holds the account, and who gets the bonus credit.

## 4. Transcreation, which is not the same as translation

Translation turns your English into Chinese. Transcreation rewrites the idea
so it works for a reader in Chengdu (who doesn't care how the line sounded in
Hamburg). Your campaign needs the second. Your paperwork needs the first, and
it has its own rules.

The law sets the baseline. Chinese is the base language for ads and for
anything on a product sold in China.

> Ads, and the names, packaging, and instructions of goods sold in China, must
> use standard Chinese as their base language.
> Source: National Common Language and Script Law (国家通用语言文字法), Article 15, revised December 2025, in force January 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2025-12-27/doc-inhefuwc6921440.shtml

Platforms also want stamped translations of your documents, and the stamps
differ. Xiaohongshu's (小红书) account verification guide wants any document
not in Chinese or English translated and stamped by a translation company.
Its ad platform guide wants non-Chinese registration documents translated
and stamped with your own company seal.

No public source puts a rate on either job. So ask your agency two things.
Is the copy written in Chinese, or translated from English? And who pays for
the stamped document translations?

## 5. A trademark, before the account can carry your brand name

Put a brand name on an account and the platform wants trademark proof. On
WeChat (微信), an overseas company naming its account after a brand must show
the trademark registration or an authorization. Xiaohongshu (小红书) ads go
further.

> On Xiaohongshu, an ad account name that matches a registered mark needs the
> certificate. A mark still under application needs the acceptance notice,
> and the application must be at least three months old.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书), Juguang registration and account opening guide, May 2022. https://fe-video-qc.xhscdn.com/fe-platform/cc8a5f81ddb6d91d6be9dfb34e62b49b999f234b/%E8%81%9A%E5%85%89%E5%B9%B3%E5%8F%B0%E6%B3%A8%E5%86%8C%E4%B8%8E%E5%BC%80%E6%88%B7%E6%B5%81%E7%A8%8B.pdf

The government fee is modest.

> Filing a trademark costs 270 yuan per class online, or 300 yuan on paper,
> covering 10 goods or services. Each extra item costs 27 yuan online.
> Renewal costs 450 yuan online.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration Trademark Office (国家知识产权局商标局), fee schedule in force since July 2019. https://sbj.cnipa.gov.cn/sbj/sbsq/sfbz/

A foreign company can't file alone, though. It pays an agent too.

> Foreign companies must file trademarks in China through a lawfully
> established trademark agency. A registered mark lasts ten years.
> Source: Trademark Law of the People's Republic of China (商标法), Articles 18 and 39, as amended April 2019. https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=66979

Agency fees aren't published by any neutral source. The bigger cost is time.

> The average trademark examination period held at four months in 2025.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration (国家知识产权局), annual work report, January 2026. https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2026/1/7/art_53_203564.html

After examination, the mark is published for three months so others can
object (Article 33 of the same law). That puts you past seven months before
registration, if nobody objects. File the Chinese name and the Latin one
early. China's IP office has published a revised Trademark Law that takes
effect on January 1, 2027. It keeps the ten-year term.

## 6. ICP filing, if you put up a website in China

Social accounts can run without a website. Ads, landing pages, and
verification forms often can't. Weibo (微博) and Xiaohongshu (小红书) both ask
for proof of filing when a website is part of your application.

A website hosted in mainland China needs an ICP filing (备案) with the
Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工业和信息化部). The
ministry's own service guide lists the filing as free of charge.

> A non-commercial website in mainland China must be filed before it goes
> live. The provincial office has 20 working days to issue a number once the
> file is complete. Running without one brings a 10,000-yuan fine.
> Source: Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工业和信息化部), Order 33, as amended by Order 68, February 2024. https://www.miit.gov.cn/gyhxxhb/jgsj/cyzcyfgs/bmgz/xxtxl/art/2024/art_84a0cfa0ebd049bbbe751dca9a008e56.html

The filing goes through your hosting company. That's the real cost: mainland
hosting, plus weeks of waiting before the page can go live. Plan the website
before the ad campaign.

## 7. Legal review of claims under advertising law

So far the numbers have been small. This one isn't.

A claim that sells in Europe can be illegal in China. "Best" and words like
it are the classic trap.

> China's Advertising Law bans terms such as "national level," "highest
> level," and "best." The advertiser faces a fine of 200,000 to 1 million yuan,
> and its license can be revoked in serious cases.
> Source: Advertising Law of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国广告法), Articles 9 and 57, as amended October 2018. https://www.gov.cn/guoqing/2021-10/29/content_5647620.htm

The fine doesn't scale with the size of the sale.

> In one 2022 case, a small food business was fined 200,000 yuan over health
> claims on a product that had sold 780 yuan before the case.
> Source: Caijing (财经), March 2023. https://www.mycaijing.com/article/detail/488674

Creator posts count too. Since May 2023, the market regulator's Internet
Advertising Measures have said a review or experience post with a purchase
link attached must be clearly labeled as an ad.

No one publishes a market rate for a legal read of social copy. Ask who does
it, before every post or once a month, and who carries the risk if a claim
slips through.

## 8. Product sampling and shipping for creator seeding

Creator seeding means sending product to many small creators, called KOCs
(key opinion consumers). On Xiaohongshu (小红书), this is how zhongcao (种草),
or product seeding, starts. The fee per post is small. The product and the
shipping are not.

> A nano KOC on Xiaohongshu, with 1,000 to 5,000 followers, may earn about 20
> to 60 US dollars a post, often through incentives or product gifting.
> Source: Long Advisory, June 2026. https://longadvisory.eu/en/kol-vs-koc-marketing-in-china-which-delivers-better-roi/

Paid deals booked through the platform's creator marketplace, Pugongying
(蒲公英), carry a service fee on top.

> Pugongying charges 10% of the deal in standard mode and 20% in the premium
> mode that adds platform promotion.
> Source: Niaoge Biji (鸟哥笔记), October 2022. https://www.niaogebiji.com/article-482538-1.html

Then there's customs. If your product isn't already in China, every box
crosses a border. China's Customs Tariff Law (关税法), in force since December
2024, waives duty only on samples and advertising goods of no commercial
value. A full-size product a creator will use and review has value. Count on
duty and tax, or seed from stock that has already cleared customs.

## 9. Local phone numbers and identity verification

China ties accounts to real people. Since 2013, a ministry rule has required
every phone number to be registered to a real identity. A foreigner registers
with a passport.

WeChat (微信) builds on that. Your Official Account needs a named admin.

> An admin's ID number and phone number can each be bound to at most five
> accounts. The admin confirms identity by scanning with a WeChat linked to
> their own bank card.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), admin information FAQ, confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/120911VrYVrA1510137JRRZr.html

Staff who post need the same: Tencent's rules say every operator's WeChat ID
must have a phone number and a bank card bound to it.

None of this has a fee. The cost is a person whose name sits on your account.
When that person leaves, the admin must change. Write the handover into the
contract.

## 10. Platform commission on social commerce

Selling inside the app means paying a share of every sale. The rates move,
and you won't always hear about it from your agency first.

WeChat Store (微信小店) is free to open.

> Opening a WeChat Store is free, with no monthly or annual fee. A technical
> service fee of 1% to 5% applies to each settled sale in most categories.
> Source: Tencent Marketing (腾讯营销), WeChat Store FAQ, August 2026. https://e.qq.com/faq/wechat-store/growth/faq-zcjc-008/

> WeChat Pay charges merchants a service fee of 0.6% to 1% of each
> transaction, depending on the business category.
> Source: WeChat Pay (微信支付), merchant onboarding guide, confirmed October 2026. https://pay.weixin.qq.com/static/applyment_guide/applyment_detail_store.shtml

Xiaohongshu (小红书) shows how fast this line moves.

> Xiaohongshu's e-commerce commission mostly runs 2% to 5% by category: 4% for
> beauty, 5% for women's wear, personal care, and pets. A plan announced in
> August 2025 waived it on a merchant's first 1 million yuan of sales.
> Source: Beijing Business Today (北京商报), August 2025. https://www.bbtnews.com.cn/2025/0826/567573.shtml

> Xiaohongshu ended that waiver on May 15, 2026, three months early.
> Source: Dianshangpai (电商派), May 2026. https://www.pai.com.cn/p/01krfdjkafdas1jcgj6y1senh1

Douyin (抖音) went the other way in 2026, for one product.

> Douyin e-commerce cut its technical service fee to 0.6% on orders made
> through its Qianchuan Chengfang ad product in 2026.
> Source: Sina Finance (新浪财经), January 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-01-08/doc-inhfqtyp3810727.shtml

## 11 and 12. Rush fees and out-of-scope requests

These two lines start small. A launch date moves up a week. Head office wants
a second Douyin (抖音) video this month, and a creator has to reshoot because
the product shots came in late. Each request is fair to charge for. None of
them was in the quote.

No neutral source publishes a market rate for rushed or extra work in China.
What you can do is fix the rules before month one.

Get three things in writing. First, a turnaround for each type of work.
Then a price, or at least a rule, for anything beyond the agreed volume. And
a change request you sign before any extra work starts.

> platforms are added at per-post rates, with a minimum of 4 posts a month.

## FAQ

**What costs are usually missing from a China marketing proposal?**

The lines paid to someone else. Account verification and its yearly renewal,
trademark filing, ICP filing for a website, stamped document translations,
legal review of claims, product samples and shipping for creators, and
commission on sales. Ad balances matter too: bonus credit is never refunded.
Ask for each line in writing, with who pays it.

**Do I need a trademark before verifying a social account in China?**

Often, yes, if the account carries your brand name. WeChat (微信) asks an
overseas company for its trademark registration or an authorization when the account
is named after a mark. Xiaohongshu (小红书) ads accept a pending mark only once the
application is three months old. Examination alone averaged four months in
2025, so file early.

**Are ad account deposits refundable?**

Partly. Tencent and Ocean Engine refund unspent cash, but only after every ad
is off, and Tencent pays it back by the route it came in. Bonus credit,
coupons, and grants are never refunded. On Ocean Engine, a refund also closes
the ad account. Keep balances small and agree in writing who holds the
account.
