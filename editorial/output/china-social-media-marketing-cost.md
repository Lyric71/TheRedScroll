# How much does China social media marketing cost in 2026

Five cost lines, most of them public. Verification is cheap, media has a
published floor, and the agency fee is the only number you usually cannot find.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

Most China social media marketing cost estimates you'll read are a single
range with no source behind it. This one is built line by line. Every platform
figure below comes from the platform's own documentation or a dated trade
source, and every one is cited where it appears.

The short version: your money goes to five places. Account setup, content,
media, influencers and the agency. Four of the five have public price points.
The fifth, agency fees, is where the market goes quiet. That is the part
TheRedScroll publishes on its pricing page, and the reason this article does
not need to guess.

| Cost line | What it covers | Where the figure comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Account setup | Verification on WeChat and Xiaohongshu | Platform documentation, annual fee |
| Content production | Posts, videos, campaign creative | Scope-driven, no platform price |
| Media spend | Ads on WeChat, Xiaohongshu, Douyin | Platform minimums and deposits |
| Influencer fees | KOC seeding and KOL campaigns | Dated 2026 market research |
| Agency fee | Strategy, production, ads, reporting | Published rate cards, where they exist |

Dollar figures in this article are rounded conversions at roughly seven yuan
to the dollar. The yuan figure is the one to trust.

## What you're actually paying for: the five cost lines

The reason China marketing feels expensive is that proposals bundle five
different things into one number. Pull them apart and the picture is clearer.

**Account setup and verification** is a one-time or annual platform fee. It
is small and fully public. It is also the one line foreign companies get wrong
most often, because overseas entities pay a different fee through a different
process.

**Content production** is the labor of making posts, videos and campaign
creative in Chinese, for Chinese platforms, at the cadence each platform
rewards. It has no platform price. It costs what the team producing it costs.

**Media spend** is money that goes to the platform to buy reach. It has
published floors: a minimum daily budget, a minimum deposit, a starting bid.
Those floors are the most reliable numbers in this whole subject.

**Influencer fees** go to creators. The range is wide, from a few dollars per
post for a small key opinion consumer (KOC) to tens of thousands of dollars for
a campaign with a mid-sized key opinion leader (KOL) on Xiaohongshu (小红书).

**Agency fees** cover strategy, production, ad operations, community work and
reporting. This is the line most agencies won't put a number on.

> 0 out of 37 agencies analyzed offer fixed-price packages.
> Source: TheRedScroll competitive analysis, April 2026

Keep those five lines apart in every proposal you read. If a proposal mixes ad
spend into the agency fee, you cannot tell what you are paying for, and you
cannot tell whether the agency is marking the media up.

## Account setup and verification: one-time costs by platform

The good news first. Verification costs less than most people expect, and the
platforms publish the fee.

A WeChat (微信) Official Account run by a mainland entity pays a review fee
each time it applies for verification, and verified status lasts one year.

> A WeChat Official Account or Service Account pays a one-time review service
> fee of 300 yuan (about 42 dollars) per verification application. Verified
> status is kept for one year from the date of approval.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), platform documentation, confirmed September 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/161219JvMNvi161219raieiY.html

Foreign companies do not use that process. An overseas entity registers
through a separate route and pays in dollars.

> Overseas entities pay a one-time review service fee of 99 dollars per
> verification application, charged whether or not the review succeeds.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), overseas entity verification FAQ, confirmed September 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/190723aQvMR31907233IF7FV.html

Xiaohongshu (小红书) charges a similar annual fee for its enterprise
professional account, the Blue V (蓝V) badge that unlocks ads, shop links and
the data dashboard.

> Xiaohongshu enterprise account verification costs 600 yuan (about 85
> dollars) per application. The verification is valid for one year and must
> be renewed through an annual review. A failed review is not refunded.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) enterprise account verification guide, platform document, confirmed September 2026; New Rank (新榜), October 2025. https://a.newrank.cn/trade/news/4199

Two things the fee does not cover. The first is the paperwork: a business
registration certificate, a signed application letter, translations stamped by
a translation company for non-Chinese documents. The second is the time. The
Xiaohongshu guide notes that overseas entities must verify through the desktop
site, not the app, and the review clock runs 30 days per order.

| Platform | Who pays | Fee | Valid for |
|---|---|---|---|
| WeChat Official Account | Mainland entity | 300 yuan per application | One year |
| WeChat Official Account | Overseas entity | 99 dollars per application | One year |
| Xiaohongshu professional account | Any enterprise | 600 yuan per application | One year, annual review |
| Douyin (抖音) enterprise account | Any enterprise | See note below | One year |

Douyin's enterprise verification fee is not in this table because we could not
confirm the current figure on a dated platform page during research for this
article. TODO: unsourced claim removed. The pattern is the same as the other
two: a modest annual review fee paid to a third-party auditor.

## Content production: per post, per video, per campaign

There is no platform price for content, so any number you see here is a
production cost, not a fee. Three things drive it.

**Format.** A Xiaohongshu image note with a Chinese caption is the cheapest
unit of content on the market. A 60-second Douyin video with a presenter,
Chinese subtitles and a hook that survives the first two seconds costs many
times more. A WeChat article of 1,500 characters with custom graphics sits in
between.

**Cadence.** Xiaohongshu rewards accounts that post several times a week.
Douyin rewards daily. WeChat can run on two to four articles a month. Your
content budget is the unit cost multiplied by the cadence the platform expects,
not the cadence you would like.

**Who makes it.** Content for China has to be written in Chinese by someone
who reads the comments, follows the trending formats and knows which words
get a post hidden. That is a mainland or Hong Kong team, not a translator.

The mistake foreign brands make here is to budget for translation of existing
assets. Translated Western content underperforms on every Chinese platform,
and the platforms' recommendation systems measure that underperformance in the
first hour. Native production costs more per piece and less per result.

For a sense of how production is priced when it is priced honestly, the
TheRedScroll pricing page lists per-item rates for posts, articles and videos
alongside its monthly packages. That is the only place those figures appear.

## Media spend: minimum viable budgets by platform

This is the cost line with the firmest numbers, because the platforms publish
them. It is also where an unsourced estimate does the most damage, so every
figure here is the platform's own.

WeChat Moments ads, the format most foreign brands start with, run on two
buying models. Scheduled buys lock inventory in advance at list price.
Bidding buys compete in real time and start much lower.

> WeChat Moments ads bought by bidding start at a daily budget of 1,000 yuan
> and a CPM floor of 30 yuan. Scheduled buys start at a total budget of 50,000
> yuan per placement. Local promotion ads also start at 1,000 yuan a day.
> Source: Tencent Ads (腾讯广告), WeChat advertising product page, confirmed September 2026. https://e.qq.com/topic/marketing/industry/weixin/

Those floors have held for years. Tencent's own self-serve guide for Moments
ads, published when the bidding model opened up, gave the same three numbers.

> The daily budget for a bidding campaign may not be set below 1,000 yuan, the
> CPM bid must be above 30 yuan, and a scheduled campaign budget may not be
> below 50,000 yuan.
> Source: Tencent (腾讯), WeChat Moments ads self-serve guide, February 2016. https://wximg.qq.com/wxp/temp/MomentsAdsGuidingBook.pdf

Douyin ads run through Ocean Engine (巨量引擎). Here the floor depends on how
you open the account.

> Opening a Douyin ad account directly with the platform carries a service
> fee. Agents may open accounts for free, but each agent sets its own minimum
> first recharge.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), account opening FAQ, June 2021. https://www.oceanengine.com/faq/douyin-kaihu-feiyong.html

We are not printing a Douyin first-deposit figure. The numbers in circulation
come from reseller blogs, not from the platform, and they vary by agent. TODO:
unsourced claim removed. Ask the agent opening your account for its threshold
in writing.

Xiaohongshu ads run through its Jiguang (聚光) platform, and for foreign
advertisers the route changed this year.

> Since February 2, 2026, overseas merchants can no longer open a Jiguang ad
> account on their own. They apply through an authorized Xiaohongshu agent,
> which reviews their qualifications before submitting to the platform.
> Source: Ziyouxing Studio (自由行Studio), Xiaohongshu Jiguang overseas guide, August 2026. https://zyxstudio.net/articles/xiaohongshu-jiguang-guide.html

The practical effect is that a foreign brand's first Xiaohongshu ad budget is
set in conversation with the agent, not read off a page. The same source
describes first deposits in the low thousands of yuan, but that is one
observer's account of agent practice and not a platform rule, so we treat it
as guidance rather than a figure.

| Platform | Ad platform | Published floor | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|---|
| WeChat Moments, bidding | Tencent Ads | 1,000 yuan a day, 30 yuan CPM | Tencent |
| WeChat Moments, scheduled | Tencent Ads | 50,000 yuan per placement | Tencent |
| Douyin | Ocean Engine | Minimum first recharge | The opening agent |
| Xiaohongshu | Jiguang | Set at account opening | The authorized agent |

A minimum viable test, then, is roughly 1,000 yuan a day on WeChat for as many
days as you want data, or whatever first recharge your Douyin or Xiaohongshu
agent requires. Plan on 30 days. Anything shorter tells you about the creative,
not the channel.

## Influencer fees: KOC, mid-tier and top-tier

Influencer spending in China is now large enough that it competes with
e-commerce for the second-biggest line in consumer brands' budgets, and its
pricing is the least transparent of the five.

> China's influencer economy is forecast at 117.2 billion yuan (about 16.8
> billion dollars) for 2026. In 2025, official KOL rate cards rose 11.6% while
> the prices advertisers actually paid fell 3.4%.
> Source: Campaign Asia, reporting R3's 2026 China Social KOLC Governance Whitepaper, January 2026. https://aws1.campaignasia.com/article/hidden-costs-emerge-in-chinas-16-8-billion-influencer-market/506973

That 15-point gap between the rate card and the paid price is the single most
useful number in this section. It means the first quote you get from a
creator's agency is a starting point, not a price.

At the bottom of the market sit KOCs, ordinary users with small followings
who post about products they were sent.

> Nano KOCs on Xiaohongshu charge approximately 20 to 60 dollars per post.
> Micro KOLs on the same platform, with followings under one million, charge
> approximately 10,000 to 35,000 dollars per campaign.
> Source: Long Advisory, June 2026. https://longadvisory.eu/en/kol-vs-koc-marketing-in-china-which-delivers-better-roi/

At the top, a single collaboration on Douyin costs several times what the same
brand would pay on Xiaohongshu.

> Average collaboration costs on Xiaohongshu typically range between 10,000
> and 40,000 dollars. On Douyin, average collaborations often range between
> 25,000 and 65,000 dollars.
> Source: Long Advisory, May 2026. https://longadvisory.eu/en/how-much-influencers-cost-in-china-kol-pricing-explained/

| Tier | Typical unit | Sourced range | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nano KOC, Xiaohongshu | Per post | 20 to 60 dollars | Volume seeding, search presence |
| Micro KOL, Xiaohongshu | Per campaign | 10,000 to 35,000 dollars | Category credibility |
| Mid-tier, Douyin | Per collaboration | 25,000 to 65,000 dollars | Reach and video |
| Top-tier celebrity | Per post or stream | Not sourced for this article | Launch moments |

One warning about that table. A seeding program of 40 KOC posts at 40 dollars
each costs 1,600 dollars in creator fees, which looks cheap next to one KOL.
It is not cheap in labor. Somebody has to find, brief, ship product to, chase
and report on 40 people. That labor lands in the agency fee.

## Agency fees: retainer, project and fixed price compared

Here is the line where the sourcing runs out, and it is worth being honest
about why. We could not find a dated, non-agency source that publishes China
social media agency fee ranges, and we will not cite an agency's own blog for
a market figure. So this section compares the three billing models rather than
quoting a market rate.

**Retainer.** A monthly fee for a team's time, usually described in hours or
headcount. Scope is elastic. This is the most common model, and the one where
the number is hardest to get before a proposal. Ad spend is typically billed
through the agency with a percentage on top.

**Project.** A fixed fee for a defined deliverable: a launch campaign, a
Double 11 (双十一) push, a batch of videos. Predictable, but nothing happens
between projects, and Chinese platforms punish accounts that go quiet.

**Fixed price.** A published monthly package with a defined scope, a defined
output and a defined price, renewed on a fixed term. Ad spend is billed at
cost. The rarest model in this market by a wide margin.

> 0 out of 37 agencies analyzed offer fixed-price packages.
> Source: TheRedScroll competitive analysis, April 2026

| Model | You know the price when | Scope | Ad spend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retainer | After the proposal | Elastic, hours-based | Often marked up |
| Project | Per project, upfront | Fixed per project | Varies |
| Fixed price | Before the first call | Fixed, published | Billed at cost |

Whichever model you choose, three questions separate a clean proposal from a
padded one. Is ad spend billed separately at cost? Is content priced per
item, so you can see the unit rate? And what is the minimum term? Six months
is a reasonable minimum for China, because verification, warm-up and the first
data cycle take most of that.

## Three realistic budgets: testing, building, scaling

These three scenarios are budget envelopes, not packages. They show how the
five cost lines stack at three levels of commitment. Use them to sanity-check
a proposal, then read the pricing page for TheRedScroll's own figures.

**Testing, about 2,000 dollars a month.** One platform, usually Xiaohongshu
for consumer brands or WeChat for B2B. Verification is a rounding error at
this level. Most of the budget goes to content at the platform's minimum
cadence, with a small KOC seeding batch, ten to twenty posts, to give the
account social proof. Paid media is the WeChat bidding floor for a few days,
or nothing. The goal is one clean data cycle, not growth.

**Building, about 6,000 dollars a month.** Two platforms. Content moves to the
cadence each platform actually rewards. Media runs continuously at the
published floor on at least one platform, so the account learns which
audiences convert. KOC seeding becomes a monthly rhythm rather than a one-off,
and the first micro-KOL test fits in the quarter. Agency fee and content are
now the two largest lines, and media is third.

**Scaling, about 15,000 dollars a month.** Three platforms or two platforms
plus a store. Media becomes the largest line. A Douyin collaboration at the
sourced mid-tier range fits in every second month. Content production includes
video at a daily or near-daily cadence. Private domain work on WeCom (企业微信)
starts, because at this spend, retention is cheaper than another month of
acquisition.

The split shifts as you grow. At the test level, labor is most of the money.
At scale, media is. If a proposal at the scaling level still has the agency
fee as the biggest line, ask what the media budget is doing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a realistic monthly budget for China social media?

It depends on how many platforms you run and whether you buy media. A single
platform test with native content and a small KOC batch runs in the low
thousands of dollars a month. Two platforms with continuous media and monthly
seeding runs in the mid single-digit thousands. Three platforms with video and
KOL work runs well into five figures.

### How much does it cost to open a WeChat Official Account as a foreign company?

Tencent charges overseas entities a 99-dollar review fee per verification
application, and verified status lasts one year. The fee is not refunded if
the review fails. Budget separately for document translation and for someone
to run the application, because the process and paperwork differ from the
mainland route.

### Do agencies mark up ad spend in China?

Many do. On a retainer model, media is often billed through the agency with a
percentage added, and the markup is not always visible in the proposal. Ask
whether ad spend is billed at cost and whether you can see the platform's own
invoice. TheRedScroll bills ad spend separately with no markup, as stated on
its pricing page.

### What is the minimum budget to test one platform?

On WeChat, Tencent's published floor for bidding Moments ads is 1,000 yuan a
day with a 30-yuan CPM minimum. On Douyin and Xiaohongshu, the first deposit is
set by the agent who opens the account. Add native content at the platform's
minimum cadence and a small KOC batch, and run the test for 30 days.

### Why do most China agencies not publish prices?

Because retainers are elastic and scoping happens after the first call, so a
published number would lock the agency in before it knows the work. That is a
reason, not an excuse. In TheRedScroll's April 2026 analysis, 0 out of 37
agencies published fixed-price packages. Published prices move the scoping
before the call, where the buyer can compare.
