# Xiaohongshu marketing for foreign brands: the complete 2026 guide

Xiaohongshu is where Chinese shoppers check before they buy. A foreign brand
can verify an account with its own overseas paperwork. Ads and stores go
through an agent.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

Most guides to Xiaohongshu (小红书) marketing for foreign brands are written
from the outside. This one is written from accounts we run for foreign
clients: what the dashboard shows, what gets throttled, and what the first
month looks like.

Start with the three facts that decide your plan. Xiaohongshu is a search
engine with a feed attached. Its users skew young, female, urban, and about
to spend money. And the account is the easy part for a foreign company; the
ads and the store are where an agent comes in.

|---|---|---|

| Can we run ads from abroad? | Only through an authorized agent since February 2026 | Entity rules |

<!-- SECTION: What Xiaohongshu is -->

## Xiaohongshu is a search engine first and a social network second

Xiaohongshu (小红书) was founded in 2013 as a shopping guide for Chinese
travelers. Its English name on the app store is RedNote, and Western press
still calls it Little Red Book. The product has not changed much: people post
short "notes" (笔记) about things they bought, places they went, and problems
they solved. Other people search for those notes before they spend.

That search habit is what makes the platform different from Douyin (抖音) or
Weibo (微博). On Xiaohongshu, the search bar is the front door.

> Xiaohongshu reported that its monthly active users passed 400 million and
> that daily searches reached 800 million.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) Video Creator Trend Report, reported by Beijing Business Today (北京商报) via Sina Finance (新浪财经), May 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-05-27/doc-inhziqxq9291575.shtml

Eighteen months earlier the count was smaller, and it was already being
compared with Baidu (百度).

> In the fourth quarter of 2024, Xiaohongshu handled about 600 million
> searches a day, up from 300 million in mid-2023.
> Source: Zhou Tian Finance (周天财经), reported by 199IT, December 2024. https://www.199it.com/archives/1731824.html

For a foreign brand this changes the job. On Douyin you compete for attention
in a feed. On Xiaohongshu you compete to be the note a buyer finds when she
types your product category, your competitor's name, or a problem your product
solves.

## Who is on Xiaohongshu in 2026: young, female, urban, ready to spend

Who will read your notes? Xiaohongshu (小红书) answers that in the media
brief it gives advertisers, and the trade press reprints it. The headline
numbers from the 2025 brief are below.

> The Xiaohongshu official brief describes 300 million monthly active users,
> a male to female ratio of 3 to 7, a 70 percent monthly search penetration
> rate, 50 percent of users born after 1995, 35 percent born after 2000, and
> 50 percent living in tier 1 and tier 2 cities.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) official brief, reported by Qian-gua Data (千瓜数据), April 2025. https://www.qian-gua.com/information/detail/3149

Seven in ten users are women. Half were born after 1995. Half live in the
biggest cities. And seven in ten use the search bar in any given month. Of
the six figures, that last one does the most work in your content plan.

A third-party count is lower, and it is worth knowing why.

> QuestMobile measured Xiaohongshu at 242 million monthly active users in
> mainland China in May 2025, up 13.2 percent from a year earlier.
> Source: QuestMobile, reported by The Paper (澎湃新闻), November 2025. https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_31995524

The platform counts every account everywhere. QuestMobile counts a panel of
mainland phones. Use the platform's number for reach and the QuestMobile
number when a finance director asks for an independent figure.

The intent data matters more than the size data. At its annual advertiser
conference in December 2025 the platform put a number on how many people
come to it for buying advice.

> About 200 million users a month seek purchase advice on Xiaohongshu. Users
> open the app 16 times a day on average, and more than 9 million notes are
> published daily.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) 2026 WILL conference, data center figures for January to November 2025, reported by 100EC (网经社), December 2025. https://www.100ec.cn/detail--6655530.html

The platform hasn't published what share of searches are product related.
The closest official figure is the one above: 200 million people a month
asking what to buy. That is the audience a foreign brand is writing for.

## The three account types, and the one a foreign brand needs

Xiaohongshu splits accounts into non-professional and professional. Inside
professional there are two tracks: personal and enterprise. That gives three
types that matter to a brand.

| Account type | Who it's for | Verified with | What it gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal account | Individuals | Nothing | Posting, no business tools |
| Personal professional account | Creators, freelancers | A mainland ID | Creator tools, brand deals through Pugongying |
| Enterprise professional account (Blue V) | Companies, mainland or overseas | Business documents and a fee | Data dashboard, ads access, shop link, employee accounts |

The enterprise professional account is the only one a foreign company should
run. It carries the Blue V (蓝V) badge, and it is the account type the
platform's own guide describes.

> Xiaohongshu enterprise accounts are open to mainland Chinese companies,
> overseas companies, and companies from Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan. The
> verification fee is 600 yuan per application, valid for one year with an
> annual review. A failed review is not refunded, and each order expires
> after 30 days.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) enterprise account verification guide, platform document, confirmed September 2026; New Rank (新榜), October 2025. https://a.newrank.cn/trade/news/4199

The same guide has three details foreign teams miss. Overseas entities verify
through the desktop site, not the app. Any document not in Chinese or
English needs a translation stamped by a translation company. And the account
name must match the store name if you later open a shop.

What the dashboard shows once you are verified: for each note, impressions,
reads, likes, saves, comments, and shares, plus the split between traffic from
search and traffic from the discover feed. That last split is the number we
watch most. A note that earns most of its reads from search will keep earning
them for months. A note that only got feed traffic is done in three days.

## What a foreign brand can and cannot do without a Chinese entity

This is the section that decides budgets, so here is the plain version.

**You can** open and verify an enterprise account with your home-country
registration documents. You can post notes, reply to comments, run a
Pugongying (蒲公英) creator campaign, and read the full dashboard.

**You cannot** open a self-serve ad account from abroad.

> Since February 2, 2026, overseas merchants gain access to Xiaohongshu's
> Jiguang ad platform only through an authorized agent, with no self-serve
> account opening.
> Source: Ziyouxing Studio (自由行Studio), Xiaohongshu Jiguang overseas guide, August 2026. https://zyxstudio.net/articles/xiaohongshu-jiguang-guide.html

Jiguang (聚光) is the platform's ad platform. An agent opens the account on
your behalf, sets the first deposit (the platform publishes no minimum, the
agent does), and usually runs the campaigns. That is where a China-based
agency earns its place in your first year.

**You can sell**, but the store has its own paperwork. The merchant
qualification rules treat overseas companies as a separate category.

> Overseas companies, including those in Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan, apply
> with a business registration and certificate of incorporation. Cross-border
> stores need a complete brand authorization chain for each brand, or purchase
> proof for at least one product if no authorization exists. Imported
> cosmetics need the China import cosmetics filing.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) merchant qualification requirements, platform document, confirmed September 2026. https://picasso-static.xiaohongshu.com/test/8b3bc7324ca06fceea379177f9eed1fa/小红书资质入驻要求.pdf

The practical sequence for a pre-entry brand is the account first, creator
seeding second, and a store only once search demand for the brand name shows
up in the dashboard. Most of our clients spend three to six months in the
first two stages before the third makes sense.

## Content that works: the note formats that get distribution

Xiaohongshu has two native formats, image notes (图文笔记) and video notes
(视频笔记). Both are vertical, both open with a cover, and both live or die
on the first line of the title.

Here is what we see across client accounts. The notes that get distribution
share five traits.

| Trait | What it looks like | Why it earns reach |
|---|---|---|
| Search-shaped title | A question or a category phrase, under 20 characters | Matches what users type |
| Cover with a text overlay | Product plus a four-character promise | Stops the scroll in a grid |
| Real use, not a studio | Hands, kitchens, bathroom shelves, office desks | Reads as a note from a friend |
| Comparison or list | "Three tried, one kept" | Gets saved, and saves drive ranking |
| Comment-ready ending | An open question, or a "which one for you?" | Comments extend the note's life |

Two formats outperform on foreign brand accounts. The first is the honest
comparison: your product against two others in the category, with a reason
to choose each. Users save it, and saves are what the ranking rewards. The
second is the how-to that happens to use your product. A skincare routine. A
coffee recipe. A packing list for a Chengdu (成都) weekend.

Brand-voice posts, campaign visuals, and anything that looks like a print ad
get throttled. Not banned. Throttled. The dashboard shows a few hundred
impressions and then nothing. We have watched polished global creative from
a client's headquarters stall at that level, while a phone photo of the same
product on a Guangzhou (广州) sidewalk kept collecting reads from search for
months.

The platform's own advertiser message points the same way. At its December
2025 conference it described zhongcao (种草), the practice of planting a
desire through real notes, as entering an "effectiveness era." The measure
the platform now sells advertisers is the purchase a note leads to. Likes
don't come into it.

## The search layer: how buyers find products through notes

Most guides skip this part. It is where a foreign account is won or lost.

A note can be found three ways: the discover feed, a follower's feed, or
search. For a foreign brand with a small following, search is the only one
that scales.

How it works in practice. A user types a query. The results page mixes notes,
products, and accounts. Notes rank on a blend of keyword match, engagement
(saves and comments weigh more than likes), recency, and the account's
history in that topic. The platform does not publish the weights. What we
see in client dashboards is that saves and comments move a note up faster
than likes, and that a note on a topic the account has posted about before
ranks sooner than a one-off.

The first job is to find out what your buyers type. The search bar's
autocomplete is the free tool: type your category and read the suggestions.
Those are real queries, ranked by volume. Do it for your category, your
competitor names, and the problems your product solves, and you have a title
bank that will last a quarter.

Then write the query into the title, once, in natural Chinese. Not the
English brand name alone. Chinese users search in Chinese, and a foreign
brand with no Chinese name has no search presence.

Last, answer the query in the first three lines of the note and tag it.
Tags (话题) are the platform's own taxonomy. A note with three relevant tags
and one good title beats a note with fifteen tags every time we've tested
it.

A typical month on a new account shows the pattern. Week one, all traffic is
feed traffic and it dies fast. By week four, if the titles are right, search
becomes the largest source in the dashboard and the older notes keep
collecting reads.

## KOC seeding versus paid: which one to buy first

Xiaohongshu runs on other people's notes about your product. There are two
ways to get them: seed small creators, or pay for reach.

Key opinion consumers (KOC) are ordinary users with a few thousand followers.
Key opinion leaders (KOL) are the bigger accounts. The fee gap between them
is wide.

> On Xiaohongshu, a nano KOC with 1,000 to 5,000 followers charges about 20 to
> 60 dollars per post, while a micro KOL with under a million followers
> charges about 10,000 to 35,000 dollars per campaign.
> Source: Long Advisory, June 2026. https://longadvisory.eu/en/kol-vs-koc-marketing-in-china-which-delivers-better-roi/

Whichever tier you use, paid creator work goes through the platform's
marketplace, Pugongying (蒲公英), and the platform takes a cut.

> Beyond the creator's fee, Xiaohongshu charges a content cooperation service
> fee on Pugongying deals: 10 percent in standard mode and 20 percent in the
> premium mode that adds platform promotion.
> Source: Niaoge Biji (鸟哥笔记), October 2022. https://www.niaogebiji.com/article-482538-1.html

Some agencies skip Pugongying to save the fee. We don't, for a foreign brand.
An unreported commercial note risks being flagged as an ad and losing reach.
A reported note keeps its distribution and can be put behind paid promotion
later, which is where the next stage of the plan needs it.

Seeding earns its keep at launch, when nobody has searched for your brand
name yet and you need 20 to 40 real notes so a search for your category
returns real people using your product. Paid comes after that. Once the
dashboard shows which notes convert, Jiguang (聚光) budget behind those
notes pushes them higher in the results for the queries that matter.

The mistake we see most is the reverse order. A brand pays for a big KOL
post first, gets a spike, and then a buyer who searches the brand name finds
one glossy post and nothing else.

## Your first 90 days on Xiaohongshu, week by week

This is the plan we run for a pre-entry client, compressed. Ninety days is
about how long it takes for search traffic to overtake feed traffic on a new
account.

| Weeks | What happens | What you should see |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Chinese brand name chosen, enterprise account verified on the desktop site, translated documents filed | Blue V badge, dashboard live |
| 3 to 4 | Title bank built from autocomplete, first 12 notes published, three a week | Feed traffic only, a few hundred impressions each |
| 5 to 8 | First 20 to 40 KOC notes seeded through Pugongying, comments answered daily | Brand name starts to appear in autocomplete |
| 9 to 12 | Best notes identified from the search split, Jiguang account opened through an agent, first paid push | Search overtakes feed as the top source |

Three notes on the table. The Chinese name comes first because everything
else depends on it. Comments get answered daily because a reply counts as
engagement on the note, and engagement is what keeps it in search. Paid
comes last. It only works on notes that have already proved themselves.

Expect one throttled note in the first month, usually the one that looked
most like an ad. Rewrite it as a comparison and post it again.

## What Xiaohongshu marketing for foreign brands costs

Costs split four ways: platform fees, content, creators, and the people who
run the account.

Platform fees are public and small. The enterprise verification is 600 yuan
a year, quoted above. Pugongying (蒲公英) takes 10 or 20 percent of creator
deals, also quoted above. Ad spend has a first deposit set by the agent who
opens the Jiguang (聚光) account. Ask any agency to bill it to you at cost,
with the platform's own invoice attached.

Content is the largest line in the first three months. A dozen notes a month
in Chinese, shot in a way that looks like a real person made them, is a
production job. Creator seeding at the KOC tier is cheap per post and adds up
by volume. The bigger KOL tier is a second-year decision for most of our
clients.

> Average KOL collaboration cost on Xiaohongshu runs 10,000 to 40,000 dollars,
> against 25,000 to 65,000 dollars on Douyin.
> Source: Long Advisory, May 2026. https://longadvisory.eu/en/how-much-influencers-cost-in-china-kol-pricing-explained/

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a foreign company open a Xiaohongshu account without a Chinese entity?

Yes. The platform's enterprise account guide accepts overseas companies and
companies from Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan, using their home registration
documents. Verification runs through the desktop site, costs 600 yuan a year,
and needs stamped translations for documents not in Chinese or English. Ads
and stores are the parts that need an agent or extra paperwork, not the
account itself.

### Is Xiaohongshu the same as RedNote?

Yes. RedNote is the English name the app adopted in its store listings, and
Little Red Book is the literal translation of Xiaohongshu that Western press
used for years. Chinese users say Xiaohongshu, and Chinese searches use the
characters 小红书. Use Xiaohongshu in your planning and RedNote when talking
to people who found the app through Western news coverage.

### How long does Blue V verification take?

The platform does not publish a review time. Its guide gives each
verification order a 30-day window and does not refund a failed review. In
our experience, a mainland company with clean documents is verified within
days. An overseas company usually takes two to three weeks, because stamped
translations and the signed application letter take time to prepare and are
the usual reason for a rejection.

### Does Xiaohongshu work for B2B?

It can, in narrow cases. The audience is consumers, so a B2B brand wins only
when the buyer is also a person who searches for advice: an architect
looking at lighting, a small business owner choosing coffee equipment, a
dentist comparing chairs. For those, search-shaped notes work the same way
they do for consumer brands. For pure procurement, WeChat (微信) and Zhihu
(知乎) are better homes.
