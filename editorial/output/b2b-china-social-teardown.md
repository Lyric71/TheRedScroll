# B2B China social media case study: Viessmann and iGuzzini

Viessmann and iGuzzini both sell through the trade. Chinese social worked for
them by reaching the household behind the order, and it took about two years.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

Most B2B brands assume Chinese social media isn't for them. This B2B China
social media case study looks at two that went ahead anyway. Viessmann, a
heating maker founded in 1917, sells through dealers and installers. iGuzzini,
an Italian lighting maker, gets specified by architects.

Both ran programs with us, and both case studies are public on this site.
Neither page publishes a lead count or a revenue figure. This teardown quotes
only what those pages say, then adds dated market data around them. Every source
was checked twice in October 2026, and this page will change if either case
study does.

What worked, in short: content aimed at the household, not the buyer on the
invoice; one job per platform; and WeCom (企业微信) to close the sale and keep the
customer. Here's how the two approaches compare.

| | Viessmann | iGuzzini |
|---|---|---|
| Trade channel in China | Dealers, installers, service companies | Architects, designers, contractors |
| Who social reached | Homeowners choosing a heating system | Consumers buying premium lighting |
| Platforms | Douyin (抖音), Xiaohongshu (小红书), WeCom (企业微信) | WeChat (微信), Xiaohongshu, Weibo (微博) |
| Where the sale closed | Tmall (天猫) and JD (京东) stores, WeCom chats | Tmall store |
| Time to a steady result | Roughly two years | 24 months |

## Why B2B brands assume Chinese social will not work

The usual B2B playbook starts on LinkedIn. In China, that door closed.

> LinkedIn had closed its social networking features in China two years earlier.
> In May 2023, it said its China job app (领英职场) would stop service on August 9,
> 2023.
> Source: China National Radio (央广网), May 2023. https://tech.cnr.cn/ycbd/20230509/t20230509_526245629.shtml

The second reason is how industrial buying works.

> China's market for maintenance, repair and operations (MRO) supplies reached
> 3.7 trillion yuan in 2024. Digital purchasing was under a tenth of it, and the
> main model is still request-for-quote, with buyers sourcing, comparing prices
> and verifying suppliers themselves.
> Source: KPMG China (毕马威), China industrial e-commerce white paper, November 2025. https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/cn/pdf/zh/2025/11/china-s-industrial-e-commerce-high-quality-development-white-paper-2025.pdf

If you sell valves to a factory, that picture is right. And no published survey
shows which social channels Chinese B2B buyers use. We looked, and we won't
invent one.

But a lot of B2B brands sell something an end user also chooses. A boiler ends
up in a family's home, and a lamp hangs over somebody's sofa. The installer or
the architect places the order (that part is pure B2B). The household often
makes the call, and it does its homework on social platforms first. The research
section below has the numbers.

Is that still B2B marketing? The order comes through a dealer, a designer or a
store. What social changes is who asks for the brand by name.

## Viessmann: heating engineering, sold as a warm home

Viessmann (菲斯曼) has made heating technology since 1917 and is now part of
Carrier. In China it sells through a trade network.

> Carrier completed its acquisition of Viessmann's climate business in January
> 2024, citing its distinctive installer and service channel model and 12,000
> employees.
> Source: Carrier China (开利), January 2024. https://www.carrier.com/commercial/zh/cn/news/news-article/carrier-completes-acquisition-of-viessmann-climate-solutions.html

> Nearly 200 Viessmann dealers from across China attended its 2026 dealer
> conference in Beijing. The company said its 2025 retail result in China was a
> record.
> Source: Viessmann China (菲斯曼), March 2026. https://www.viessmann.cn/zh/company/News/News12111.html

So why would a dealer brand need Douyin (抖音)? Because the market moved under it.

> In the first half of 2026, China's project channel for gas wall-hung boilers
> shipped 170,000 units, down 29.1%. Retail rose to 76.1% of domestic sales,
> driven by homes replacing boilers installed eight to ten years earlier.
> Source: ChinaIOL (产业在线), August 2026. https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/-CDxCuHn7BtOUHzeRt86-Q

Fewer boilers go into new buildings in bulk. More get chosen one home at a time,
by households who want a warm home and have no interest in boiler engineering.
Viessmann already had Tmall (天猫) and JD (京东) stores when the program began. Our
Viessmann case study is blunt about the problem: "traffic was thin."

So each platform got one job.

Douyin short videos talked about warmer homes and lower energy bills, not boiler
specs, and demos and livestreams sent viewers to the Tmall store. On Xiaohongshu
(小红书), the test was a search for "best heating system." It had to turn up
reviews, installation guides and homeowner stories. WeCom (企业微信) carried the
questions.

> "Douyin quickly became one of Viessmann's biggest sources of new online
> buyers."
> Source: TheRedScroll, Viessmann case study, October 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/work/viessmann/

## iGuzzini: from the architect's drawing to the living room

iGuzzini's problem was different. It has made architectural lighting since
1959. Its fixtures go into museums and hotels. In China, the trade business
carries on.

> China News Service called iGuzzini (依古姿妮) an international leader in
> architectural lighting, active in more than 20 countries. It signed a deal
> with a Shanghai developer to light the homes and public spaces of its North
> Bund project.
> Source: China News Service Shanghai (中新网上海), October 2024. https://www.sh.chinanews.com.cn/bdrd/2024-10-26/129729.shtml

The gap was the consumer. Our iGuzzini case study puts it in five words:
"Architects knew iGuzzini. Consumers didn't." It also names the hard part.
Nobody scrolls Xiaohongshu (小红书) looking for pendant lamps, and most people buy
lighting through a contractor or a designer they trust.

The program used the trade as the messenger instead. On Xiaohongshu, interior
designers, architects and renovation creators featured iGuzzini in real
projects. Weibo (微博) carried launches and design events. WeChat (微信) was home
base. Livestreams showed the lights switched on in real rooms, walked through
the design thinking and took questions live, which a product photo can't do.

Lighting buyers were already shopping online.

> Home lighting sold 22.5 billion yuan on Taobao (淘宝) and Tmall in 2023, up
> 6.8%. Douyin (抖音) sold 3.1 billion yuan, up 105.6%. September to December is
> peak season, when many households renovate.
> Source: China Association of Lighting Industry (中国照明电器协会), December 2024. http://www.cali-light.com/index/index/newsart/id/26336.html

## What content a B2B brand's China audience reads

Neither program led with specifications. Both led with the room, which matches
how Chinese households research a home purchase.

> 89% of users search Xiaohongshu (小红书) first when buying home appliances and
> home furnishing products, according to the platform's 2024 home-living
> methodology.
> Source: Baixing Jiadian (百姓家电网), via Sina Tech (新浪科技), March 2024. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2024-03-19/doc-inanweuq9235781.shtml

Line the two programs up by the job each piece of content did, and the overlap
is plain.

| Content type | Viessmann | iGuzzini | Platform | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benefit video | Warmer homes, lower bills | Light in real rooms | Douyin, livestream | Reach |
| Search proof | Reviews, installation guides | Designer and architect projects | Xiaohongshu | Research |
| Showcase | None listed | Project showcases, launches | WeChat | Trust |
| Live Q&A | Sizing and floor plan questions | Questions during livestreams | WeCom, livestream | Decision |
| Event reach | None listed | Design events, launches | Weibo | Reach |

Technical content has a place. It has to answer the household's question,
though. The first one quoted on the Viessmann page is "What size system do I
need?"

## WeChat as a home base, not a magazine

Begin with reach. WeChat (微信) is the app nearly every buyer in China already
uses.

> WeChat and WeChat combined monthly active accounts reached 1.439 billion in
> the second quarter of 2026, up 2% year on year.
> Source: Jiemian (界面新闻), via 21st Century Business Herald (21财经), August 2026. https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260812/herald/0c6b89715bfa13d4ecf96e41a685bdcd.html

For iGuzzini, the Official Account (公众号) was home base. It carried project
showcases and designer collaborations, plus product launches. The case page says
people bought because they'd been following on WeChat, saved a Xiaohongshu (小红书)
post weeks earlier, or watched a livestream.

Viessmann's program used WeChat differently. Its case page lists Douyin,
Xiaohongshu and WeCom (企业微信), so the WeChat piece was the chat, not a feed of
articles. That fits a boiler. Few people follow a heating brand, and most buyers
have a question or two before they pay for one.

If you haven't launched in China yet, build the home base around the questions
buyers bring from search. Press releases can wait. If you already run an
Official Account that posts company news and nobody replies, that's the symptom.
Your agency may have done exactly what it was asked to do, so look at the brief
before you look for a new agency.

## The WeCom handoff: where a question becomes a sale

If you only read one section, make it this one.

WeCom (企业微信) is the business version of WeChat (微信). Staff chat with customers
from a work account, and the customer stays in the WeChat they already use.

> WeCom connects more than 14 million companies and organizations, and companies
> serve more than 750 million WeChat users a day through it.
> Source: Xinhua (新华网), August 2025. http://www.news.cn/tech/20250820/fbe9a69fd7a742629cf8275238bb7b91/c.html

For Viessmann, this is where the program turned into sales. Buyers asked what
size system they needed and whether it would work with their floor plan. Our
team, based in China, answered in WeCom, in Chinese, in real time. After the
sale, the same thread became the service channel: installation first, then
maintenance bookings and follow-ups.

> "One conversation from first question to long-term client."
> Source: TheRedScroll, Viessmann case study, October 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/work/viessmann/

The flow ran in four steps:

1. A Douyin (抖音) video or a Xiaohongshu (小红书) note raises the question.
2. The buyer adds the brand in WeCom and asks it.
3. The sale closes on Tmall (天猫) or JD (京东), with distributors and
   offline retail joined to the same loop.
4. The same WeCom thread handles installation and service.

> Each company gets 2,000 WeCom external contacts free. An unverified company
> can add only 100.
> Source: WeCom help center (企业微信帮助中心), confirmed October 2026. https://open.work.weixin.qq.com/help2/pc/21326

That limit is the practical reason to run chats from a verified work account
rather than a salesperson's personal WeChat. Verify the company before the first
campaign goes live. Our CRM and Private Domain Traffic service page covers how
we set up that layer, and our pricing page lists what each package includes.

## How long this B2B China social media case study took to pay off

Short answer: about two years, by the case pages' own account.

> "Within roughly two years, Viessmann's online revenue in China was growing
> month over month. Tmall and JD went from new storefronts to real, consistent
> sales channels."
> Source: TheRedScroll, Viessmann case study, October 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/work/viessmann/

> "Over 24 months, iGuzzini went from a name known only in trade circles to a
> brand Chinese luxury consumers actually recognized."
> Source: TheRedScroll, iGuzzini case study, October 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/work/iguzzini/

The iGuzzini page adds that Tmall (天猫) sales backed it up: "Good numbers, steady
growth." In both cases the stores show the result: Tmall and JD (京东) for
Viessmann, Tmall for iGuzzini.

Neither page publishes the month of a first sale or a first lead, so we won't
either.

Part of the reason it's slow is the product. A family buys a boiler once in a
long while. ChinaIOL ties today's replacement wave to units installed eight to
ten years ago. Lighting sales cluster in the renovation season. Social has to be
in place before that moment comes.

## What we would do differently

Three things, based on what the case pages show.

First, bring the trade into the loop from day one. Viessmann's page ends with
offline retail, distributors and service companies joined to the same funnel.
We'd plan that from the first month, so a dealer gets the WeCom (企业微信) chats
from their own area.

Second, give iGuzzini's designers a direct line. Its page lists WeChat (微信),
Xiaohongshu (小红书) and Weibo (微博), with no WeCom layer. A designer who specifies
one project may specify the next. A WeCom group for the designers already
posting the brand's projects would keep that conversation with the brand.

And we'd test one more tool: Xiaohongshu's own lead accounts, aimed at the trade
buyer.

> More than 40,000 companies run KOS (employee) accounts on Xiaohongshu,
> producing more than 6 million leads a month, according to platform data for
> August 2026.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) 2026 KOS guide, reported by Huaxia Morning News (华夏早报) on ifeng (凤凰网), September 2026. https://baby.ifeng.com/c/8wUkh912dXd

Xiaohongshu doesn't split those leads into B2B and consumer. Try it with one
sales rep's account before you build around it.

## Questions B2B brands ask about China social

### Does B2B marketing work on Chinese social media?

It works when an end user helps choose the product. Viessmann and iGuzzini both
sell through trade channels, and both used social to reach the homeowner or
consumer behind the order. For pure procurement, such as parts for a factory
line, buying still runs on request-for-quote. There, social supports credibility
rather than creating the order.

### Which platform is best for B2B in China?

WeChat (微信), with WeCom (企业微信) behind it, for most B2B brands. LinkedIn's China
app closed in 2023, so WeChat is where buyers check you out and talk to sales.
Add Xiaohongshu (小红书) when an end user researches the product, as with heating
or lighting. Add Douyin (抖音) when the product needs to be seen working.

### How long before B2B social generates leads in China?

Plan in years, not months. Our Viessmann and iGuzzini case studies both describe
about two years of work behind the result. Products bought rarely, like a boiler
or the lighting for a renovation, have long cycles. Judge the first year on one
thing: can buyers find you when they search?
