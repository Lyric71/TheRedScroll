# Best China social media agencies: compare the fit

Choose an agency for the work your brand needs, then compare the full cost
of that work. A published fee helps, but it doesn’t prove a team can deliver.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

The best China social media agencies for a retail launch may be poor choices
for an industrial sales team. Start with the job, not an agency ranking.

TheRedScroll sells China social media services, so we have a financial
interest in this comparison. We compare agency types rather than name
rivals. This is a buying guide, not an independent awards list.

Our October 16, 2026 review checked selected agency pages for stated
services and public fees. It did not test campaign results or request
private quotes. The table pairs those service categories with questions
we recommend asking before you sign. This is a buyer’s checklist, not a
scorecard. Categories overlap; they are not ranks.

| Agency type | Consider it for | Evidence to request | Pricing check | Main risk to test |

| International network | Work across markets and business units | Named local delivery team | Scope of the China fee | Global credentials without local capacity |
| Search specialist | Chinese search and sales inquiries | Search-to-inquiry example | Site work versus media fees | Traffic without useful inquiries |

| Focused social team | A defined ongoing content brief | Actual posts and reporting samples | Full committed cost | A small scope that leaves essential work out |

## How to compare the best China social media agencies

Define the outcome before reading pitches. A new entrant might need to
learn which product questions Chinese buyers ask. An established brand
might need to find out why inquiries stall after someone sends a message.
Those problems call for different work, even on the same platform.

We chose a small sample to cover different service types, excluding ourselves.
We inspected a Chinese network page, a Chinese search agency page, a
creator-focused agency homepage, and a commerce agency homepage. Their
published services informed the categories above. These are overlapping
types, not exclusive labels or a ranking of individual businesses.

> competitive analysis, April 2026).

We have not rerun that original sample for this article. Neither
review measures client satisfaction or campaign quality. Pricing
transparency is useful evidence about buying, not proof of performance.

Also check what a published price buys. Store software, a training session,
and ongoing social content are different purchases. A monthly software fee
doesn’t tell you the cost of writing posts, answering comments, or running
a campaign. Don’t mark an agency as transparent or opaque until you have
identified the service being compared.

## When an international network fits

Consider a network when your China brief must work alongside other markets,
several product divisions, or a large paid advertising program. A shared reporting
process may matter more to your company than a smaller agency’s shorter
approval chain.

Test whether the network gives you access to the disciplines you need. The
company’s global size does not tell you who will write your Chinese posts.
Get the proposed account team’s names and location. Then find out which
work sits outside that team. If a subcontractor is involved, name the
person responsible for checking and accepting its work.

A directory of offices cannot answer those questions.

Request an example of how a local team changed a global brief. What
changed? Find out who approved it and what happened next. You’re checking
whether the people doing the work can challenge an instruction that won’t
fit the audience.

For a brand already in China, ask who has authority when local results
conflict with headquarters’ plan. A regional contact who can only pass
messages upward may add delay without helping the campaign.

This type can be worth considering even when its quote is higher. Your
company may need help meeting its purchasing rules, specialist teams, or
coordination across markets. Price that work explicitly. Otherwise a
small social brief and a regional program appear to compete on the same
spreadsheet when they buy different things.

## Search experience matters when inquiries are the goal

If the immediate problem is capturing Chinese searches for your product,
include a search specialist in the shortlist. Ask it to show how a query
on Baidu (百度) leads to a useful page and then a sales conversation.

A paid search plan should explain which searches it targets and which it
excludes. Ask to see the landing page and inquiry form. How does the team
decide whether an inquiry is useful? A click report alone cannot
show whether your sales team received anything worth following up.

For an industrial brand, give the candidate an actual product category.
Ask how it would separate buyers looking for specifications from people
looking for repairs, jobs, or general information. You don’t need a free
campaign plan. You need enough reasoning to judge whether the team
understands the buying task.

Separate website work from search promotion. Translation, page design,
technical fixes, and advertising may have different owners. Put each
responsibility into the proposal so a weak landing page doesn’t become a
dispute between suppliers after launch. Name who can make and approve
technical changes to the site.

A social agency may still contribute useful content or follow-up messages.
It should not claim that posting alone will solve a broken inquiry path.
If you already have traffic, start by checking what visitors meet after
they arrive before buying more of it.

<!-- SECTION: Creator launches -->

## When a creator campaign team fits a launch

For a product that needs a demonstration, compare how agencies select
creators and judge their work. A roster of famous faces tells you little
about whether those people can explain your product to a likely buyer.

On Xiaohongshu (小红书), also called RedNote, ask the candidate to walk
through a paid creator brief. Start with the product claim. How much
freedom does the creator have to describe the experience? Check how the
cooperation is recorded and which platform process applies today.

> Xiaohongshu’s 2022 commercial convention encouraged creator cooperation
> through its official creator platform and opposed fake recommendations.
> Source: 21st Century Business Herald (21世纪经济报道), May 2022.
> https://m.21jingji.com/article/20220506/herald/8aab5572c0f69554f5722de1364879d2_zaker.html

That dated guidance is a reason to examine the cooperation process. It is
not a substitute for checking current platform rules before booking.
For your shortlist, ask to see a sample order record and the approval
steps the agency proposes to use.

Then inspect the economics. Separate the agency’s work from creator fees,
product samples, shipping, and paid promotion. Ask who can reuse the
content. Confirm where that permission applies and when it ends. A successful
post may be less useful than it looks if you cannot use it in advertising.

If the last launch disappointed you, take the original brief into the
meeting. Was the creator hired to demonstrate the product, drive
store visits, or generate orders? Judge the proposed fix against that
objective. Don’t accept a new roster as the whole diagnosis.

<!-- SECTION: Commerce -->

## Check the work that happens after a store visit

The handoff to the store is worth checking next. Social content and store
operations need different evidence. If a buyer
can discover your product but cannot get a delivery answer, more posts
won’t fix the immediate problem.

Consider a commerce operator when the brief includes product listings,
store promotions, customer questions, and the daily work around orders.
Ask how the operator divides those tasks with your distributor or
internal team. A service description on a website is a starting point,
not confirmation that all of it is included in your quote.

For a new entrant, begin with the route to purchase. Who supplies stock,
answers a customer, and accepts a return? Ask the candidate to describe
what happens after a campaign produces an order. If it stops at the click,
you still need someone to cover the rest.

For a brand already trading, request a sample report that separates orders
from cancellations and refunds. Ask how discounts and media spending enter
the calculation. A larger sales total can be a poor result if the costs
needed to produce it consume the margin.

| Responsibility | Evidence to request | Put in the proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Product listings | An actual listing and revision process | Who supplies, checks, and updates product details |
| Customer questions | Sample reply workflow | Coverage, proposed reply times, and escalation |
| Promotions | Campaign calendar tied to available stock | Who approves discounts and paid media |
| Orders and returns | A report with cancellations and refunds | Source records and reporting definitions |
| Handover | Account and asset register | Access, export formats, and delivery dates |

Keep the store operator’s fee separate from a social agency’s fee when
both are involved. Shared work should have a named owner. Otherwise both
suppliers may expect the other to answer product questions or explain a
drop between clicks and orders.

<!-- SECTION: Smaller budgets -->

## Keep a smaller budget focused

A small budget needs a narrow brief. Ask candidates what they would leave
out and why. A narrower brief does not necessarily mean a shorter contract.
An agency that proposes every platform before learning your
product has not yet helped you choose.

For a first launch, a focused brief might cover a single audience question,
a limited content plan, and a defined review point. Treat that as a
planning example, not a promised route to sales. Account eligibility,
product readiness, and the way buyers contact you still need checking.

If you already have accounts, don’t pay to rebuild them automatically.
Request a review of what can be reused: content, audience insights,
creative assets, and the records needed to understand past spending.
The agency should explain what it would preserve before proposing a reset.

Compare the total commitment, not just the monthly headline. Include the
minimum term, setup work, and outside costs needed to use the service.
Compare quotes in the same currency and confirm how taxes are treated.
Also count the work your team must supply. A lower quote may depend on you
providing finished video or answering every incoming message. Compare that
with a higher quote that includes filming and replies. The first may still
fit, but only if your team has the time and skills to do its share.

Ask what happens when the planned work exceeds the scope. You need a
change process with written approval before extra spending. A fixed fee
only remains predictable when the deliverables and exclusions are clear.

## Build a usable shortlist in an afternoon

Write a short brief before contacting agencies. Describe the product and
the buyer. Explain how sales happen now and what you want to fix.
State what already exists, including accounts and creative material.
Give every candidate the same information.

Use an afternoon to organize the comparison. References and final
proposals may take longer. The aim is to identify useful conversations,
not to force a hiring decision before the evidence arrives.

Start with a relevant case study from each candidate. Ask what work the
agency itself did, when it happened, and whether the team on that project
would work on yours. A recognizable client name is not enough to answer
any of those questions.

Then compare proposals against the same checks:

- Does the scope name the channels, formats, delivery frequency, and
  approval process?
- Are media, creator fees, production, and taxes separated where relevant?
- Who has account access, and what records can your company export? Ask
  for an account register that identifies the owner and each user’s role.
- Can you understand the report without asking the agency to interpret
  every label?
- What happens when work is delayed, scope changes, or the contract ends?

For an in-market brand, add a sample of the current report and describe
what it fails to explain. Ask each candidate to show how its reporting
would answer that specific question. Keep confidential customer data out
of materials shared during the pitch.

Before signing, speak with a relevant client reference if one is available.
The agency chooses whom to introduce, so a reference is partial evidence.
Ask about missed deadlines and difficult decisions, not just the strongest
campaign. A useful reference can describe how the team behaved when the
work did not go to plan.

Keep a written reason for each rejection. If the remaining options still
cannot be compared, narrow the brief and request clarification. Resolve
unanswered contract questions before choosing a supplier.

## Questions buyers ask

### Which China social media agency publishes prices?

TheRedScroll publishes a full rate card on its pricing page. Read the
deliverables and minimum term alongside it. For any other candidate, check
whether a public fee buys ongoing agency work, software, training, or a
single project. Our selected-page check does not establish that other
agencies never publish service prices elsewhere.

### How do I shortlist a China marketing agency?

Start with the problem you need solved and choose agency types with
relevant work to show. Send the same brief to each candidate. Compare
the proposed team, deliverables, total commitment, and reporting. Use
case studies to ask better questions, then seek a relevant reference
before treating a sales presentation as proof of delivery.

### What should a China agency proposal contain?

A proposal should name who is responsible for the work. It should also
define the evidence you’ll receive. Ask for deliverables, approval steps, account
access, reporting definitions, and the full payment commitment. Separate
outside costs from the agency fee. Include a written process for changes
and a clear handover plan for when the relationship ends.
