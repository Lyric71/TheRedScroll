# Camper in China: a social media case study, 43,000 to 187,000 followers

A Mallorcan shoe brand quadrupled its Chinese following in 18 months without
running a single discount campaign. The content was about how the shoes are
made.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

A China social media case study usually publishes the win and stops there.
This one marks the two things we can't show you, and says why.

Camper came to us with 43,000 followers spread across three Chinese platforms
and an engagement rate of 1.2%. Eighteen months later the accounts held
187,000 followers at 4.7% engagement, and same-store sales were up 31% year
on year.

The follower number isn't the interesting part. What's interesting is that
the smallest of the three audiences sent the most store traffic, and that for
the first few months it looked like nothing was happening.

| | Month zero | Month 18 |
|---|---|---|
| Followers, three platforms | 43,000 | 187,000 |
| Engagement rate | 1.2% | 4.7% |
| Share of e-commerce traffic from Xiaohongshu | baseline | 38% |
| Same-store sales, year on year | baseline | up 31% |
| Customer retention | baseline | up 22% |
| Purchases per customer, per year | 1.3 | 2.1 |

> Camper grew from 43,000 to 187,000 followers across WeChat (微信), RedNote
> (Xiaohongshu, 小红书) and Weibo (微博) over 18 months. Engagement rose from
> 1.2% to 4.7%. Xiaohongshu drove 38% of e-commerce traffic. Same-store sales
> grew 31% year on year, retention improved 22%, and purchase frequency moved
> from 1.3 to 2.1 per customer a year. No discount campaigns were run.
> Source: TheRedScroll, Camper case study, September 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/work/camper/

Every source on this page was checked twice in September 2026. We review it
each quarter, because the Xiaohongshu audience and commerce figures move
faster than an article does.

## Where the account was in month zero

Three accounts, opened, posting, going nowhere. WeChat (微信), Weibo (微博)
and Xiaohongshu (小红书) held 43,000 followers between them. Engagement was
1.2%. Camper had stores in first-tier cities and social media was sending
nobody to them.

Nothing was broken, which is the part foreign teams find hardest to believe,
and it's worth being precise about, because the accounts were posting on
schedule, the captions were in Chinese, the grid looked tidy, and the whole
operation was running the global content calendar with translated captions
laid over the top of it. That's a different thing from being in the Chinese
market.

The constraint that shaped everything came from the client. Camper has made
shoes in Mallorca since 1975, and its design identity had to survive contact
with China: no discount campaigns, no generic lifestyle posts. In a category
where price promotion is the default lever, that removed the default lever.

It helps to see the size of the rooms those three accounts were standing in.
Reach was never the constraint.

> WeChat and its international app reached 1.418 billion combined monthly
> active accounts at the end of 2025, up 2% year on year.
> Source: Tencent Holdings Q4 and full-year 2025 results, reported by Tencent News (腾讯新闻), March 2026. https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260318A07APT00

> Weibo (微博) reported 567 million monthly active users and 252 million daily
> active users at the end of the fourth quarter of 2025.
> Source: Weibo Q4 and full-year 2025 earnings release, reported by 100EC (网经社), March 2026. https://www.100ec.cn/detail--6657687.html

Two platforms, roughly two billion accounts between them, and 43,000
followers to show for it. What was missing was a reason to follow.

<!-- SECTION: The pillar rewrite -->

## The decision that changed everything: the content pillar rewrite

We stopped posting product and started posting the reasons the product exists.

Camper's buyer in China is a design-conscious professional, roughly 28 to 45.
That reader doesn't respond to a hard sell, and doesn't need one. So the
pillars became design stories, artist collaborations, and how people in
Chinese cities actually wear the shoes. Three pillars, not twelve.

This works on Xiaohongshu (小红书) for a structural reason. The platform is
searched, not just scrolled, and a note that explains something keeps getting
found after it stops being new.

> Xiaohongshu (小红书) passed 400 million monthly active users and 800 million
> searches a day.
> Source: Xiaohongshu Video Creator Trend Observation Report, reported by Beijing Business Today (北京商报) via Sina Finance (新浪财经), May 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-05-27/doc-inhziqxq9291575.shtml

> Xiaohongshu's own advertiser brief puts the audience at a 3:7 male to female
> split, 70% monthly search penetration, half the user base born after 1995,
> and half in tier 1 and tier 2 cities.
> Source: Xiaohongshu official brief (小红书官方通案), reported by Qian-gua Data (千瓜数据), April 2025. https://www.qian-gua.com/information/detail/3149

Half the audience in the biggest cities, born after 1995, arriving through
search. That is the Camper buyer, described by the platform itself.

<!-- SECTION: The three formats -->

## What we published, and the three formats that carried the growth

One format per platform, matched to what that platform is for. We didn't
cross-post.

| Platform | Format that carried it | Why it fit |
|---|---|---|
| Xiaohongshu (小红书) | Micro-influencer notes on materials, construction and design intent | Search-led discovery, and saves are the growing signal in footwear |
| WeChat (微信) | Mini-documentaries from the Mallorca workshops | Owned audience, no algorithm to beat, room for substance |
| Weibo (微博) | One argument worth having, run as a thread | Public conversation at national scale |

Xiaohongshu got most of the energy. We worked with micro-influencers in
architecture, interiors and creative fields rather than fashion creators. The
notes went deep on materials and construction. Why the sole is shaped that
way. What the last is built from. It reached a smaller audience than a fashion
influencer push would have, and engagement was significantly higher.

Trading reach for depth looks like a bad deal on a media plan. The category
data says otherwise.

> In the first half of 2026, Xiaohongshu commercial notes in apparel grew
> saves by more than 60% year on year and interactions by more than 52%.
> Clothing and footwear took 68.34% of category interaction, ahead of jewelry
> and accessories at 22.38% and bags at 9.28%.
> Source: Qian-gua Data (千瓜数据), 2026 first-half industry data brief for Xiaohongshu, reported by NetEase (网易), July 2026. https://c.m.163.com/news/a/L29A904P05531DFD.html

Saves grew faster than interactions. A save is a person planning to buy later,
and content that explains a product earns saves in a way a lifestyle image
does not. Our fashion and apparel industry page traces the same discovery
path across the wider category.

WeChat took the long form. Mini-documentaries from the workshops, behind the
scenes of how the shoes get designed and built. Nobody has to beat a feed on
WeChat, so length is allowed to be useful instead of risky.

Weibo did one job. A thread asking whether shoes should be designed for how
they feel or how they look ran for three days and pulled in thousands of
comments. We didn't ask Weibo to do anything else.

What is worth explaining is why slow first months are the normal shape, not a
warning sign. Distribution on Xiaohongshu runs on recency.

> Xiaohongshu's own distribution engineers described a home feed built on
> recall, coarse ranking and fine ranking, rebuilt from daily to minute-level
> updates, in which notes published within the last day account for close to
> half of home feed exposure.
> Source: Xiaohongshu technology and intelligent distribution department (小红书技术部 / 智能分发部), published on AIQ (6AIQ), March 2023. https://www.6aiq.com/article/1679451572481

Every note competes mostly against notes published today, so no stock of past
work carries you in the feed. The compounding happens in search instead, and
search needs a body of notes that answer real queries. Month three looks flat
because the library isn't written yet.

<!-- SECTION: What did not work -->

## What sinks campaigns here, and the part we cannot publish

Three things sink foreign brand campaigns on these platforms. All three are
documented and cheap to avoid. They are market patterns, not Camper results.

The first is paying rate card for reach. Published influencer prices and
prices actually paid have been moving in opposite directions.

> China's influencer economy was forecast at 117.2 billion yuan for 2026. In
> 2025, official KOL rate cards rose 11.6% while prices actually paid fell
> 3.4%.
> Source: Campaign Asia, reporting R3's 2026 China Social KOLC Governance Whitepaper, January 2026. https://aws1.campaignasia.com/article/hidden-costs-emerge-in-chinas-16-8-billion-influencer-market/506973

A brand that accepts the published number is paying above market before the
campaign starts. The gap is the negotiation.

Then there is the paperwork, which quietly kills more campaigns than bad
creative does.

> Content that promotes goods through knowledge sharing, experience sharing or
> product reviews, with a purchase link attached, must be conspicuously
> labeled as advertising.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (国家市场监督管理总局), Internet Advertising Measures (互联网广告管理办法), Order 72, Article 9, in force May 2023. https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10506/202306/content_6885261.html

Undeclared commercial notes get suppressed. The campaign then reads as a
creative failure when it was a paperwork failure.

The last one is treating a campaign as an event. Given how much of the feed
goes to notes published in the last day, a burst with no follow-up decays
fast, and the chart looks like a spike followed by nothing.

Campaign-level post-mortems for this account aren't cleared for publication,
so none appear here. We're not going to reverse-engineer them from the three
patterns above either, which is what most teardowns quietly do.

## How social growth turned into a 31% sales lift

Xiaohongshu had the smallest follower count of the three platforms and sent
38% of all e-commerce traffic. That is the most useful number in the case.

The mechanism is intent. People arrive on Xiaohongshu (小红书) looking for
advice about something they're considering buying.

> Xiaohongshu said 39 million users a day show product purchase-seeking
> behavior, amounting to 140 million instances a day.
> Source: Xiaohongshu e-commerce GROW merchant conference, reported by 36Kr (36氪), April 2026. https://www.36kr.com/newsflashes/3758099821871879

A reader who searched for how a shoe is constructed, and then read a note
about it, is much closer to buying than a reader who was shown a campaign
image. Intent is why the smallest audience moved the most traffic, and why
retention and repeat purchase, the two slowest metrics in retail, moved at
all.

The category wasn't doing the work. Clothing and footwear retail in China has
been growing in low single digits.

> Retail sales of clothing, footwear, hats and knitwear grew 3.2% in 2025
> across units above the designated size, against total retail growth of 3.7%.
> Source: National Bureau of Statistics of China (国家统计局), 2025 Statistical Communique, February 2026. https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202602/t20260228_1962662.html

Against a 3.2% category, a 31% same-store lift isn't the tide.

<!-- SECTION: The reporting -->

## The reporting we used, and what we measured

Weekly reviews, run by the team in China, in Chinese, on the platforms' own
data. Not a monthly deck assembled after the fact.

Three things were read every week. Note-level diagnosis on Xiaohongshu
(小红书), which attributes reads and tells you whether a note was found in
search or served in the feed. Then two measures that are ours rather than the
platforms': follower quality on WeChat (微信), meaning who stayed subscribed
after a long-form piece rather than who opened it once, and comment substance
on Weibo (微博), because the thread that ran three days was worth more than
the ones that got polite likes.

> Xiaohongshu's creator data center offers a per-note diagnosis with specific
> suggestions, and attributes reads to recommendation, search, shares or the
> profile page.
> Source: Niaoge Biji (鸟哥笔记), March 2024. https://www.niaogebiji.com/article-666136-1.html

That attribution split is the report that matters. A note carried by
recommendation tells you the creative worked this week. A note carried by
search tells you the library is starting to pay, which is the thing that makes
month 14 look different from month 4.

Follower count was never the headline. It went last.

<!-- SECTION: What we would change -->

## What we would do differently in 2026

Three changes, and the first is the big one.

The program covered WeChat, Xiaohongshu and Weibo. Douyin (抖音) wasn't part
of it, and for a footwear brand today that's a gap. Shoes are a category
people want to see move.

Second, we would build the Xiaohongshu store and the content around the same
plan from month one rather than treating commerce as a later phase. The
platform has spent the intervening period turning seeding into something it
measures.

> Xiaohongshu said it works closely with more than 2,100 brands, and that the
> number of merchants using its seeding-direct product grew 109% over the 618
> festival period.
> Source: Economic Observer (经济观察网), December 2025. http://m.eeo.com.cn/2025/1223/774754.shtml

Third, video on WeChat. When this program ran, the Official Account article
was the natural home for a mini-documentary. That is no longer the only option.

> Tencent said WeChat Channels total user time grew more than 20% year on year
> in 2025, credited to a long-sequence model recommendation upgrade.
> Source: Tencent 2025 annual results, reported by China Fund News (中国基金报), March 2026. https://www.chnfund.com/article/AR1231fcdd-25ff-01cd-2836-3a2015e46b1d

The workshop films would be cut for Channels now, not only for the article.

What we would not change is the constraint. No discount campaigns is a hard
brief, and it is the reason the content had to be about the shoes.

<!-- SECTION: FAQ -->

## Questions foreign brands ask about this

**How long does it take to grow a Chinese social account?**

Plan in quarters, not weeks. The Camper accounts took 18 months to go from
43,000 to 187,000 followers, and the first few months were slow. On
Xiaohongshu (小红书), where notes from the last day take close to half of home
feed exposure, growth compounds through search rather than through old posts,
and a searchable library takes months to build.

**What content format grows a brand account fastest in China?**

The format that answers a question. In the first half of 2026, saves on
Xiaohongshu apparel notes grew faster than interactions, and a save is a
person planning to buy later. For Camper, notes on materials and construction
from creators in architecture and interiors outperformed a broader fashion
influencer push on engagement.

**Does follower growth actually drive sales in China?**

Not on its own. Follower count is the weakest predictor on the list. In the
Camper case the platform with the fewest followers sent the most store
traffic, and the metrics that moved alongside sales were retention and repeat
purchase. Judge a China program on traffic, retention and repeat purchase,
and read follower count last.
