# Which China social media platform to start on: how to pick your first three

Pick three platforms, not one. WeChat is the base for almost every brand, and
four questions about your product and your buyer pick the other two.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

Which China social media platform should a foreign brand start on? Most should
start on three, chosen by how the business sells rather than by how big each
app is.

WeChat (微信) holds the customers you win. The other two slots go to the apps
where your buyers find and judge products like yours. Usually that means two
of Xiaohongshu (小红书), Douyin (抖音), Kuaishou (快手), Bilibili (哔哩哔哩), and Weibo
(微博). Four questions decide which two.

| Question                  | Answer A          | Points to           | Answer B            | Points to        |
|---------------------------|-------------------|---------------------|---------------------|------------------|
| How do buyers find you?   | They search first | Xiaohongshu         | They see it and buy | Douyin, Kuaishou |
| What is your price point? | Premium           | Xiaohongshu         | Mass market         | Douyin, Kuaishou |
| Who sells it in China?    | You, direct       | Douyin              | A distributor       | Xiaohongshu      |
| Who signs off on the buy? | One consumer      | Xiaohongshu, Douyin | A committee         | WeChat and WeCom |

The scoring table further down turns these answers into numbers. It takes
about five minutes. Every figure in this article was checked against its
source in October 2026.

<!-- SECTION: Why three -->

## Why three platforms, and not one or eight

One platform misses too many buyers. Chinese users split their time across
several apps, and each app draws a different crowd.

> In October 2025, 39.8% of users of China's main content platforms used three
> or more of them.
> Source: QuestMobile, 2025 new media report, reported by 36Kr (36氪), December 2025. https://www.36kr.com/p/3597577937502473

The same report shows how far apart those crowds sit.

> Users under 30 make up 58.6% of Weibo (微博), 54.2% of Xiaohongshu (小红书), and
> 66.5% of Bilibili (哔哩哔哩). Users aged 51 and over are 22.2% of Douyin (抖音).
> Both figures are for October 2025.
> Source: QuestMobile, reported by 36Kr (36氪), December 2025. https://www.36kr.com/p/3597577937502473

Eight platforms fail for a different reason. Each one wants its own format:
long articles on WeChat (微信), photo notes on Xiaohongshu, vertical video on
Douyin. Eight formats means eight content plans. Few first-year budgets can
feed that many.

Chinese advertisers feel the same strain.

> 45% of Chinese advertisers named media fragmentation as a top challenge for
> 2026. Zhongcao (种草) platforms, where users recommend products to each other,
> led planned spending increases at 76%, ahead of short video at 56%.
> Source: Miaozhen Marketing Science Institute (秒针营销科学院), 2026 China Digital Marketing Trends Report, reported by China News Service (中新网), January 2026. https://www.sh.chinanews.com.cn/chanjing/2026-01-14/144009.shtml

Cost works the same way. Every platform you add needs its own content and
someone to answer the comments. Often it needs its own ads as well. Our
pricing page lists what each package covers on the core platforms.

Three gives you a home base, a place to be found, and a place to reach people
at scale. No platform publishes that split. It's the working rule we use when
we set up accounts for foreign brands.

Why WeChat for slot one? It's where customers come back after the first sale,
to ask about a late parcel or to reorder. And almost everyone in China has it.

> WeChat and its international version had 1.439 billion combined monthly
> active accounts in the second quarter of 2026.
> Source: Tencent Holdings Q2 2026 results, reported by Jiemian (界面新闻) via 21st Century Business Herald (21财经), August 2026. https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260812/herald/0c6b89715bfa13d4ecf96e41a685bdcd.html

## Which China social media platform fits you: the four questions

The four questions below are about your buyers and your sales model. App size
comes into it less than you'd think. Answer each one for your main product in
your main target city. If you sell two very different products, run the test
twice.

<!-- SECTION: Question 1 -->

### Question 1: do buyers search for you, or stumble on you

Some products get searched. A shopper looks up a sunscreen for oily skin, or a
hotel near the station in Lyon, and reads reviews before buying.

Others get found by accident. Nobody searches for a snack they've never heard
of. They see it in a video, and they tap.

Not sure which camp you're in? Type your product category into the search bar
on Xiaohongshu (小红书). If real users are already asking about it, you're in the
first.

Searched products belong on Xiaohongshu. Chinese shoppers use it the way many
Western shoppers use Google plus a review site.

> Xiaohongshu passed 400 million monthly active users, with 800 million
> searches a day.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) Video Creator Trend Observation Report, reported by Beijing Business Today (北京商报), May 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-05-27/doc-inhziqxq9291575.shtml

> 77% of Xiaohongshu's daily active users use search to solve a problem.
> Source: Huxiu (虎嗅), May 2026. https://www.huxiu.com/article/4861801.html

Discovered products belong on Douyin (抖音), where the feed decides what people
see. Search is growing there too, but most of it starts with a video.

> Douyin had about 1.03 billion monthly active users in June 2026, second only
> to WeChat (微信).
> Source: QuestMobile H1 2026 app ranking, reported by Sina Tech (新浪科技), August 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/discovery/2026-08-04/doc-inimcxxp2069391.shtml

> On Douyin, 53% of searches come after watching a video and 47% are started
> by the user.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎) 2025 search white paper, reported by China Daily (中国日报网), November 2025. https://caijing.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202511/25/WS69252082a310942cc499329e.html

<!-- SECTION: Question 2 -->

### Question 2: is your product premium or mass market

Price changes how long people think. A cheap product can sell on one good
video. An expensive one gets researched for weeks, sometimes months.

Premium brands should weight Xiaohongshu (小红书) and WeChat (微信). Xiaohongshu's
users lean toward bigger cities.

> 37.2% of Xiaohongshu users live in first-tier and new first-tier cities,
> October 2025.
> Source: QuestMobile, reported by 36Kr (36氪), December 2025. https://www.36kr.com/p/3597577937502473

Luxury shows how long that research can run.

> More than 30 luxury brands run official stores on Xiaohongshu. A luxury
> buyer's path from first read to purchase can take more than 90 days.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) data, reported by Luxe.co (华丽志), June 2026. https://luxe.co/post/468870

A 90-day decision needs somewhere to keep the conversation going. That's
WeChat's job, so a premium brand puts Xiaohongshu and WeChat to work together
from the start.

Mass-market brands face the opposite problem. They need volume in smaller
cities, and they need it fast. Douyin (抖音) and Kuaishou (快手) reach furthest
there.

> Douyin's monthly active users in lower-tier markets passed 500 million.
> Source: QuestMobile, reported by 36Kr (36氪), July 2026. https://www.36kr.com/p/3884935514648839

> 56.3% of Kuaishou users live in third-tier cities or smaller, October 2025.
> Source: QuestMobile, reported by DoNews, December 2025. https://www.donews.com/news/detail/1/6308940.html

<!-- SECTION: Question 3 -->

### Question 3: do you sell direct, or through a distributor

This question decides whether a platform has to close the sale or only start
it.

If you sell direct, the checkout matters, and each app sets its own rules for
who may open a store. A foreign company should check them before it picks.

| Store                                   | Who can open it                                                  |
|-----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Douyin Global Purchase (抖音全球购)     | A company registered outside the mainland, plus a mainland agent |
| Xiaohongshu (小红书) cross-border store | An overseas company with a brand authorization letter            |
| WeChat Store (微信小店)                 | Companies registered in mainland China only                      |

> Douyin's cross-border store needs a company registered outside the mainland
> and a mainland agent that shares liability.
> Source: Douyin E-commerce Learning Center (抖音电商学习中心), entry rules revised July 2026. https://school.jinritemai.com/doudian/web/article/108056

> Xiaohongshu cross-border stores need an authorization letter from the brand
> owner for every brand sold.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) merchant qualification requirements, platform document confirmed September 2026. https://picasso-static.xiaohongshu.com/test/8b3bc7324ca06fceea379177f9eed1fa/%E5%B0%8F%E7%BA%A2%E4%B9%A6%E8%B5%84%E8%B4%A8%E5%85%A5%E9%A9%BB%E8%A6%81%E6%B1%82.pdf

> WeChat Store accepts only entities legally registered in mainland China.
> Source: WeChat Store (微信小店) entry rules, June 2026. https://store.weixin.qq.com/chengzhang/webdoc/wiki/2063/9c63c672daee8eca/growth_center_rule_for_store

So a brand selling direct from abroad usually leans on Douyin for the sale.
WeChat's own store stays closed until you have a China-registered company. Our
guide on whether you need a Chinese entity covers that side of the choice.

A distributor changes the job. You don't need a checkout, because the
distributor already has one, on a marketplace or in stores you may never
visit. What you need is people typing your brand name into a search bar and
reading what other buyers say. Then they go and find the product wherever the
distributor stocks it.

That points back to Xiaohongshu.

<!-- SECTION: Question 4 -->

### Question 4: who signs off, a consumer or a committee

Think about who approved the last order for a product like yours. If it was
one person, often in one evening, you sell to consumers. If it went through
meetings and a budget sign-off, with a chat thread that never quite ends, you
sell to a committee.

For consumer brands, this question rarely moves the result. For B2B brands, it
decides the first platform. It also takes away the channel Western B2B teams
know best. LinkedIn no longer works inside China.

> LinkedIn closed its social features in China in 2021 and stopped its China
> job app on August 9, 2023.
> Source: China National Radio (央广网), May 2023. https://tech.cnr.cn/ycbd/20230509/t20230509_526245629.shtml

Chinese business buyers talk to suppliers on WeChat (微信). Suppliers answer
through WeCom (企业微信), the business version of WeChat.

> WeCom connects more than 14 million companies and organizations, which serve
> more than 750 million WeChat users a day through it.
> Source: Xinhua (新华网), August 2025. http://www.news.cn/tech/20250820/fbe9a69fd7a742629cf8275238bb7b91/c.html

With a committee, WeChat stops being only the base and becomes the main stage.
Xiaohongshu (小红书) can still help, through staff accounts that answer questions
in public.

> More than 40,000 companies run employee accounts on Xiaohongshu, known as
> KOS (key opinion sales). Those accounts brought in more than 6 million leads
> a month in August 2026.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) 2026 KOS guide, reported by Huaxia Morning News (华夏早报), September 2026. https://baby.ifeng.com/c/8wUkh912dXd

The platform doesn't say how many of those leads come from business buyers.

## The scoring table: run your own brand through it

That's the hard part done. The rest is arithmetic.

WeChat (微信) already holds slot one. This table picks slots two and three.

For each question, choose answer A or B. Take the first number in each cell
for A, the second for B. Add up each row. Your two highest totals are your
second and third platforms.

| Platform             | Q1 search / see | Q2 premium / mass | Q3 direct / distributor | Q4 consumer / committee |
|----------------------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------------|
| Xiaohongshu (小红书) | 2 / 1           | 2 / 1             | 1 / 2                   | 2 / 1                   |
| Douyin (抖音)        | 1 / 2           | 1 / 2             | 2 / 1                   | 2 / 0                   |
| Kuaishou (快手)      | 0 / 2           | 0 / 2             | 1 / 0                   | 1 / 0                   |
| Bilibili (哔哩哔哩)  | 1 / 0           | 1 / 0             | 0 / 0                   | 1 / 1                   |
| Weibo (微博)         | 0 / 1           | 1 / 1             | 0 / 1                   | 1 / 0                   |

A zero means the platform does little for that answer. The points are our
judgment, not a platform score. They rest on the figures in this article, from
where each app's users live to which stores a foreign company can open.

Take a French skincare brand sold through a cross-border distributor. It
answers search, premium, distributor, and consumer.

| Platform    | Q1  | Q2  | Q3  | Q4  | Total |
|-------------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-------|
| Xiaohongshu | 2   | 2   | 2   | 2   | 8     |
| Douyin      | 1   | 1   | 1   | 2   | 5     |
| Bilibili    | 1   | 1   | 0   | 1   | 3     |
| Weibo       | 0   | 1   | 1   | 1   | 3     |
| Kuaishou    | 0   | 0   | 0   | 1   | 1     |

Its first three are WeChat, Xiaohongshu, and Douyin.

A snack brand selling direct through Douyin's cross-border store gets a
different answer. It picks see, mass, direct, and consumer. Douyin scores 8,
Kuaishou 6, and Xiaohongshu 5, so its three are WeChat, Douyin, and Kuaishou.

Ties happen. When two platforms tie, pick the one your team can actually feed
with content every week, holidays included.

## Four common shortlists, and who they suit

Run the scoring table for four common types of business and you get these four
shortlists. Where two platforms finish close, the table shows both.

| Shortlist        | Slot 1                        | Slot 2               | Slot 3                         | Suits                                   |
|------------------|-------------------------------|----------------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Consumer premium | WeChat (微信)                 | Xiaohongshu (小红书) | Douyin (抖音)                  | Beauty, fashion, jewelry, premium food  |
| Mass consumer    | WeChat                        | Douyin               | Kuaishou (快手) or Xiaohongshu | Snacks, home goods, everyday products   |
| B2B              | WeChat, with WeCom (企业微信) | Xiaohongshu          | Bilibili (哔哩哔哩) or Douyin  | Industrial, software, building products |
| Travel           | WeChat                        | Xiaohongshu          | Douyin                         | Hotels, destinations, tour operators    |

**Consumer premium.** Xiaohongshu does the research work. Douyin adds reach at
launch. WeChat keeps the buyer after the first order.

**Mass consumer.** Douyin carries the volume. The third slot depends on where
your buyers live. Kuaishou fits buyers in smaller cities. If yours compare
before they buy, take Xiaohongshu instead.

**B2B.** WeChat and WeCom carry the sales conversation. Xiaohongshu staff
accounts answer questions in public. Bilibili suits engineers who watch long
explainers. Douyin suits a product that shows well in 30 seconds.

Travel is the clearest case of all. Chinese travelers plan their trips on
Xiaohongshu, and many book hotels through Douyin live rooms.

> Xiaohongshu has more than 230 million monthly travel-interest users, and
> over 80% of them check travel guides on the app.
> Source: TravelDaily (环球旅讯), reporting Xiaohongshu commercial data for November to December 2025, January 2026. https://www.traveldaily.cn/article/189197

> 32% of Douyin users have ordered hotel stays through a live room.
> Source: China Tourism News (中国旅游报), June 2026. https://www.ctnews.com.cn/dongtai/content/2026-06/18/content_188669.html

## What to add in year two

Add a fourth platform only once the first three publish on schedule and report
results you trust. Building an audience in China takes months of steady
posting, so give the first three time. When you're ready, look first at the
row that came fourth in your scoring table. The table below covers the four
usual candidates.

| Platform                 | Scale, latest figure                 | Add it when                             |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Weibo (微博)             | 561 million monthly users, June 2026 | You have launches, celebrities, or news |
| Bilibili (哔哩哔哩)      | 371 million monthly users, Q2 2026   | Your product needs a long explanation   |
| Kuaishou (快手)          | 797 million monthly users, Q2 2026   | You want smaller cities after Douyin    |
| WeChat Channels (视频号) | Tencent publishes no user count      | Your WeChat base needs video            |

## Frequently asked questions

**How many China platforms should a brand start with?**

Three suits most brands. WeChat (微信) holds the customers you win, and two more
platforms bring new ones in. One platform misses too many buyers. QuestMobile
found that 39.8% of users of China's main content apps used three or more of
them in October 2025. More than three spreads a first-year content budget too
thin to publish well anywhere.

**Which China platform is best for a premium product?**

Xiaohongshu (小红书), with WeChat (微信) behind it. Premium buyers research before
they spend, and Xiaohongshu is where they search, with 800 million searches a
day in May 2026. A long decision then needs a place to keep talking, which is
WeChat's job. Douyin (抖音) works as the third platform, mainly for launches.

**Should B2B brands use Douyin?**

Not first. B2B buyers decide in committees, and those conversations run on
WeChat (微信) and WeCom (企业微信). No platform or trade publication publishes a
figure for B2B buyers on Douyin (抖音). It earns the third slot when your
product shows well in a 30-second video, such as a machine at work. Otherwise,
the longer explainers on Bilibili (哔哩哔哩) fit better.
