# Is Weibo still relevant in 2026? Who it reaches and when it is worth the budget

Yes, but for a narrower job than it used to do. Weibo is now where launches and
public news play out, and where celebrity campaigns live. It's no longer the
place to grow a community one post at a time.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

Is Weibo still relevant? Look at the size first. Weibo (微博) had 561 million
monthly users in June 2026. Xiaohongshu (小红书) says it has passed 400 million.
But the bigger number hides how people use the app. They open Weibo to see what
is happening right now. They research a purchase somewhere else, and they buy
somewhere else again.

The useful question is narrower. Does your brand have moments the public will
talk about?

| Weibo vs Xiaohongshu      | Weibo (微博)                        | Xiaohongshu (小红书)           |
|---------------------------|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Monthly users             | 561 million, June 2026              | Over 400 million, May 2026     |
| Users under 30            | 58.6%, October 2025                 | 54.2%, October 2025            |
| Tier-one and new tier-one | 36.6%, October 2025                 | 37.2%, October 2025            |
| What people go there for  | News, trends, and celebrities       | Product research before buying |
| Best job for a brand      | Launches, news, and public response | Search visibility and reviews  |

Weibo's count comes from its quarterly results, Xiaohongshu's from the platform
itself. These are the latest figures each had published as of October 2026, and
every one is sourced below and was checked twice.

## Is Weibo still relevant? What it is for in 2026, and what it is not

The biggest change came in 2025. The home page of Weibo (微博) used to lead with
posts from accounts you follow. Now it leads with what the system recommends.

> Weibo switched to a new home page built around a recommendation feed in late
> July 2025. With that feed as its main channel, Weibo says, content can travel
> past the old follower relationships.
> Source: Weibo Q4 and full-year 2025 results, reported by Sina Tech (新浪科技), March 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/2026-03-18/doc-inhrmayp7470775.shtml

That cuts both ways. A big follower count buys less than it used to. A strong
post can reach people who have never heard of you (good news for a brand that
arrives with no followers at all).

Advertisers are still paying for it.

> Weibo's advertising revenue reached 1.5 billion US dollars in 2025.
> Source: Weibo Q4 and full-year 2025 results, reported by Sina Tech (新浪科技), March 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/2026-03-18/doc-inhrmayp7470775.shtml

> Weibo's ad revenue was 381 million US dollars in the second quarter of 2026,
> down 1%. Phone and game advertisers spent less, while internet services and
> car brands spent more.
> Source: The Paper (澎湃新闻), on 21st Century Business Herald (21财经), August 2026. https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260819/herald/aa73a001934288af9e21e49f5b527807.html

What Weibo is not: a research tool for shoppers, a store, or a place to keep
customers. Shoppers do their homework on Xiaohongshu (小红书). They buy on Douyin (抖音) or
the big e-commerce sites, and once they're customers, brands keep them on WeChat
(微信).

## Who is still on Weibo: audience, age, and behavior

The audience is huge, and it is shrinking slowly.

> Weibo (微博) had 561 million monthly active users and 254 million daily active
> users in June 2026.
> Source: Weibo Q2 2026 results, reported by Sina Finance (新浪财经), August 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/usstock/c/2026-08-19/doc-ininvwqk8304798.shtml

> Weibo had 567 million monthly active users at the end of 2025.
> Source: Weibo Q4 and full-year 2025 results, reported by Sina Tech (新浪科技), March 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/2026-03-18/doc-inhrmayp7470775.shtml

It skews young. It isn't a big-city app, either: most of its users live outside
China's top-tier cities.

> Users under 30 make up 58.6% of Weibo (微博) and 54.2% of Xiaohongshu (小红书).
> Users in tier-one and new tier-one cities make up 36.6% of Weibo and 37.2% of
> Xiaohongshu. Both figures are for October 2025.
> Source: QuestMobile, 2025 new media report, reported by 36Kr (36氪), December 2025. https://www.36kr.com/p/3597577937502473

Behavior matters more than age. People come to Weibo to find out what is going
on, and more of them now ask the app directly.

> Monthly users of Weibo's AI search, Zhisou (智搜), passed 80 million in December
> 2025.
> Source: Weibo Q4 and full-year 2025 results, reported by Sina Tech (新浪科技), March 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/2026-03-18/doc-inhrmayp7470775.shtml

Already running a Weibo account and watching reach slide? Check the dates first.
If the drop started in the second half of 2025, the home page switch is a likely
cause, and it isn't your agency's doing.

## The three jobs Weibo still does best

The first is launch day. Posts on Weibo (微博) are public, so anyone can see them and
repost them, reporters included. Weibo says AI model makers now treat it as
their first stop.

> Weibo says it has become the first-choice platform for AI model makers to
> launch products, explain the technology, and build word of mouth.
> Source: Weibo Q4 and full-year 2025 results, reported by Sina Tech (新浪科技), March 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/2026-03-18/doc-inhrmayp7470775.shtml

That's Weibo talking about itself. It does show who the platform is courting:
companies with something new to announce.

Our work for the lighting brand iGuzzini used Weibo the same way.

> "Weibo gave us broader reach. Campaign launches, design events, trending
> architecture content. It put the name in front of people who hadn't been
> looking for a lighting brand."
> Source: TheRedScroll, iGuzzini case study page, checked October 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/work/iguzzini/

Public response comes next. Think of a delayed shipment, a rude store clerk, or
an ad that lands badly. When that happens, Chinese users argue about it on
Weibo, in the open. A clear answer from your verified account lands in the same
feed as the complaint, where the people arguing can see it. That only works if
someone reads the comments every day and can answer within hours.

Then there's the celebrity moment. Weibo is built for fandom. It even runs its
own verification route for celebrity fan clubs.

> Weibo's institution verification covers official celebrity support groups,
> celebrity fan clubs, and celebrity studios, alongside sports teams and
> museums.
> Source: Weibo customer service (微博客服), institution verification, updated August 2026. https://kefu.weibo.com/faqdetail?id=20074

And Weibo is selling that hard to advertisers.

> Celebrity marketing was one of the priorities Weibo kept pushing in the second
> quarter of 2026.
> Source: The Paper (澎湃新闻), on 21st Century Business Herald (21财经), August 2026. https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260819/herald/aa73a001934288af9e21e49f5b527807.html

| Job              | Why Weibo does it best               | If you skip Weibo, use                            | What you give up                         |
|------------------|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Launch day       | Public posts travel past followers   | Douyin (抖音) video, Xiaohongshu (小红书) reviews | A public place where news gets picked up |
| Public response  | Open threads where the argument runs | A WeChat (微信) Official Account statement        | Your answer next to the complaint        |
| Celebrity tie-in | Verified fan clubs and star accounts | A Douyin live session with the star               | Fans reposting on your behalf            |

## How Weibo hot search actually works

Hot search (热搜) is the trending list on Weibo (微博). The short version: a brand can earn a
place, buy a labeled ad slot, or rise through a topic list. It can't buy a rank.
The help center gives the basics.

> Weibo ranks its hot search list on search heat and on how many people join the
> topic discussion. It also weighs how people interact with posts on the results
> page.
> Source: Weibo customer service (微博客服), hot search ranking rules, updated March 2026. https://kefu.weibo.com/faqdetail?id=20023

> The real-time hot search list updates every minute and ranks keywords by
> short-term search volume. Weibo monitors it for manipulation.
> Source: Weibo customer service (微博客服), real-time hot search rules, updated October 2024. https://kefu.weibo.com/faqdetail?id=20020

The full rulebook dates from 2021. The help center page above still links to it.

> Under the 2021 rules, heat is search heat plus discussion heat plus spread
> heat, multiplied by the interaction rate. It is recalculated every minute, and
> the top 50 are shown. Two ad slots sit outside the ranking, under the third
> and sixth entries, and commercial ads carry a 商 label.
> Source: Weibo hot search rules, reported by The Paper (澎湃新闻), August 2021. https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_14166759

The law backs the rule.

> China's algorithm rules bar platforms from manipulating rankings or
> controlling hot search to sway online opinion.
> Source: Cyberspace Administration of China (国家互联网信息办公室), algorithmic recommendation rules (互联网信息服务算法推荐管理规定), article 14, in force March 2022. https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-01/04/c_1642894606364259.htm

Late in 2025, Weibo reshaped the top of the list. "Vertical" here means the
topic lists it runs for subjects such as sports, tech, and anime, comics, and
games.

> In November 2025, Weibo split the top ten into three groups, public affairs,
> vertical topics, and entertainment, at about 30% each. Hot topics from
> vertical lists such as sports and tech can now qualify for the top ten.
> Source: Xinhua (新华网), November 2025. http://www.news.cn/tech/20251129/5cee901ca12643bda875ee04d323b5a6/c.html

> From January to March 2026, entertainment took 31% of the main list, social
> topics 31%, and vertical topics 38%. Weibo removed about 40 misleading entries
> a day.
> Source: Weibo Q1 2026 hot search report, reported by Cover News (封面新闻), May 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2026-05-21/doc-inhysaik9066055.shtml

The 商 on those two ad slots means commercial, so readers can tell them apart.

Anyone selling a guaranteed organic ranking is selling something the rules
forbid.

## Campaign bursts or always-on: where the Weibo budget should go

Hot search covers the big moments. The harder budget question is what you pay
for between them.

Our view, based on how the new feed hands out reach: a light always-on base plus
a few funded bursts suits most foreign brands.

The base is a verified account, steady posts, and someone reading the comments
in Chinese every day. The bursts are your launches and events, plus any
celebrity tie-in. That is where the paid boosts, the KOL posts, and any hot
search slot belong.

Say you open a store in Shanghai in March and launch a new product line in
September. That's two bursts. In between, the account posts a few times a week
and answers every comment.

The recommendation feed is why. Reach now depends more on how a post performs
than on who follows you. A strong burst finds people who have never seen your
account. A routine post that nobody engages with goes almost nowhere.

| Plan             | What runs                            | It fits when                | Watch out for                     |
|------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Always-on only   | Daily posts and comment replies      | You have news every week    | Little reach between moments      |
| Bursts only      | Nothing between campaigns            | You launch once a year      | An empty account when people look |
| Base plus bursts | Light posting, monitoring, campaigns | You have a few real moments | Needs a calendar of real moments  |

If you post every day and get nothing back, your agency may be doing exactly
what the plan asked. In that case, change the plan first.

## Working with Weibo KOLs and the celebrity tier

Sponsored creator posts on Weibo (微博) run through Weirenwu (微任务), Weibo's own
marketplace. Creators set a price there, and Weibo approves it before brands can
order.

> Weibo treats a post as commercial if it tags a brand account or uses a brand
> hashtag. A link to another shopping site counts too, and so does a visible
> product or logo. Its system flags commercial posts automatically. Weirenwu is
> its marketplace for commercial content.
> Source: Weibo customer service (微博客服), commercial post rules, updated November 2024. https://kefu.weibo.com/faqdetail?id=21382

A paid post dressed up as a casual one is likely to be caught. Book through the
marketplace and the deal is on the record. If an agency books creators for you,
ask for the Weirenwu order and the live post link for each one.

Celebrities are a separate tier, with separate rules.

> Celebrities may not endorse products they have not used. Nor may they endorse
> tobacco and e-cigarettes, after-school tutoring, medical services, drugs,
> medical devices, health food, or special medical formula food.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation, Cyberspace Administration of China, and five other regulators, endorsement guidance, November 2022. https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-11/02/c_1669013796039375.htm

> China's internet regulator ordered all celebrity ranking lists canceled in
> August 2021.
> Source: Xinhua (新华网), August 2021. http://www.xinhuanet.com/2021-08/27/c_1127801497.htm

For a brand, that means two things. If you sell supplements or medical products,
the celebrity route is closed. And because fan rankings are gone, judge a
celebrity on real engagement and fit, not on a chart.

## When to skip Weibo entirely

Some brands should wait on Weibo (微博). Here's the quick test.

| Run Weibo if                                           | Skip Weibo for now if                                 |
|--------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| You have launches, events, or news a few times a year  | Your news calendar is empty for months at a time      |
| Your buyers follow celebrities, sports, games, or tech | Your buyers research quietly before they buy          |
| Someone can answer comments in Chinese the same day    | Nobody can watch the comments daily                   |
| Your category can use celebrities                      | You sell health food, medical products, or tutoring   |
| WeChat and one discovery app already run well          | Your budget covers one platform, so start with WeChat |

Already on Weibo with little to say? You don't have to delete anything. Keep the
verified account, keep answering comments, and stop paying for daily posts
nobody asked for.

B2B brands selling to buying committees can usually wait too. WeChat (微信) and
WeCom (企业微信) carry that conversation. Our guide to choosing your first China platforms
scores Weibo against the others in about five minutes.

## Weibo questions brands ask

### Is Weibo still used in China?

Very much so. Weibo (微博) had 561 million monthly users and 254 million daily users in
June 2026, according to its second-quarter results. That is down from 567
million at the end of 2025, so the audience is shrinking slowly. People use it
to follow the news and their favorite stars, and its AI search had more than 80
million monthly users by December 2025.

### Should a foreign brand be on Weibo?

If you have launches, events, or celebrity tie-ins, yes. Weibo is where Chinese
news and fan talk happen in public. If your buyers research quietly and you have
little news, start with WeChat (微信) and Xiaohongshu (小红书) and add Weibo later. When you do
join, budget for enterprise verification, which Weibo charges at 600 yuan per
application.

### How does Weibo hot search work?

Weibo ranks hot search on search heat, discussion, and interaction, and the
real-time list updates every minute. Since November 2025, the top ten has been
split roughly evenly between public affairs, vertical topics, and entertainment.
Under Weibo's 2021 rules, two labeled ad slots sit outside the ranking. China's
algorithm rules forbid platforms from manipulating the list.
