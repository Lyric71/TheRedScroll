# Change your China marketing agency: the handover checklist

You can change your China marketing agency without losing your accounts. The
risk sits in anything an agency registered under its own company, and you
need to find it before you give notice.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

The reports stopped making sense, or the results stalled, and you want out.
If you plan to change your China marketing agency, the weeks before you say
so are the most useful ones. Use them to find out what you actually control.

In China, a social account belongs to whichever company passed the
platform’s verification. Ad money sits in prepaid balances with their own
refund rules. Content belongs to whoever the contract says, and if the
contract says nothing, the law gives it to the maker. A termination letter
changes none of that.

Here is where each asset usually sits, and how you get it back. Every
platform rule below was checked twice against the platform’s own pages in
October 2026.

| Asset | Who usually holds it | How you get it back |
|---|---|---|
| WeChat Official Account | The company that passed verification | Admin change if verified on your license; migration if not |
| Douyin and Weibo accounts | The verified entity | Only with the platform’s consent, and usually the agency’s help |
| Ad accounts | Your entity or the agency’s | Admin access if in your name; otherwise a platform ruling |
| Ad balance | The platform, as prepaid credit | Cash refund; unspent bonus credit is lost |
| Content and source files | The agency, unless the contract says otherwise | A written copyright assignment and file delivery |
| Creator relationships | The agency’s address book | Your platform order history and a creator list |
| Followers | The account, never a spreadsheet | Keep the account; an ID export does not move followers |

<!-- SECTION: Before you give notice -->

## Before you change your China marketing agency: five things to secure

Once notice is out, every request depends on the old agency’s goodwill. Do
the quiet work first.

- An account register: every account on every platform, with the company
  name that passed verification. On WeChat it shows in the account’s entity
  information.
- The admin’s identity. Find out whose personal WeChat is the admin on the
  Official Account (it is always a person, not a company). If it belongs to
  an agency employee, plan the change now.
- A balance statement for each ad account, split into cash, bonus credit and
  any credit line.
- The contract’s notice period and copyright clause. If there’s no
  copyright clause, the default works against you.
- Your creator history: every creator order placed in your name, with the
  usage rights you paid for.

<!-- SECTION: WeChat Official Account ownership -->

## Who actually owns your WeChat Official Account

Strictly speaking, Tencent does. The WeChat (微信) agreement says so
plainly.

> Tencent owns every Official Account. The right to use it belongs to the
> entity that passed the qualification review, and gifting, lending, renting,
> transferring or selling that right is prohibited.
> Source: Tencent (腾讯), WeChat Official Account Platform service agreement,
> clause 6.1, confirmed October 2026.
> https://mp.weixin.qq.com/cgi-bin/readtemplate?lang=zh_CN&t=home%2Fagreement_tmpl&token=&type=info

So which entity passed review? If it was your company, the account is yours
to use, and the handover is mostly a change of admin.

> To change the admin on a company account, the current admin first scans a
> code with their WeChat. Then the new admin’s ID and phone number are entered
> and verified.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), admin information change guide,
> confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/120911VrYVrA150527bmIVJZ.html

What if the agency’s admin won’t scan? If your company is the verified
entity, Tencent’s account recovery process swaps the admin’s WeChat, the
operator’s ID details and the login email together.

If the agency’s company passed verification, the account is the agency’s to
use. You cannot be handed it. What you can do is migrate its followers and
articles into an account your company owns.

> An overseas account can migrate to a mainland China account or to an
> account in the same overseas region. A mainland account cannot migrate to
> an overseas one, and an Official Account (formerly a subscription account)
> cannot migrate to a service account.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), account migration guide,
> confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/170221uQ7Vz2170221ZzIZv6.html

That rule hurts foreign brands most. If your company only exists overseas,
a mainland account verified by your agency cannot move into it. You would
need a mainland entity of your own to receive it.

Don’t assume the agency acted in bad faith. Tencent’s rules for overseas
companies are tight.

> An overseas entity can register one account, overseas regions support the
> service account type only, and the account works only after verification.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), overseas service account
> registration guide, confirmed October 2026.
> https://kf.qq.com/faq/180312zQjeYV180312FNNbmA.html

Plenty of agencies verified on their own license because it was the quick
way to launch. The brand was glad of it at the time. Followers piled up
on the agency’s license. Nobody revisited the setup, because nothing was
wrong with it.

The problem only shows when you try to leave.

When migration is possible, it needs the agency’s signature.

> Migration between different entities costs a 300 yuan review fee, needs a
> notarized application letter, and both admins must confirm. The target
> account can have no more than 1,000 followers if all followers move, and
> the old account is reclaimed.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), account migration guide,
> confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/170221uQ7Vz2170221ZzIZv6.html

Other platforms work the same way. Douyin’s (抖音) enterprise account
agreement, in force since August 2026, bans transfer without the platform’s
approval. Weibo (微博) bans it
without Weibo’s consent, and adds a lock of its own.

> Weibo routes an overseas-to-overseas entity change to the overseas agent
> that served the account at verification.
> Source: Sina Weibo customer service (新浪微博客服), enterprise entity change
> rules, confirmed October 2026. https://kefu.weibo.com/faqdetail?id=21369

So the agent that verified your Weibo account has to take part in any move.

Our guide on whether you need a Chinese entity to run social media in China
covers registration in more depth.

## Who owns your ad accounts and the money left in them

As with social accounts, what matters is whose company opened it. Tencent
leaves that choice to you.

> A client may run its own Tencent ad account, or entrust it to a third
> party such as a service provider or agency.
> Source: Tencent Marketing (腾讯营销), service provider cooperation agreement,
> confirmed October 2026.
> https://ad.qq.com/customer_service_cgi/v1/agreement/show?agreement_type=5

If the account is in your name, take admin access and keep it. If it is in
the agency’s name, the agency is the account holder. Moving it to your new
agency may not be possible: neither Tencent nor Ocean Engine (巨量引擎)
publishes a general rule. Get the platform’s answer in writing before you
plan around it.

Refunds come with conditions. On Tencent, every ad must be offline first.

> Before applying for a refund, all ads must be offline. The money returns by
> the original payment route.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), advertiser refund guide,
> confirmed October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/170214jyaU3y170214eEFJRb.html

That second line matters. If the agency topped up the account, the cash
goes back to the agency. Agree in writing how it reaches you.

Ocean Engine, the ad platform behind Douyin (抖音), goes further. A refund
ends the account.

> Applying for a balance refund closes the advertiser account and freezes the
> cash balance. Grant balance is cleared once the refund is approved.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), refund help page, confirmed October 2026.
> https://www.oceanengine.com/help/622

Then there’s the bonus credit.

| Balance type | Tencent Ads | Ocean Engine |
|---|---|---|
| Cash you topped up | Refundable, original route | Refundable, account closes |
| Rebate or bonus credit | Not refundable | Cleared on refund |
| Credit line | Not refundable | Not refundable |

> Tencent’s advertiser agreement says the non-cash part of an account,
> including rebates and credit, is not refundable.
> Source: Tencent Ads (腾讯广告), advertiser cooperation agreement, confirmed
> October 2026. https://e.qq.com/contract.html

So spend the bonus before you switch, or accept losing it. Ask for the
statement early, because a balance that looks large can be mostly bonus.

## Your content, source files and Chinese copy

Here the contract matters more than any platform rule. Chinese copyright law
gives commissioned work to the maker unless the contract says otherwise.

> For a commissioned work, the contract decides who owns the copyright. If
> the contract is silent or there is no contract, copyright belongs to the
> party that made the work.
> Source: National Copyright Administration (国家版权局), Copyright Law of the
> People’s Republic of China, Article 19, as amended November 2020.
> https://www.ncac.gov.cn/xxfb/flfg/flfg_532/202103/t20210309_50530.html

Published posts are not the issue. Reuse is. Without an assignment, the
designs and videos you paid for may not be yours to edit and run again. Nor
may the Chinese copy.

Ask for a copyright assignment that covers everything made under the
contract. Next come the source files, from layered designs to raw footage.
And don’t skip the working documents. They hold the Chinese keyword lists and
the approved product claims, plus the glossary of how your brand terms are
translated.
Without them, a new agency starts from zero.

If the agency won’t assign copyright, ask at least for a written license to
edit and reuse the work, with no end date.

Creator content is a separate case.

> On Xingtu (星图), copyright in a video a creator makes for an order stays
> with the creator. Ad-use authorization is free by default and the creator’s
> authorization is long-term.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), Xingtu help center, pages updated May and
> June 2026. https://www.xingtu.cn/help-center/demander/109120 and
> https://www.xingtu.cn/help-center/demander/139122

So for creator content on Xingtu, Douyin’s creator marketplace, what you
hold is a usage right, not the copyright. List each right with its scope and
end date.

## Your KOC and KOL relationships

KOLs are the big-name creators. KOCs are everyday users with small, loyal
followings. An agency’s contacts with both are part of what it sells, so
don’t expect them. What you can keep is the history of work done in your name.

On Xiaohongshu (小红书), that history sits in Pugongying (蒲公英), the
platform’s creator marketplace.

> An agency can start a collaboration in a brand’s name only with the brand’s
> clear consent. The brand can stop or change the authorization at any time,
> and stopping takes effect at once.
> Source: Xiaohongshu Pugongying help center (小红书蒲公英帮助中心), agency
> brand authorization manual, August 2026. https://pgy.xiaohongshu.com/help/home

Stopping it blocks new orders only. Orders already placed carry on, so
agree who sees each live order through to publication before you end it.

Before you switch, build a creator list: each creator, platform, order date,
fee, content link and the usage rights you hold. With it, the next agency can
rebook the people who worked.

<!-- SECTION: Followers -->

## The follower list problem: an export cannot move followers

Can you export a follower list and use it to move followers to another
account? No. The export contains account-specific IDs.

> The follower list is a list of OpenIDs, encrypted IDs that are unique to
> each user for each Official Account.
> Source: WeChat Open Docs (微信开放文档), follower list API, confirmed October
> 2026. https://developers.weixin.qq.com/doc/service/api/usermanage/userinfo/api_getfans.html

On WeChat (微信), an OpenID means nothing outside its own account. Your
followers move only when the account stays with you, or through Tencent’s
migration process.

Private contacts are a separate problem: the customers your team chats with
one to one. If they sit in a WeCom (企业微信) organization your company owns, you
can reassign them when staff change.

> A company can move an active employee’s customers and customer groups to a
> colleague. The customer and the new member become contacts automatically
> after 24 hours.
> Source: WeCom help center (企业微信帮助中心), active employee reassignment
> guide, September 2026. https://open.work.weixin.qq.com/help2/pc/14704

If the agency ran customer chats from its own WeCom organization, or from
staff personal WeChat accounts, those relationships remain under the agency’s control. You cannot assume
they will move with your contract.

## The 30-day handover checklist

If you read only one part of this page, make it this table. Run it from the
day you give notice. It covers WeChat (微信), the ad platforms and
Pugongying (蒲公英).

| Days | Task | Who | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | Send written notice listing everything to return | You | Agency confirms receipt |
| 1 to 3 | Change the WeChat admin to your own staff | Both | Your person scans in |
| 1 to 5 | Remove agency operators from every account | You | No agency staff left |
| 3 to 7 | Get balance statements: cash, bonus, credit | Agency | Statement matches the dashboard |
| 3 to 10 | End Pugongying and other creator authorizations | You | Authorization shows ended |
| 5 to 10 | Collect source files and working documents | Agency | Files open on your drive |
| 5 to 14 | Sign the copyright assignment | Both | Signed copy on file |
| 7 to 21 | Begin WeChat migration if the agency verified | Both | Both admins confirmed |
| 10 to 21 | Spend bonus credit or request cash refunds | You | Refund approved or credit used |
| 14 to 30 | Brief the incoming agency with the full register | You | New agency confirms access |
| 30 | Recheck every login, admin and operator | You | Nothing left in agency hands |

## Questions to ask the incoming agency

Ask these before you sign.

- Whose company will verify any new account? It should be yours.
- Will ad accounts open in your name, with you as admin?
- Who pays the platform when the account is topped up? If you pay it
  directly, a refund comes back to you.
- Who will be the admin on the Official Account, and who are the operators?
- Does the contract assign you the copyright in everything it makes?
- The exit clause. Read what it says about returning files and access.
- Where will customer contacts sit: your WeCom (企业微信), or theirs?

Get the fee and the exit terms in the same document. Our guide to China
marketing agency pricing compares the fee models, and our pricing page
publishes our own rate card in full.

<!-- SECTION: Clean handover -->

## What a clean handover looks like

In a clean handover, nothing is left to chase. Your staff hold the admin role
everywhere, and every account was verified on your license. Each ad account
lists your company, and you know which part of the balance is cash. Your drive
holds the source files, a signed copyright assignment and a creator list with
rights and end dates.

The new agency gets its access from you, not from the old one.

## FAQ

### Who owns my WeChat Official Account, me or my agency?

Tencent owns every WeChat (微信) Official Account. The right to use it
belongs to the company that passed verification. If that was yours, change
the admin and keep it. If it was the agency’s, direct transfer is banned. Migration to your own account may be possible
under Tencent’s rules, with the agency’s cooperation and a notarized letter.

### Can I transfer a Chinese ad account to a new agency?

No general transfer rule was found in Tencent’s or Ocean Engine’s published
guidance. If your company holds the account, changing agency access may be
enough. If the agency holds it, get the platform’s ruling first. Check cash
refund conditions separately, and remember that bonus credit is not cash.
Do not close the account before that review.

### How long does an agency handover take in China?

Plan on about 30 days. The admin change requires the old admin’s scan, plus the new admin’s
identity and phone verification.
Tencent puts an account migration at 7 to 10 days, notary stage included. Each
Ocean Engine (巨量引擎) refund review stage takes a working day or two. What drags
is collecting files and signatures, and that depends on the old agency.
