# How to sell on WeChat: a guide for brands

*WeChat is not one app. It is five sales tools stacked inside one login.
Here is how each one works, and how to make them work together.*

TheRedScroll | 8 min read | July 21, 2026

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

Most brands still treat WeChat as a place to post, and that is where they lose.
WeChat is where the buying actually happens in China, from the first tap to the
repeat order six months later.

In the third quarter of 2024, transactions running through WeChat Mini Programs
passed a level most national economies never see.

> WeChat Mini Program transaction volume passed 2 trillion yuan in a single
> quarter (Q3 2024), up by double digits year over year.
> Source: 腾讯新闻 / Tencent News

That is not a niche channel. It is a shopping mall the size of a country, and
the audience inside it is enormous.

> WeChat Mini Programs reached 949 million users as of October 2024, opened
> close to 70 times a month on average.
> Source: QuestMobile / 贵士移动

This guide covers the five parts of WeChat that move product, with
current 2024 figures throughout. Read them in order. Each one feeds the next.

<!-- SECTION: AT A GLANCE -->

## The five moves, at a glance

| Tool | What it does | What it is best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official Account | Your content hub and follower base | Publishing, service, staying in front of people |
| Video Accounts | Short video inside WeChat | Reaching people who don't follow you yet |
| Mini Programs | Your store inside the app | Selling, loyalty, checkout |
| Ads and KOLs | Paid reach and borrowed trust | Getting found fast |
| WeCom | One to one at scale | Keeping customers and driving repeat sales |

None of these works alone. A great store with no traffic just sits there empty,
and all the reach in the world is wasted if there is no store to send it to. What
makes WeChat work is the way these pieces feed each other.

<!-- SECTION: OFFICIAL ACCOUNTS -->

## 1. Build a base with Official Accounts

Your Official Account is your home on WeChat. It holds your followers, your
articles, and the menu that sends people to your store. Everything points back
to it. There are two types, and the choice is made for you.

| Account type | Who it is for | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service Account | Foreign companies (this is you) | 4 pushes a month, WeChat Pay, menus, APIs |
| Subscription Account | Local publishers and creators | Daily posts, but no push alerts |

Nearly every international brand runs a Service Account. Four pushes a month is
all you get, so each one has to earn its place. In late 2024 Tencent tucked
Service Account posts into their own folder, which quietly cut how many people
see them by default. That raised the bar, and thin content tends to sink out of
sight now.

**Write things people actually want.** Chinese readers treat Official Account
articles like a cross between a blog and a newsletter. How-to guides, honest
product breakdowns, behind the scenes looks, these hold attention. A straight
sales pitch does not. If your article doesn't help the reader, they won't finish
it, let alone share it.

The menu bar on your profile is prime real estate, so use it. Point it at your
store, your best article, and a simple FAQ. Then set a welcome reply with a
first-order coupon, and add keyword replies so a follower who types "sizing"
gets an instant answer. Handled well, it runs like a service desk that staffs
itself.

**Turn followers into members.** Offer a digital membership card with points and
perks. Capture city and preference data against the WeChat ID so you can send
the right offer to the right person later. This is your owned audience, and it
costs nothing to build.

<!-- SECTION: VIDEO ACCOUNTS -->

## 2. Reach new people with Video Accounts

Short video is where discovery happens now, and WeChat has its own version built
in: Video Accounts (视频号). What makes it different is that it sits on top of
WeChat's social graph. When someone likes a clip, their friends can see it. That
is reach beyond your own followers, spreading through the relationships people
already have.

The audience skews a particular way, and it matters for who should lean in.

> On WeChat Video Accounts, women make up about 63% of viewers, and users aged
> 25 and over account for 97.7%.
> Source: 知乎 / Zhihu

So this is an older, calmer audience. They come for useful, grounded content:
explainers, product demos, short interviews, quiet human stories. Scale is hard
to pin down, because Tencent keeps the official number to itself.

> Third-party industry estimates put WeChat Video Accounts near 600 million daily
> active users. Tencent does not release an official figure.
> Source: 中国短视频行业报告 / China Short Video Industry Report (third-party estimate)

Whatever the exact figure, the base keeps expanding, and fast.

> The number of Video Accounts with over one million followers roughly doubled
> across 2024.
> Source: 新榜 / Newrank

A few things that work. Hook people in the first two seconds, because drop-off
is instant. Add Chinese captions, since plenty of people watch on mute. Post two
or three times a week so the algorithm keeps testing you. And link every video
back to your Official Account or store, so a view becomes a follow, and a follow
becomes a sale.

<!-- SECTION: MINI PROGRAMS -->

## 3. Sell with Mini Programs

Think of the Mini Program as your cash register. It is a full store that lives
inside WeChat. There is no download and no app store, and you never push people
out to an outside website where half of them vanish on the way. They browse, add
to cart, and pay with WeChat Pay, all without leaving the app.

We saw the scale of what moves through them. The user base is just as big.

> Life-service, mobile-shopping, and finance Mini Programs each drew more than
> 860 million monthly active users in 2024.
> Source: 数英 / Digitaling

Most brands underestimate what comes next. Mini Programs are not just for
selling once. They are the best retention tool on the platform. Sync your membership
here, add points and tiers, give people a reason to come back. The luxury houses
figured this out early, and the growth they pulled from it was steep.

> In 2024, monthly active users on the Hermès and Cartier WeChat Mini Programs
> grew 704% and 2,551% year over year.
> Source: 数英 / Digitaling

**Keep it fast and simple.** A slow store loses people before they buy, and in
our work the drop-off shows up fastest right here, at load time. Strip the
checkout down. Add group-buying or friend-referral discounts, which travel
naturally through WeChat's social side. **Put entry points everywhere:** a "Shop"
button in your menu, a card inside each article, a QR code on packaging and at
the register. Scanning a code to open a store is second nature here.

For B2B, the same tool does a different job. Build a product catalog, a quote
calculator, or a booking form that drops a lead straight to your sales team. The
friction stays low, which is the whole point.

<!-- SECTION: ADS AND KOLS -->

## 4. Buy reach with ads and creators

That covers the parts you build and own. The last two are about speed and
staying power. Organic growth works, but it takes time. Paid reach and the right creators buy
you speed, which matters most at launch, when nobody knows your name yet.

WeChat's ad system runs across Moments, Official Accounts, Video Accounts, Mini
Programs, and Search, and each placement does a different job at a different
price. Budgets start small on bidding buys and climb fast for premium placements.
A Moments scheduled buy, for instance, starts at 50,000 yuan, about 7,000
dollars, for a single campaign.

> A WeChat Moments scheduled ad buy starts at roughly 50,000 yuan (around
> 7,000 dollars) per campaign.
> Source: 九枝兰 / Jiuzhilan

We covered the full breakdown, format by format, with the actual numbers, in our
guide
to WeChat advertising formats and costs. Start there before you spend.

On the creator side, WeChat runs on trust more than flash. Key Opinion Leaders
(KOLs), the big names, carry reach. A respected Official Account writing an
honest piece about your product pulls weight, because the reader chose to be
there. Smaller voices matter too. Key Opinion Consumers (KOCs), everyday
customers with real followings, spread recommendations inside groups and Moments
where a hard ad would never land. One rule holds across all
of it: check real engagement before you pay, because follower counts get
inflated and a big number can hide a dead audience.

<!-- SECTION: WECOM -->

## 5. Keep customers with WeCom and private domain

Winning a customer is the expensive part. The margin is in keeping them. And in
China's open web, that first sale keeps getting more expensive.

> The cost of acquiring a new customer through public channels has climbed from
> tens of yuan to several hundred yuan since 2018.
> Source: 甲子光年 / Jiazi Guangnian

That is the whole case for private domain, your own owned audience of followers
and members you can reach again and again at almost no cost. The tool for it is
WeCom (企业微信), Tencent's business version of WeChat. A customer adds your
advisor like they would a friend, and now you have a direct line.

The playbook is not complicated. Bring new followers over with an incentive, tag
them by interest and what they have bought, then use that line for order updates,
after-sales help, and the occasional well-timed offer that is not spam. Do it
right and a one-time buyer becomes a regular. Most of China's biggest companies already run
this way.

> More than 80% of Fortune 500 companies operating in China have adopted WeCom.
> Source: 群应用 / WeScrm

Set it up once and it becomes the cheapest sales channel you own, working
quietly in the background long after the ad budget is spent. We go deep on how
private domain traffic works, and how to build it, in our full
explainer on WeCom. If retention is your priority, read that next.

## Where brands go wrong

The most common mistake is running WeChat like a posting calendar rather than a
sales system. The brands that pull ahead connect the pieces. Reach on Video Accounts
feeds followers on the Official Account. The Official Account feeds the Mini
Program store. The store feeds WeCom, and WeCom brings people back to buy again.

The second mistake is skipping real localization, which is not the same thing as
translation. It means tone, timing, the festival calendar, the visual language
people expect. Content built for another market and dropped into WeChat reads as
foreign, and it underperforms.

<!-- SECTION: FAQ -->

## Common questions

A few things brands ask us before they start.

**Do I need a Chinese company to run WeChat?**
For a verified Service Account and WeChat Pay, you need a Chinese business entity
or an authorized local representative. Sort this out first. Nothing else runs
without it.

**Can a smaller brand win without a big ad budget?**
Yes. Plenty do it on private domain and community instead of heavy spend.
Consistent Official Account content, QR-driven sign-ups, and a tight WeCom setup
grow steadily on a modest budget.

**How do people discover new brands on WeChat?**
Mostly through friends, creator mentions, and QR codes in the real world, on
packaging, at events, in stores. WeChat Search matters too when your content
carries the right Chinese keywords.

**How fast do customers expect a reply?**
Fast. WeChat is a chat app first, so people expect a conversation, not a support
ticket. Answer within a day, and use auto-replies for the common questions.

**Which part should I build first?**
The Official Account and a Mini Program store, a place to publish and a place to
sell. Add Video Accounts for reach and WeCom for retention once the base runs.
