# TheRedScroll — Brand & Content Style Guide

> Version 1.1 — April 2026
> This document governs all written, visual, and tonal decisions across TheRedScroll's website, LinkedIn, marketing materials, sales decks, and client-facing communications. Every person who writes for TheRedScroll follows this guide. No exceptions.

---

## 1. Brand Identity

### 1.1 Brand Name
- **Full name:** TheRedScroll
- **Written as:** One word, PascalCase. Always.
- **Never write it as:** "The Red Scroll," "the red scroll," "theredscroll," "TRS," "Red Scroll," or any other variation
- **In a sentence:** "TheRedScroll is a China-based social media agency." Not "The Red Scroll is..."
- **No abbreviations.** Not internally. Not externally. Not ever.

### 1.2 Tagline

**Primary tagline:**
"We don't talk about China's platforms. We post on them."

**Supporting lines (pick one per context):**

| Supporting Line | Best Used On |
|---|---|
| Fixed scope. Fixed price. Team on the ground. | Homepage hero, sales deck cover and closing slide |
| WeChat. Douyin. RedNote. Weibo. And 14 more. | Service overview page, LinkedIn banner |
| More followers. More engagement. More conversions. | Landing pages, ad copy |
| From Shanghai and Hong Kong. Every day. | About page, sales deck intro slide |

**Rules:**
- Never use the primary tagline without a supporting line on the homepage or in a deck. On LinkedIn and email signatures, it can stand alone.
- Never combine the primary tagline and a supporting line into a single sentence. They are two separate lines.

International brands located outside of China that want to grow their social media presence inside China.

### 2.2 Who We're Talking To
- **The Marketing Director** — Has budget authority. Needs to justify spend. Cares about ROI, clear deliverables, and predictable costs.
- **The Founder or CEO** — Knows China is important but doesn't know where to start. Values straight talk over polished pitches.

## 3. Voice & Tone

### 3.1 Brand Voice (Constant)

| Attribute | What It Means in Practice |
|---|---|
| **Expert** | We state facts. We cite data. We don't say "we believe" when we can say "we know." |
| **Direct** | Short sentences. Active voice. Subject, verb, object. No filler. |
| **Practical** | Every claim has a deliverable, metric, or action behind it. |
| **Grounded** | We name real platforms. We cite real numbers. Abstract language is the enemy. |
| **Confident, not arrogant** | We state what we do well. We never trash competitors by name. We let the data speak. |
| **Human** | We write like people talk. We use contractions. We're warm, but we don't waste time. |

### 3.2 Tone Adjustments by Channel

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|
| **Website hero / services** | Confident and benefit-driven. Lead with the problem, follow with what we do. |
| **LinkedIn posts** | Sharp, concise, human. Lead with the point. |
| **Sales deck** | Data-backed confidence. Visuals over paragraphs. |
| **Blog / educational** | Helpful teacher. Explain complexity without condescension. |
| **Case studies** | Results first. Let the numbers carry the weight. |
| **Email / proposals** | Professional but warm. Specific. No fluff. |
| **CTA / contact** | Low-friction. Invite conversation. Don't push. |

### 3.3 The Expertise Test
Before publishing any content, ask: "Does this prove we know China's platforms, or does it just say we do?"

### 3.3.1 Allowed Domain Terms

Some words read as agency-jargon in most contexts but carry technical meaning in ours. They are allowed only when used precisely.

| Term | Allowed Use | Banned Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ecosystem | A specific platform's interconnected products (e.g. "the WeChat ecosystem of Service Accounts, Mini Programs, WeCom, Pay"). The China social commerce stack, where audience, content, influencers, platforms, and commerce sit on the same surface. | "Digital ecosystem," "ecosystem of partners," "our ecosystem of solutions." If the sentence works after removing the word, remove it. |

### 3.4 Words and Phrases That Are Banned

| Banned | Use Instead |
|---|---|
| Partners | Clients |
| Solutions | Services |
| Manage / handle / leverage | Grow |
| Brand awareness / visibility | More followers, more engagement, more conversions |
| Holistic | [Be specific about what's included] |
| Synergy / synergies | [Delete the sentence] |
| Digital landscape | [Delete the sentence and start over] |
| Unlock / unleash | Grow, build, launch |
| Cutting-edge | [Name the specific technology] |
| Best-in-class | [Cite a number instead] |
| Seamless | [Describe the actual process] |
| End-to-end | [List what's included] |
| Turnkey | [Describe what the client gets] |
| Em dash | Period, comma, or rewrite the sentence |
| Exclamation marks (!) | Period |

## 4. Writing Rules

### 4.1 Language
American English. Always. "Optimize" not "optimise." "Color" not "colour."

### 4.2 Style
Journalist style. Every sentence earns its place.

### 4.3 Reading Level
Grade 8. Short sentences, common words, clear structure.

### 4.4 Sentences
- Aim for 12 to 20 words. Hard max: 25 words.
- If a sentence needs a semicolon, split it into two sentences.

### 4.5 Paragraphs

| Channel | Max Paragraph Length |
|---|---|
| Website | 2 to 3 lines |
| LinkedIn | 1 to 2 lines |
| Blog | 3 to 4 sentences |
| Sales deck | 1 to 2 sentences per text block |
| Email | 2 to 3 sentences |

### 4.6 Numbers and Data
- Spell out one through nine. Use numerals for 10+.
- Always use numerals for metrics: "3 months," "37 competitors."
- Percentages: "60%" not "sixty percent."
- Currency: USD by default. "$5,000/month."

### 4.7 Capitalization
- **Headings:** Sentence case. Exception: the tagline uses title case.
- **Platform names:** Always capitalize. WeChat, Douyin, RedNote, Weibo, Bilibili, Kuaishou, Zhihu.
- **KOL / KOC / AIGC:** Always uppercase.
- **TheRedScroll:** PascalCase. Always.

### 4.8 Contractions
Use them. "We're," "you'll," "don't," "won't," "it's."

### 4.9 Oxford Comma
Always. "WeChat, Douyin, and RedNote."

### 4.10 CTAs

| Use | Don't Use |
|---|---|
| Book a call | Schedule a complimentary consultation |
| Get your proposal | Request a bespoke strategy session |
| See our services | Explore our comprehensive service offerings |
| Talk to us | Reach out to our dedicated team |

## 8. SEO Guidelines

### 8.1 Keyword Rules
- Primary keyword appears in: page title, H1, meta description, first 100 words, one H2.
- Never stuff. Use natural variations.
- Every service page targets one primary keyword.
- Blog posts target one long-tail keyword per post.

### 8.2 Meta Descriptions
- 140 to 155 characters.
- Include primary or secondary keyword.
- End with a benefit or CTA.
- Pattern: [What we do] + [for whom] + [key differentiator].

### 8.3 URL Structure
- Lowercase, hyphenated, English.
- Service pages: `/wechat-marketing/`, `/douyin-marketing/`, `/rednote-marketing/`
- Blog posts: `/blog/how-to-set-up-wechat-official-account/`
- No dates in blog URLs unless time-sensitive.

### 8.4 Page Requirements
- One clear H1 related to the page's target keyword.
- Meta title (60 characters max) and meta description (155 characters max).
- At least one CTA.
- Internal links to at least one other service page or blog post.

## 9. Editorial Integrity Rules

### 9.1 Claims and Proof
Every claim needs proof. No exceptions.

### 9.2 The Expertise Rule
Every piece of external content must include at least one platform-specific insight that the reader didn't already know. Non-negotiable.

### 9.3 Accuracy
- Double-check every number before publishing.
- When citing platform user numbers, note the date.

### 9.4 Honesty
- Never promise what we can't deliver.
- Never imply guaranteed results.
- Transparency is a core value. It applies to our content too.
