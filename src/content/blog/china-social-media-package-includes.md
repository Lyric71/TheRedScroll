## Month one: what setup actually involves

Most of the first month is setup. We study your competitors on each platform. We
write your content pillars, the themes every post keeps coming back to. Then we
build the first editorial calendar.

If your accounts don't exist yet, we open and register them during onboarding.
They go in your company's name, with your documents.

> TheRedScroll registers client WeChat (微信) accounts under the client's company
> name. If the work ends, the client keeps the account, the followers, and the
> content archive.
> Source: TheRedScroll WeChat agency page, September 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/wechat-agency/

Our published target is first content live within two weeks of signing. Platform
review can push that out, and we don't control the queue.

## Content: how many pieces, in what format, made by whom

One piece a week per platform sounds thin until you see the specs.

| Platform             | Volume           | Format and length                              | Produced how                         |
|----------------------|------------------|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| WeChat (微信)        | 1 article a week | Up to 1,500 Chinese characters, 3 images       | Written in Chinese                   |
| Weibo (微博)         | 1 post a week    | Up to 300 Chinese characters, 1 image          | Written in Chinese                   |
| Xiaohongshu (小红书) | 1 post a week    | Up to 500 Chinese characters, 6-image carousel | Written in Chinese                   |
| Douyin (抖音)        | 1 video a week   | Up to 60 seconds                               | Your footage, our edit and subtitles |

Nothing is translated from English. Your brand guidelines are the brief, and the
copy is written in Chinese from the first line.

The WeChat cadence has a reason behind it.

> An overseas company can register only a WeChat service account. A service
> account can send followers a push notification four times per calendar month;
> articles published without one still appear on the account's profile page.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), confirmed September 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/180312zQjeYV180312FNNbmA.html https://kf.qq.com/faq/120911VrYVrA131025QniAfu.html

One article a week is about what that ceiling allows. Extra articles would go
out quietly, with no alert to followers.

Douyin is the line to check against your own team. The entry package edits
footage you send; it doesn't film. Don't assume the agency brings a camera crew,
because in this package it doesn't. With no footage, you need one of three
things. AI video comes with the larger packages. A shoot is quoted separately.
Or someone on your side films with a phone and a plan. Settle which one before
you sign.

## Community work: replies, escalation, and what we don't publish

We publish less on this part of the job than you might want. This is what's
written down.

We reply to comments and answer direct messages on the accounts we run. Before
launch, you and we agree on a response playbook. It sets the tone and the stock
answers, plus a list of what gets escalated to you.

> Community work includes comment moderation, a response playbook agreed with
> the client upfront, and same-day escalation when something could spread. In a
> real crisis, a response plan reaches the client within hours.
> Source: TheRedScroll Weibo agency page, September 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/weibo-agency/

What we don't publish is an hours figure or a reply-time target. We'd rather
leave that gap than print a number we haven't committed to in public.

On Xiaohongshu (小红书), direct messages are a rules question as much as a speed
question.

> Since January 7, 2025, Xiaohongshu has barred professional accounts from
> putting a WeChat ID or phone number in automatic replies and welcome messages.
> Only the platform's social business card may be used.
> Source: Jiemian (界面新闻) via Sina Tech (新浪科技), January 2025. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-01-12/doc-ineerpnr4074384.shtml

A service provider told the same reporter that accounts which kept a WeChat ID
in their auto-replies would be muted. So the playbook, which spells out what may
go in a reply, counts for as much as speed.

Monitoring what people say about you elsewhere on the platforms is a different
job. That's social listening, and it isn't in the entry package.

## When you should buy the bigger package instead

The larger packages change four specific things. If none of them describes you,
stay where you are.

| If this is true                                             | The larger packages add                                             |
|-------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Weekly is too slow for Xiaohongshu (小红书) or Weibo (微博) | 3 posts a week, then 5 on Xiaohongshu and 7 on Weibo in the largest |
| You have no Douyin (抖音) footage                           | AI video, up to 15 seconds, brief approved before production        |
| You need to hear what's said about you                      | Brand mention monitoring, competitor tracking, a sentiment summary  |
| Once a month isn't enough contact                           | Weekly strategy calls, or anytime in the largest package            |

The entry package suits a brand that is testing the market and has footage on
hand. Comment volume should still be low.

One row fitting is a reason to look at the next package up. Two or more usually
point to the largest.

> Under Tencent's service provider agreement, a client brought in by an agency,
> called a sub-client (子客), may run its own ad account or hand it to the agency.

## FAQ

### How many posts a month should a China package include?

Our entry package runs one piece a week on each of four platforms, so about four
a month per platform. On WeChat (微信), that sits at the four monthly pushes a
service account allows. If you need more on Xiaohongshu (小红书) or Weibo (微博), our
larger packages raise those two to between three and seven posts a week.

### Is ad spend included in an agency retainer?

It shouldn't be. Ad spend is money you pay the platform for reach. The agency
fee pays for the work. When the two are blended, a markup is easy to hide. With
us, ad spend is billed separately and paid directly to the platform, with no
markup. [Running the ads is its own service](/services/advertising/), priced on our pricing page.

### What is usually excluded from a China social media package?

Ad spend, influencer fees, platform verification fees, extra platforms, shoots,
and social listening are the usual exclusions. Verification alone runs from 300
yuan to 600 yuan per platform for a mainland company. Ask any agency which of
these its quote covers, and get the answer in the contract, not the pitch.
