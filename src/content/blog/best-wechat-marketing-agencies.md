This isn't a ranked list. TheRedScroll sells WeChat work, so ranking our rivals
would mean grading our own exam. We compare agency types instead, and we score
them on checks you can run yourself.

WeChat (微信) is a technical platform. An Official Account is the easy part (it's
mostly content and menus). The hard parts are the Mini Program, the WeCom (企业微信)
accounts your staff use to talk to customers, and the data that ties them
together. A logo wall tells you none of that.

Here's how the common types compare.

| Agency type              | Strongest at                       | Pricing to ask for                  | Proof to ask for                 | Best for                       |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|----------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Mini Program builder     | Custom builds tied to WeChat Pay   | Build fee apart from monthly upkeep | A live Mini Program you can open | Stores, bookings, loyalty      |
| WeCom and CRM specialist | Staff accounts, tags and follow-up | Tool licenses apart from service    | Contacts that stay in your WeCom | High-ticket and B2B sales      |
| Network agency           | Volume across markets and teams    | Fee per article, video and report   | Named editors in China           | Enterprise content programs    |
| Official Account studio  | Articles, menus and Channels       | A fixed task list each month        | Accounts it runs today           | A first presence               |
| Overseas-based agency    | English contact, head-office links | Who in China does the work          | Names of the China team          | Global teams wanting one voice |

## How we judged the best WeChat agencies

We used five checks. Three are technical. We asked whether the agency builds
Mini Programs and whether it sets up and runs WeCom (企业微信). We also asked
whether your customer data stays with you. The other two are about trust. Does
it publish a price? Does it name clients you could call?

On October 22, 2026, we read the WeChat (微信) service pages of nine agencies that
sell to foreign brands in English. We left ourselves out.

> Of nine agencies selling WeChat work to foreign brands, six said they build
> Mini Programs and five offered WeCom setup or operation. Three offered both.
> Source: TheRedScroll selected-page check, October 2026. Checked URLs are kept in our editorial source ledger.

The trust checks turned up less.

> On the same pages, none showed a monthly fee figure for ongoing WeChat work.

> brand. No page showed both a price figure and a named client. Eight of the
> nine gave a mainland city for an office or team.
> Source: TheRedScroll selected-page check, October 2026. Checked URLs are kept in our editorial source ledger.

One more page showed results by industry with no brand names. Another listed

These are page-level results from one day. They don't show what each firm

### What "access" means on WeChat

Xiaohongshu (小红书) publishes a dated list of its creator agencies, as our guide
to Xiaohongshu agencies explains. We found no WeChat list like it to check a
name against. What WeChat does publish is a set of rules for how an agency gets
into your accounts. They're worth ten minutes before any proposal.

> A brand can authorize its Official Account, Mini Program, WeChat Store and
> other accounts to up to 15 service providers at once. A provider cannot end
> the authorization on its own. Only the brand can.
> Source: WeChat Open Docs (微信开放文档), June 2026. https://developers.weixin.qq.com/doc/oplatform/Third-party_Platforms/2.0/getting_started/how_to_service.html

Some permissions can only go to one provider at a time.

> A mutually exclusive permission set can be authorized to only one third-party
> platform at a time.
> Source: WeChat Open Docs, June 2026. https://developers.weixin.qq.com/doc/oplatform/Third-party_Platforms/2.0/product/third_party_authority_instructions.html

Already working with an agency? You can see who holds access today. If you are
the admin, open the Official Account Platform security assistant (公众平台安全助手)
inside WeChat. It lists the third parties each of your accounts has authorized.

> Only the brand can end a provider's authorization. It does so in the Mini
> Program or Official Account admin, or in the security assistant inside WeChat.
> Source: WeChat Open Docs, October 2026. https://developers.weixin.qq.com/doc/oplatform/Third-party_Platforms/2.0/operation/authorization/authorization_management.html

The account itself matters more than any login. WeChat ties an Official Account
to the company that passed its verification.

> The right to use a WeChat Official Account belongs to the entity that passed
> the qualification review. Giving, lending, renting, transferring or selling it
> is banned.
> Source: Tencent (腾讯), WeChat Official Account Platform service agreement, October 2026. https://mp.weixin.qq.com/cgi-bin/readtemplate?lang=zh_CN&t=home%2Fagreement_tmpl&token=&type=info

So the account should be verified in your company's name. Not the agency's. Ads
work on the same principle.

> A Tencent ads client may run its own ad account or entrust it to a third
> party, such as a service provider.
> Source: Tencent Marketing (腾讯营销), service provider agreement, October 2026. https://ad.qq.com/customer_service_cgi/v1/agreement/show?agreement_type=5

| Account            | How an agency gets in                        | Who controls it                          |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Official Account   | Your authorization to its platform           | You: only the brand can end it           |
| Mini Program code  | Development authorized to its platform       | The agency, while the authorization runs |
| WeCom tools        | A standard app or a custom app built for you | Your WeCom, under your verified entity   |
| Tencent ad account | You run it, or you entrust it                | The company it's registered to           |

Now to the jobs themselves. Each of the next four sections names the agency type
that fits one.

## Best for Mini Program and commerce builds

Pick a Mini Program builder with its own developers. A Mini Program (小程序) is an
app that runs inside WeChat. A store or a booking flow is only as reliable as
its code.

The first question is how the agency builds. WeChat (微信) lets providers build
from templates.

> A provider can push one Mini Program template to a very large number of
> merchants' Mini Programs and update them in bulk. Once a merchant authorizes
> development to a provider, it can no longer control versions or domain
> settings in its own admin.
> Source: WeChat Open Docs (微信开放文档), June 2026. https://developers.weixin.qq.com/doc/oplatform/Third-party_Platforms/2.0/product/how_to_dev.html

A template isn't bad (it's cheaper and faster). But you need to know whether
you're buying custom code or renting a shared one, and who holds the source when
you leave.

Already have a Mini Program that an agency built? If development is authorized
to the agency's platform, you can't publish new versions from your own admin.
Find out before you plan a change of agency.

Public contracts give a sense of build prices in China.

> Zhejiang Economic and Trade Polytechnic paid 199,800 yuan for a lecture Mini
> Program and related platform updates.
> Source: China Government Procurement Network (中国政府采购网), November 2025. http://www.ccgp.gov.cn/cggg/dfgg/zbgg/202511/t20251120_25730500.htm

> Guangdong's maritime safety administration paid 2.288 million yuan to build
> the Haishitong WeChat Mini Program.
> Source: China Government Procurement Network, May 2025. http://www.ccgp.gov.cn/cggg/zygg/zbgg/202505/t20250516_24611552.htm

Both are public-sector contracts, not private quotes. They show how wide the
range runs, not what your build will cost.

For a foreign company, the account has its own rules.

> An overseas entity pays 99 US dollars per Mini Program verification, charged
> whether it passes or fails. Review takes 7 to 15 working days.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/190712yYfY7v190712u2YjQ3.html

> Overseas Mini Programs currently need no filing and no annual review.
> Source: Tencent overseas Mini Program team, WeChat Open Community (微信开放社区), August 2025. https://developers.weixin.qq.com/community/minigame/doc/00044a834b47f83e94c35fbee6b809

## Best for WeCom and private domain CRM

This is a job for a WeCom and CRM specialist. WeCom (企业微信) is the work version
of WeChat. Your sales and service staff use it to chat with customers who use
ordinary WeChat. Brands call the result private domain traffic: a customer list
you can reach without paying for ads each time.

> WeCom connects more than 14 million companies and organizations, which serve
> more than 750 million WeChat users a day through it.
> Source: Xinhua (新华网), August 2025. http://www.news.cn/tech/20250820/fbe9a69fd7a742629cf8275238bb7b91/c.html

An agency can plug tools into WeCom in two ways.

> WeCom service providers offer standard third-party apps, the same for every
> company, or custom apps built on a company's behalf.
> Source: WeCom developer center (企业微信开发者中心), June 2024. https://developer.work.weixin.qq.com/document/path/90568

The first is quicker. The second fits workflows that a standard app can't
handle, and takes longer. Either way, there's a cost for every seat, and the
provider may pass it on in its quote. Ask to see that line.

> Since May 16, 2022, WeCom has charged service providers a license per account
> per year. With 1 to 5 accounts, a basic account costs 5 yuan and a
> customer-contact account 50 yuan. The price per account falls as numbers grow.
> Source: WeCom (企业微信), May 2022. https://open.work.weixin.qq.com/wwopen/common/readDocument/38829

Next comes a check that none of the nine pages raised. A foreign company that
verifies WeCom as an overseas company loses some features.

> A WeCom verified as an overseas company does not support some functions,
> including external payment collection, conversation archiving and the
> third-party app marketplace. Review usually takes 1 to 3 working days.
> Source: WeCom help center (企业微信帮助中心), June 2026. https://open.work.weixin.qq.com/help2/pc/16535

Ask any WeCom pitch which entity it plans to verify, and which tools still work
under it.

Two more rules belong in the plan.

> Each company gets 2,000 external contacts free. An unverified company can add
> only 100 customers.
> Source: WeCom help center, October 2026. https://open.work.weixin.qq.com/help2/pc/21326

> A company can move an active employee's customers and groups to a colleague.
> They become the colleague's contacts after 24 hours.
> Source: WeCom help center, September 2026. https://open.work.weixin.qq.com/help2/pc/14704

That last rule is the reason to run sales on WeCom rather than on a rep's
personal WeChat. Reps leave. With WeCom, their customers don't go with them.

## Best for enterprise-scale content operations

A network agency fits a brand that publishes a lot, in several markets, with
many approvers. The job is volume with control. WeChat (微信) articles, Channels
videos and creator posts go out every week, and each one needs sign-off on time.

Ask who writes in China and who approves. Then ask for one month of a real
editorial calendar, with names removed, and the turnaround they commit to for a
legal or head-office review.

This is also where an overseas-based agency can make sense. A global team often
wants one contact and reports in English. That works if the agency names its
writers in China and says which city they work in. Most of the pages we checked
did.

Creator work runs through WeChat's creator marketplace (互选), and its fees
changed this year.

> In WeChat's creator marketplace, one-to-one deals carry a 5% platform fee.
> From April 1, 2026, new recruitment tasks carry 10% on Channels and 20% on
> Official Accounts.
> Source: Tencent Marketing creator marketplace help center (腾讯营销互选平台帮助中心), September 2026. https://huxuan.qq.com/trade/free/help_detail?post_ids=13088

That fee goes to the platform. Keep it on its own line, apart from the agency's
fee and the creator's price.

## Best for a first WeChat presence on a small budget

An Official Account studio fits here. A small budget needs one account, a steady
publishing plan and a clear next step for readers.

A foreign company starts with a narrow choice on WeChat (微信).

> An overseas entity can register one Official Account, and only as a service
> account.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/180312zQjeYV180312FNNbmA.html

If you want a Mini Program (小程序) later, a verified service account can register
one without a second fee.

> A verified service account can quick-register a Mini Program without paying
> the 99-dollar overseas verification fee.
> Source: Tencent customer service, October 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/170705YVZFZZ170705eyI7Rr.html

Paid reach has floors.

> WeChat Moments ads bought by bidding need a daily budget of at least 1,000
> yuan, and bidding is open only outside core and key cities. Scheduled buys
> start at 50,000 yuan per placement.
> Source: Tencent Ads (腾讯广告), October 2026. https://e.qq.com/topic/marketing/industry/weixin/

On a small budget, ads can wait. The studio's plan for growing followers without
them is the part to read closely.

## The technical questions to ask before you sign

1. Which company will each account be verified under: yours or ours?
2. Will you get access through WeChat's authorization, which we can end, or
   through our passwords?
3. Is our Mini Program custom code or a shared template, and who holds the
   source code when we leave?
4. Which WeCom entity will you verify, and which tools still work under it?
5. Which WeCom tools will we pay for, per seat, and who holds the license?
6. Where does customer data live, and can we export it?
7. Who in China writes, who approves, and in which city do they work?

Expect the answers in writing. An agency you already pay can answer question two
today; check its answer against the security assistant.

## What a WeChat proposal should contain

A good proposal reads like a work order. It lists the articles, videos, Mini
Program features and WeCom setup you will get, and who approves each one.

Money comes in several kinds, and each needs its own line. List the agency's
fee, any build fee, WeCom tool licenses, creator and platform fees, and ad
spend.

Accounts come next. A proposal should name every one of them: the Official
Account, the Mini Program, WeCom and the ad account. It should say which company
each one is verified under. It should list every person with access, and your
own staff should hold the admin roles. That way nothing has to be handed back on
the day the contract ends (and nothing can be held back, either).

Define the numbers. A follower, a read, a Mini Program visit and a WeCom contact
should mean the same thing every month.

And the exit? What you keep, in what format, by which date. For a Mini Program,
that includes the code.

## Questions buyers ask

### What should a WeChat agency charge?

No platform, regulator or trade body publishes a market rate for WeChat agency
fees. Quotes move with scope: articles a month, video, Mini Program work, WeCom
setup, WeCom tool licenses and reporting. Two public Mini Program contracts we
checked ran from 199,800 yuan to 2.288 million yuan. TheRedScroll publishes its
full rate card on its pricing page.

### Does my WeChat agency need Mini Program developers?

Only if you need a Mini Program. A content-only plan runs fine on an Official
Account. If you need a store or a member club, the agency should have developers
or name the firm that does the build. Ask whether the code is custom or a shared
template, and who keeps it when the contract ends.

### How do I compare WeChat agency proposals?

Put them side by side on the same lines: fee, build costs, tool licenses,
platform fees and ad spend. Then check accounts: whose company is each one
verified under, and can you end the agency's access yourself? And ask each one
for a named client you can call about its WeChat work.
