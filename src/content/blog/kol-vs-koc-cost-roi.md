The KOL vs KOC cost question has a short answer on price and a longer one on
return. A KOL (key opinion leader) is a creator you pay for reach. A KOC (key
opinion consumer) is a small account you pay for trust, often in product.
We book both from Shanghai and Hong Kong. This is the gap, using the planning
ranges on [our influencer marketing service page](/services/influencer-marketing/).

| | KOC | KOL |
|---|---|---|
| Xiaohongshu followers | Under 5,000, or 5,000 to 20,000 | 20,000 to 100,000, or over 100,000 |
| Planning rate per post | 100 to 500 yuan, or product only; 500 to 3,000 yuan | 3,000 to 15,000 yuan; 15,000 to 80,000+ yuan |
| What you are buying | Search presence and real-user proof | Reach and a launch moment |
| How it is bought | In volume, often paid on results | One creator at a time, fixed price |
| Main risk | Same-looking notes nobody saves | One post that misses |

> TheRedScroll publishes these ranges as first-quarter 2026 benchmarks: "Not
> quotes." Video notes cost 20% to 30% more than photo notes.
> Source: TheRedScroll, influencer marketing service page, October 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/services/influencer-marketing/

They are planning figures, not measured averages. At those rates, a
40-note KOC round and one top-tier KOL plan at roughly the same money:
about 40,000 to 150,000 yuan each, before product. The worked budgets below
show the lines. Every outside source was checked twice in October 2026.

## KOL vs KOC cost per post, per view, and per engagement

Cost per post is the only unit with a published price. We searched Chinese
data firms, platforms, and brokers for a cost per thousand reached (CPM) or a
cost per engagement (CPE) by follower tier. None publishes one in text you can
check. The numbers that circulate sit in images, in agency decks, or in posts
with no named source, so we don't repeat them.

What is published compares platforms, not tiers.

> Xiaohongshu KOLs lead the major platforms on completion rate, engagement
> rate, and price per thousand followers. Bilibili and Douyin offer better
> value on CPE and CPM.
> Source: Weiboyi (微播易), 2026 social platform KOL report, on Digitaling (数英), May 2026. https://www.digitaling.com/articles/1509312.html

On Xiaohongshu (小红书), then, you pay more for each follower, and you get more
attention for it. Format moves the price too.

> On Xiaohongshu, image-note KOLs cost 35% less per thousand followers than
> video KOLs.
> Source: Weiboyi (微播易) with the China Advertising Association (中国广告协会), on TopMarketing, May 2025. https://www.itopmarketing.com/info20096

One reach price is public: paid boost. When a brand pushes a creator's note
to more people through Pugongying (蒲公英), Xiaohongshu's creator marketplace,
it pays per thousand impressions.

> Pugongying raised the price of boosting a cooperation note to 25 yuan per
> 1,000 impressions, up from 15. The change was finished on January 23, 2026.
> Source: Xiaohongshu Pugongying help center (小红书蒲公英帮助中心), September 2026. https://pgy.xiaohongshu.com/help/home

That gives you a yardstick. Divide a creator's fee by the impressions the
post earned on its own, times 1,000. If the answer is far above 25 yuan, the
creator cost you more than buying roughly the same eyes with boost.

| Unit | Published price | How to get your own number |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per post | Our planning ranges, by tier | The creator's quote, plus the platform fee |
| Cost per thousand reached | 25 yuan for a Pugongying boost | Fee divided by natural impressions, times 1,000 |
| Cost per engagement | No tier figure published in text | Fee divided by likes, saves, and comments |
| Cost per search lift | No public figure | Brand searches before and after, same weeks |

Compute CPE yourself, post by post, from the platform's own report. Then
compare creators of the same size and category.

## What KOC seeding returns, with a worked budget

KOC seeding means dozens of small accounts posting about one product in the
same weeks. [Our service page](/services/influencer-marketing/) plans a seeding campaign at 20 to 50 KOCs. Here
is one at 40, on Xiaohongshu (小红书). This is planning arithmetic from published
rates, not a client result.

| Line | Basis | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| 40 micro-KOC image notes | 500 to 3,000 yuan each, our ranges | 20,000 to 120,000 yuan |
| Pugongying platform fee | 10% of the creator fee | 2,000 to 12,000 yuan |
| Boost the 8 best notes | 100,000 impressions each, 25 yuan per 1,000 | 20,000 yuan |
| Product shipped to creators | Your cost of goods | Not priced here |
| Total before product | | 42,000 to 152,000 yuan |

> A creator quoting 10,000 yuan costs the brand 11,000: the fixed price plus a
> 10% platform service fee.
> Source: Xiaohongshu Pugongying help center (小红书蒲公英帮助中心), September 2026. https://pgy.xiaohongshu.com/help/home

What does it buy? Forty chances to rank when a buyer types your category
into the search bar. On this platform, search is where buyers look.

> 77% of Xiaohongshu's daily active users use search to solve a problem.
> Source: Huxiu (虎嗅), May 2026. https://www.huxiu.com/article/4861801.html

The platform itself says follower count is the wrong yardstick for small
creators.

> In one of Xiaohongshu's own cases, a creator with 5,000 followers can be
> worth more commercially than a general entertainment creator with a
> million, if those followers are precise users of the product.
> Source: Huxiu (虎嗅), quoting Xiaohongshu's creator commercialization lead, May 2026. https://www.huxiu.com/article/4861801.html

Brands are buying on that logic.

> In 2024, seeding orders on Xiaohongshu rose 95%, and the number of creators
> earning money rose 200%.
> Source: Sina Tech (新浪科技), via Ebrun (亿邦动力), reporting Kasi Data (卡思数据), March 2025. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-03-24/doc-ineqtsmu3611362.shtml

The return is slow and spread out, and no single note carries the campaign.
Some notes will rank for months. Some will vanish in a day. You won't
know which until two weeks have passed, so the boost line in the table goes
only to the eight notes that lift early. Measure the
whole set in saves, in comments that ask where to buy, and in brand searches
over the following weeks.

Likes alone tell you little.

## What one top-tier KOL returns, with a worked budget

Now spend roughly the same money on one creator with over 100,000 followers.
Again, this is planning arithmetic, not a result.

| Line | Basis | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| One top-tier video note | 15,000 to 80,000+ yuan, plus 20% to 30% for video | 18,000 to 104,000+ yuan |
| Pugongying platform fee | 10% of the creator fee | 1,800 to 10,400+ yuan |
| Boost the note | 800,000 impressions at 25 yuan per 1,000 | 20,000 yuan |
| Product for the creator | Your cost of goods | Not priced here |
| Total before product | | 39,800 to 134,400+ yuan |

Same budget, one note instead of forty. What you get back is a spike.
Xiaohongshu publishes no figure for its size, but Douyin (抖音) does, and it
shows how much rides on one post landing.

> Creator content on Xingtu accounts for 43% of brands' A3 audience (people
> who have engaged with a brand). A hit video adds 30 times more of that
> audience than a non-hit, at a 16 times lower cost per person.
> Source: Morketing, via Tencent News (腾讯新闻), reporting Ocean Engine's (巨量引擎) Xingtu creator festival, December 2023. https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20231203A01OOA00

Look at what that measures. The payoff belongs to the hit, not to the tier,
and no creator can promise you a hit. The fee buys an attempt.

Boost the post and the effect reaches search too.

> Xingtu seeding videos with paid boost and search terms raised search after
> viewing 49% over non-Xingtu content, and seven times with a search widget
> attached.
> Source: Beijing Daily (京报网), on Ocean Engine's 2025 search white paper, November 2025. https://news.bjd.com.cn/2025/11/25/11425198.shtml

The risk is concentration. If that one note misses, there's no second one
to fall back on.

## The budget split that works for a launch

So which do you buy first? That depends on whether anyone has posted about
your product yet.

A new product has no notes behind it. Search for its name and you find
nothing. The first job is to fill that space, and a Xiaohongshu (小红书)
sales executive named a mix for it.

> During a launch, head creators can be about 10% of the creator mix, mainly
> to set the tone. Mid-tier creators can be 40%, to make seeding more
> efficient. Lower-tier creators can be 50%, to build buzz through density.
> Source: Modern Home Appliances (现代家电网), quoting a Xiaohongshu senior sales executive for direct clients, May 2025. https://m.xdjd.cn/mArticle/542102/0/129936A75789/Article.aspx

That split counts creators, not yuan. At our rates the 10% at the top will
take a much larger share of the money. [Our influencer marketing service page](/services/influencer-marketing/)
plans a hero launch as one top-tier creator supported by 10 to 15 KOCs.

The platform's ad method builds on the same order. Xiaohongshu calls it KFS:
creator notes (K), feed ads (F), and search ads (S), in that sequence.

> Xiaohongshu's KFS method pairs strong creator content with feed ads for
> reach and search ads to capture searches. Just piling up creators and notes
> does not always work.
> Source: GeekPark (极客公园), on a Xiaohongshu Inspiration Marketing (小红书灵感营销) report, November 2022. https://www.geekpark.net/news/311320

## The budget split that works for an established account

An account with a year of notes has something a launch lacks: data. The
split should come from your own results, not from a ratio someone published.
No platform publishes a fixed split for an established brand.

Xiaohongshu (小红书) showed what that looks like at its own sales conference.

> After a test run, Vaseline raised one audience's share of its seeding budget
> from 0.7% to 17%. The product's in-store cost then fell 80%, during a big
> sale.
> Source: Caijing (财经网), via Sina Tech (新浪科技), quoting Xiaohongshu's chief marketing officer at its 2025 WILL conference, December 2024. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2024-12-19/doc-inczyxct6797065.shtml

The market as a whole has already moved down the tiers.

> In 2025, head KOLs took 13.1% of placements and 20.5% of spend. Mid-tier and
> tail creators took 86.8% of placements. On Xiaohongshu, tail creators took
> close to 20% of spend.
> Source: TopMarketing, reporting Topklout (克劳锐), June 2026. https://www.itopmarketing.com/info22850

Most advertisers still lead with KOLs, though.

> For 2025, 74% of advertisers named KOL content a social media focus, 47%
> named brand livestreams, and 37% named amateur-creator marketing.
> Source: Miaozhen (秒针系统), 2025 China digital marketing trends report, December 2024. https://www.miaozhen.com/all-reports/industryinsight/9653.html

For an established account, rank last quarter's notes by cost per save. Move
money toward the creators and formats at the top. Keep a small test budget
for new ones.

| | Launch | Established account |
|---|---|---|
| Creator mix | About 10% head, 40% mid, 50% lower tier | Set by your own cost per note |
| Top-tier KOL | One, to set the tone | Only for a new product or a big date |
| KOC seeding | Heavy, to fill empty search | Steady, to keep search fresh |
| Paid boost | On the notes that lift early | On proven notes, as ads |
| How you decide | Platform guidance and planning rates | Your own data, retested each quarter |

## Why KOC volume beats KOL reach on Xiaohongshu

Three reasons, all published.

First, search. Buyers type before they buy, and they type a lot.

> Xiaohongshu's monthly active users passed 400 million, with 800 million
> searches a day.
> Source: Beijing Business Today (北京商报), via Sina Finance (新浪财经), May 2026. https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-05-27/doc-inhziqxq9291575.shtml

Each note is one more chance to show up for those searches, and forty notes
cover far more of them than one does.

Then there's who makes the content. Mostly small accounts.

> Xiaohongshu's creator base is mostly long-tail and amateur creators, more
> than 85% of it.
> Source: Campaign China (Campaign中国), reporting Ebiquity (思媒思智), September 2025. https://www.campaignchina.com/article/ebiquity%E6%9C%80%E6%96%B0%E8%B0%83%E7%A0%94%EF%BC%9A2025%E5%B9%B4%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E7%A4%BE%E4%BA%A4kol%E8%90%A5%E9%94%80%E5%B8%82%E5%9C%BA%E5%B0%86%E5%8D%A0%E5%85%A8%E7%90%83kol%E6%8A%95%E8%B5%84%E7%9A%8458/504529

And the platform itself prices creators on fit rather than size. Its own
case above puts a matched 5,000-follower account ahead of a general one
with a million.

There is a catch. Small creators are getting more expensive.

> On Xiaohongshu, head KOL costs fell in 2025, while mid-tier, tail, and KOC
> costs rose sharply.
> Source: Campaign China (Campaign中国), reporting Ebiquity (思媒思智), September 2025. https://www.campaignchina.com/article/ebiquity%E6%9C%80%E6%96%B0%E8%B0%83%E7%A0%94%EF%BC%9A2025%E5%B9%B4%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E7%A4%BE%E4%BA%A4kol%E8%90%A5%E9%94%80%E5%B8%82%E5%9C%BA%E5%B0%86%E5%8D%A0%E5%85%A8%E7%90%83kol%E6%8A%95%E8%B5%84%E7%9A%8458/504529

Volume still wins here. Budget for the higher rates when you plan it.

## Where KOLs are still worth the money

Top creators earn their fee in four places.

**A launch that needs a tone.** The platform's own launch mix keeps a head
layer at about 10% of creators. Someone has to set the look that small
accounts then copy.

**Douyin (抖音).** Its Xingtu (巨量星图) marketplace sells creator video that
brands can boost as ads, and the hit data above comes from there. Our
planning range for a top Douyin creator runs 30,000 to 200,000+ yuan a
video. Paid posts from creators above 10,000 followers are growing fast.

> Over the past year, sponsored posts on Xingtu rose 19%. Sponsored posts by
> creators with more than 10,000 followers rose 58%.
> Source: Sina Tech (新浪科技), reporting 36Kr (36氪), February 2025. https://finance.sina.com.cn/tech/roll/2025-02-28/doc-inemzuik9845090.shtml

**Ad material.** A strong KOL video can keep running as your ad long after
it posts. On Pugongying (蒲公英) you tick ad use when you order. Skip it and the platform's
smart-creative tools for that note can't be switched on later.

**A big date.** Double 11 (双十一) and 618 pack a season of buying into a few
days. Book the KOL for the date, and seed the KOCs weeks before it.

Outside those four, a top-tier fee is hard to defend on Xiaohongshu (小红书).
Some brands have reached the same view.

> Reading NewRank's (新榜) listing filing, Yema Caijing reported that its brand
> clients increasingly swap head KOLs for cheaper KOCs, so each deal is
> shrinking.
> Source: Yema Caijing (野马财经), via Tencent News (腾讯新闻), May 2026. https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260510A06KQ900

## How to brief both without wasting the fee

A good creator can't rescue a bad brief.

A KOL brief and a KOC brief do different jobs, so they shouldn't read alike.

Brief a KOL on one idea. Give the product truth and the one message.
Add the date, then leave the format to the creator. Their audience came for
their style. Ad use, the posting window, and any exclusivity
belong in the order itself.

KOCs need the opposite: a range. Forty notes saying the same sentence look
bought, and readers notice. Give each creator a different angle: a use case, a
question buyers ask, a comparison, a routine. Ask for real photos.

[Our guide to a KOL campaign with views and no ROI](/insights/kol-campaign-no-roi/) shows what a
scripted brief costs, and how to audit the result.

On both, use the marketplace. Pugongying (蒲公英), Xiaohongshu's creator
marketplace, lets the price move before you order.

> A Pugongying creator can re-price an order to 80% to 150% of the listed price.
> The 10% fee is charged on the agreed price.
> Source: Xiaohongshu Pugongying help center (小红书蒲公英帮助中心), August 2026. https://pgy.xiaohongshu.com/help/home

On Douyin (抖音), buy KOC volume through recruitment tasks.

> Xingtu's recruitment task is built for hiring mid-tier creators and KOCs in
> bulk, from 1,000 to 50 million followers. The creator receives 0.8 times the
> client's bid.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), Xingtu service provider help center, August 2026. https://www.xingtu.cn/help-center/facilitator/140778

Label every paid note, too. The law treats a seeding note with a purchase
link as an ad.

> Paid content that sells through experience sharing or reviews, with a purchase
> link, must be clearly labeled "广告" (advertisement).
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (国家市场监督管理总局), Internet Advertising Measures, Article 9, February 2023. https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10506/202306/content_6885261.html

Last, negotiate. In 2025 the market paid less than list.

> In 2025, KOL list prices (刊例价) rose 11.6% while actual net prices fell 3.4%.
> Source: Pangjing (胖鲸), reporting R3's 2026 KOLC governance white paper, January 2026. https://pangjing.cn/6106244-2/

## FAQ

### Is KOC marketing cheaper than KOL marketing?

Per post, yes, by a wide margin. Our planning rates put a small Xiaohongshu
(小红书) account at 100 to 3,000 yuan a post. A top-tier creator plans at
15,000 to 80,000 yuan and up. Per campaign, not always. Seeding takes 20 to 50
creators, and in our worked budgets the two totals land in the same range.
KOC costs on Xiaohongshu also rose sharply in 2025.

### How many KOC posts equal one KOL post?

On price, one top-tier Xiaohongshu post equals 5 to 160 micro-KOC posts at
our planning rates. On return there is no fixed exchange rate. No platform
publishes a cost per engagement by tier. Run both, then compare cost per save
and brand searches across the same weeks.

### What budget split should a launch use?

Start with creator counts, not money. A Xiaohongshu sales executive suggested
about 10% head creators, 40% mid-tier, and 50% lower tier during a launch.
Then add feed and search ads to the notes that work, in that order. Rebalance
after the first month, using your own cost per save.
