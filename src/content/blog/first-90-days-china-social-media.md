*Everyone sells the strategy. Almost nobody publishes the schedule. This is
the timetable we hand to a board when a brand asks how to start social media
marketing in China: what happens in week 2, what is still not live in week 6,
and when the first lead arrives.*

It's written for a brand that hasn't launched yet, and the slow parts are
left in. Verification for an overseas company runs in working days, not
hours. A Chinese name needs a trademark filing that takes months to clear.
The first month of posts looks weak in the dashboard, and that's normal.

| Weeks | What happens | What is live at the end |
|---|---|---|
| Before week 1 | Documents gathered, entity question settled | Nothing yet |
| 1 to 2 | Applications filed, verification fees paid, launch note posted | Accounts live, badges still under review |
| 3 to 4 | Chinese name chosen, trademark filed, profiles localized | Verified profiles |
| 5 to 6 | Content pillars set, first calendar approved | A month of content in the queue |
| 7 to 8 | The full calendar starts | Posts, and small numbers |
| 9 to 12 | First paid tests, first creator seeding | Search traffic and the first leads |

Every wait below comes from a platform's own help pages, the trademark
office, or our own process, and each is cited where it appears. All sources
were checked in September 2026.

## Before week one: the four documents to have ready

The clock starts when the documents are ready, not when the contract is
signed. Four items cover most of what the platforms ask an overseas company
for, and the same four come back at every annual review.

Tencent's help center lists what an overseas entity needs to verify a
[WeChat (微信)](/platforms/wechat/) Official Account.

> An overseas entity verifying a WeChat account needs its company
> registration certificate or business license, a color scan of both sides
> of the contact person's ID, an application letter, an operation
> authorization letter, the contact person's phone bill (or a stamped office
> phone bill or bank statement), and a trademark certificate if the account
> is named after a trademark.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), overseas entity verification materials, confirmed September 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/190723Mjey67190723ey6Fbi.html

[Xiaohongshu (小红书)](/platforms/rednote/) takes the same kind of file and
adds a translation rule.

> Xiaohongshu enterprise accounts accept applications from companies outside
> mainland China. The form asks for entity information, an application
> letter, supplementary documents, and operator details. Any document not in
> Chinese or English needs a translation stamped by a translation company.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) enterprise account verification guide, platform document, confirmed September 2026. https://fe-video-qc.xhscdn.com/picasso-editor/7072d509ee7370545a056b6f9030a926e8879dca.pdf

[Douyin (抖音)](/platforms/douyin/) is the exception. Its own help pages
describe the enterprise account as a product for companies in China.

> Douyin's FAQ describes the enterprise account as an account that companies
> in China can apply to verify.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), "What is a Douyin enterprise account," May 2022. https://www.oceanengine.com/faq/douyin-qyh-kslj.html

> Douyin enterprise verification asks for a business license and an
> application letter, costs 600 yuan for the first year and 120 yuan a year
> after that, and takes about two working days to review.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), Douyin Blue V verification FAQ, May 2022. https://www.oceanengine.com/faq/douyin-qyh-zmrzn.html

So a pre-entry brand opens Douyin through a mainland entity it already has,
or leaves it for a later phase. Ask your agency which applies before the
launch date is set.

| Document | WeChat Official Account | Xiaohongshu enterprise account | Douyin enterprise account |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company registration certificate | Overseas certificate accepted | Overseas certificate accepted | Chinese business license |
| Contact or operator ID | Both sides, color scan | Operator details on the form | Legal representative ID for sole traders |
| Application letter | Chinese or English template | Platform template | Platform template |
| Operation authorization letter | Required | Not listed | Not listed |
| Phone bill or bank statement | Required | Not listed | Not listed |
| Stamped translation | Not listed | For documents not in Chinese or English | Not listed |

Have four things scanned in color before week one: the registration
certificate, the contact person's ID, the two signed letters, and a phone
bill or bank statement that ties the contact person to the company.
Everything else is platform-specific and small.

## Weeks 1 to 2: how to start social media marketing in China from abroad

The first decision is which entity applies. WeChat (微信) and Xiaohongshu
(小红书) accept an overseas company. Douyin (抖音), as above, is built for a
Chinese one. Settle this in week one, because it decides which accounts can
be live in week two.

Then the applications go in and the platform clock starts. Tencent publishes
its overseas review time.

> An overseas entity pays a one-time review fee of 99 US dollars per
> application, and the review is completed within 7 to 15 working days after
> complete documents are submitted. Payment is by WeChat Pay or by card.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), overseas entity verification FAQ, confirmed September 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/190723aQvMR31907233IF7FV.html

Three weeks at the long end. A mainland company moves faster on the same
platform.

> For a mainland entity, WeChat verification review takes 1 to 3 working
> days, and verification must be completed within 30 calendar days of
> registration.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), Official Account registration review time, confirmed September 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/120911VrYVrA1312123qyyqI.html

Two more WeChat rules catch foreign teams. An overseas entity gets one
account, and it has to be a service account.

> Overseas entities can register one account on the WeChat Official Account
> platform, and only the service account type is open to them. The account
> becomes usable after verification.
> Source: Tencent customer service (腾讯客服), overseas entity service account registration, confirmed September 2026. https://kf.qq.com/faq/180312zQjeYV180312FNNbmA.html

Xiaohongshu publishes no review time. What it publishes is a deadline, a fee,
and a rule about where to apply.

> Xiaohongshu enterprise verification costs 600 yuan per application, is
> valid for one year with an annual review, and each order expires after 30
> days. Overseas entities, annual reviews, and renames go through the
> desktop site, not the app.
> Source: Xiaohongshu (小红书) enterprise account verification guide, platform document, confirmed September 2026. https://fe-video-qc.xhscdn.com/picasso-editor/7072d509ee7370545a056b6f9030a926e8879dca.pdf

We don't publish our own turnaround figures per platform. In our experience
the long end of Tencent's range is the safe one to put in a board paper, and
a Xiaohongshu order for an overseas company clears well inside its 30 days
when the translations are stamped.

Our own process promises two weeks from contract to first post.

> TheRedScroll's process is a 30-minute discovery call, a fixed-price
> proposal, and live in two weeks: onboarding, accounts set up, first
> content published, fourteen days from signed contract to first post going
> live.
> Source: TheRedScroll homepage, September 2026. https://www.theredscroll.com/

That promise holds when the documents are ready on day one, which is why the
section above comes first. The first post is a launch note on Xiaohongshu,
where the account can post while its verification runs. The full calendar
waits for the pillars in week 5. The WeChat badge can land in week three if
Tencent takes the long end (the account exists from registration, the badge
is what's waiting), and nothing is lost while it does.

## Weeks 3 to 4: brand localization and the Chinese name problem

While the reviews run, the naming work starts. Chinese users search in
Chinese. A brand with no Chinese name has no search presence on Xiaohongshu
(小红书) or Baidu (百度), and whatever name resellers or customers have
already been using fills the gap.

A good name sounds close to the original and means something that fits the
category. It also has to be free to register, and that is the slow part.

> China's trademark office reported that the average trademark examination
> period held steady at four months.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration (国家知识产权局), 2026 national conference work report, January 2026. https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2026/1/7/art_53_203564.html

Four months is examination only. Publication and registration add more. So
the name is chosen in week 3, filed in week 4, and protected some time after
day 90. The accounts launch under it anyway. Both platform guides quoted
above let an account name follow the registered company name or a trademark,
so a brand without a Chinese trademark launches on its registered name and
carries the Chinese name in the profile and the content until the filing
clears.

Localization in these two weeks also covers the profile: a bio that reads
like a Chinese brand statement rather than a translated tagline, a customer
service contact on WeChat (微信) or WeCom (企业微信), and the compliance
lines your category requires in the profile text. Dull work, and the first
customer who lands on the page checks all of it.

## Weeks 5 to 6: content pillars and the first calendar

Beyond the launch note and the profile, nothing is published yet. That's
deliberate. Weeks 5 and 6 are when the first month of content gets built,
and building it before the calendar starts is what keeps week 7 from
stalling.

Three or four pillars are enough. For a consumer brand on Xiaohongshu
(小红书), the pillars we use most are the search-shaped how-to, the
comparison, the behind-the-scenes, and the customer's own note reposted with
permission. For a B2B brand on WeChat (微信), it's the case story, the
explainer, the event, and the product update. Each pillar gets a cadence, and
the calendar shows twelve to sixteen pieces for the first publishing month.

Two things a Western content team usually gets wrong here. The first is the
look. Polished global creative gets throttled on Xiaohongshu, and phone
photography of the product in a real Chinese setting does not. Our guide to
[Xiaohongshu marketing for foreign brands](/insights/xiaohongshu-marketing-foreign-brands/)
shows what the dashboard looks like when that happens.

The second is approvals. A piece that needs headquarters sign-off in another
time zone takes a week to clear, every time. Agree the approval rule in week
5, not week 7.

## Weeks 7 to 8: publishing starts, and why the numbers look bad

The calendar starts. The numbers will be small. Two reasons.

On Xiaohongshu (小红书), a new account's notes reach the discovery feed
first, and feed traffic dies in about three days. Search traffic, the kind
that keeps a note alive for months, builds only once the platform has seen
several notes on the same topic from the same account. In the client
dashboards we run, that takes three to four weeks of steady posting.

On WeChat (微信), a service account's pushes reach the people who already
follow it. In week 7 that is your own staff and a handful of early
customers. The read count on the first push is not a verdict on the content.
It's a headcount.

What to watch instead of followers: saves and comments per note on
Xiaohongshu, the share of reads that come from search, and reads per
follower on each WeChat push. Those three tell you by week 8 whether the
pillars are right. Follower count tells you nothing yet.

## Weeks 9 to 12: first paid tests and first creator seeding

By week 9 the dashboard shows which notes earn search traffic. Spend there,
not on new ones.

Two things start. The first is seeding on Xiaohongshu (小红书): 20 to 40
key opinion consumers (KOC), ordinary users with a few thousand followers,
post their own notes about the product through the platform's creator
marketplace, [Pugongying (蒲公英)](/services/influencer-marketing/), so that
a search for the category returns real people using it. The second is a paid
test behind the two or three notes that already proved themselves.

For a foreign company, the ads go through an agent.

> Since February 2, 2026, overseas merchants gain access to Xiaohongshu's
> Jiguang ad platform only through an authorized agent, with no self-serve
> account opening.
> Source: Ziyouxing Studio (自由行Studio), Xiaohongshu Jiguang overseas guide, August 2026. https://zyxstudio.net/articles/xiaohongshu-jiguang-guide.html

So the agent's account opening belongs in the week 9 plan, and the deposit
it sets is ad spend, billed at cost with the platform's invoice attached.
What the seeding and the test cost is covered in our guide to
[how much China social media marketing costs in 2026](/insights/china-social-media-marketing-cost/).
TheRedScroll's own rate card is on its [pricing page](/pricing/).

The first lead usually arrives in this window. Someone searched the
category, read a seeded note and then a brand note, and messaged the account
or the WeCom (企业微信) contact in the profile. It rarely looks like much
in the inbox.

## What good looks like at day 90

Not a follower number. This is the checklist we use.

| Check | Good at day 90 | Not yet |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts | WeChat and Xiaohongshu verified, Douyin decided | Any account still under review |
| Name | Chinese name in use, trademark filed | Name still in committee |
| Content | Twelve or more pieces a month, on schedule | Posting when approvals allow |
| Search | Search is the largest traffic source on the best notes | All traffic from the feed |
| Seeding | First creator notes live and reported through Pugongying | Unreported creator posts |
| Paid | One paid test with a cost per read to compare against month four | No test yet |
| Leads | First inbound messages routed to a named person | Messages waiting for days |

The line that matters most is search. When a note earns most of its reads
from search, the account has started to compound, and the plan for month
four is mostly to feed it.

## The three things that most often delay a launch

Documents arrive one at a time. A missing phone bill or an unsigned
authorization letter restarts a review. Tencent's range only starts when the
file is complete, and the 30-day order window on Xiaohongshu (小红书)
doesn't pause. Gather the four documents before week one.

The second delay is the name, when it goes to committee. A naming decision
that sits with a global brand team for six weeks pushes everything from week
3 to week 9. Give it to two people and a deadline.

Approvals are the third, and they cross time zones. Content that needs
headquarters sign-off for every piece ships late and reads late by the time
it lands. A pre-approved pillar with local sign-off on each piece is what
keeps the week 7 date.

None of these three delays happens in China. They happen at headquarters.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to launch on Chinese social media?

Two weeks from signed contract to first post, when the documents are ready
on day one. That is the timeline on our [homepage](/) and the one this
schedule is built on. Verification badges can land later: Tencent quotes 7
to 15 working days for an overseas entity. Ninety days is how long it takes
for search traffic and the first leads to show up.

### What documents do I need to open accounts?

Four items cover most of it: the company registration certificate, the
contact person's ID scanned in color on both sides, a signed application
letter plus an operation authorization letter, and a phone bill or bank
statement tied to the contact person. Xiaohongshu (小红书) adds a stamped
translation for anything not in Chinese or English. Douyin (抖音) asks for a
Chinese business license.

### When should I expect the first results?

Small numbers in weeks 7 and 8, search traffic on the best notes by week 10,
and the first inbound lead somewhere in weeks 9 to 12. Follower counts stay
low for the whole first quarter, and that is not a problem. Judge the launch
on the share of reads that come from search and on whether messages get
answered the same day.
