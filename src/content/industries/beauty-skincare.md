*Most brands arrive at a beauty marketing agency in China with a launch date
and a deck full of campaign ideas. The launch date is usually wrong, because
nobody told them a product has to clear the regulator before a single review
can name it.*

Beauty in China is a filing problem, a claims problem and a search problem,
in that order. The content only works once the first two are settled. We have
watched good launches slip a quarter because nobody read the regulation until
the copy was already approved.

| Gate | Who says yes | What it blocks until it is done |
|---|---|---|
| Product filing or registration | National Medical Products Administration | Legal import, and naming the product in copy |
| Domestic responsible person | You appoint one, the regulator records it | The filing itself |
| Claim evidence | You, published on the regulator's site | Every efficacy line in every post |
| Verified accounts and store | Xiaohongshu, Douyin, WeChat | Paid reach, the shop, creator briefs |

Every rule below comes from the regulation, the law or a platform's own
documentation, and each is cited where it appears. All sources were re-checked
in September 2026. We review this page every quarter, because Chinese platform
rules move faster than the regulations do.

## What a beauty marketing agency in China checks before the first post

China splits cosmetics in two, and the split decides your timeline.

> Cosmetics for hair dye, perming, spot removal and whitening, sun
> protection and hair loss prevention, plus any cosmetic claiming a new
> efficacy, are special cosmetics. Everything else is ordinary.
> Source: State Council, Cosmetics Supervision and Administration Regulation (化妆品监督管理条例), Order 727, Article 16, in force January 2021. https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2020-06/29/content_5522593.htm

Special cosmetics have to be registered with the National Medical Products
Administration (国家药监局) before they can be produced or imported. Imported
ordinary cosmetics only have to be filed with the same body. A whitening
serum and a cleansing balm are not on the same schedule, and a brand that
plans both together will miss one of them.

You also cannot do it alone from outside.

> An overseas registrant or filer must appoint a legal entity inside China to
> handle registration and filing, help with adverse reaction monitoring and
> carry out product recalls.
> Source: Cosmetics Supervision and Administration Regulation, Article 23, in force January 2021. https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2020-06/29/content_5522593.htm

Then the claims. This is the part that catches good brands, because in China
the evidence behind a claim isn't private. You publish it.

> Efficacy claims must have sufficient scientific basis, and the registrant
> or filer must publish a summary of the literature, research data or
> efficacy evaluation behind them on a website designated by the regulator,
> open to public scrutiny.
> Source: Cosmetics Supervision and Administration Regulation, Article 22, in force January 2021. https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2020-06/29/content_5522593.htm

So a claim your home market treats as marketing copy is, in China, a testing
invoice and a public file. The method for that testing is its own document.

> The Cosmetic Efficacy Claim Evaluation Specification (化妆品功效宣称评价规范)
> was issued by the National Medical Products Administration and took effect
> on May 1, 2021.
> Source: China Center for Food and Drug International Exchange (中国食品药品国际交流中心), announcement 2021 No. 50, April 2021. https://www.ccfdie.org/cn/yjxx/hzp/webinfo/2021/04/1614390286444040.htm

Advertising law then sits on top of all of it. It's blunt.

> Advertisements may not use terms such as "national level", "highest level"
> or "best".
> Source: Advertising Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 9, in force since September 2015. https://www.gov.cn/guoqing/2021-10/29/content_5647620.htm

> Outside medical, drug and medical device advertising, no advertisement may
> refer to disease treatment or use medical language that could confuse the
> product with a drug or a device.
> Source: Advertising Law, Article 17. https://www.gov.cn/guoqing/2021-10/29/content_5647620.htm

Neither rule is enforced gently, and the fine is written into the law itself.

> Publishing an advertisement that breaches Article 9 draws a fine of 200,000
> to 1,000,000 yuan on the advertiser, and in serious cases the business
> licence can be revoked.
> Source: Advertising Law, Article 57. https://www.gov.cn/guoqing/2021-10/29/content_5647620.htm

| Do not write | Why it fails | What works instead |
|---|---|---|
| Repairs the skin barrier | Reads as a medical effect, Advertising Law 17 | Made for skin that reacts easily |
| Best sunscreen in Japan | Superlative banned outright, Advertising Law 9 | The SPF number, and where it was tested |
| Whitening, on an unfiled product | Whitening is a special cosmetic, Regulation 16 | Texture, finish and how it is used, until registration lands |
| 97% of users saw results | Cited data must be true and sourced, Advertising Law 11 | The same figure with panel size, date and testing house |
| Clinically proven, with nothing filed | The evidence summary is public, Regulation 22 | The named test, and a pointer to the published summary |

## How a beauty product actually gets recommended

No brand controls the organic route to scale on Xiaohongshu (小红书). What a
brand does control is whether its paid creator work is declared. Since 2023
that has not been optional.

> Where goods or services are promoted through knowledge sharing, experience
> sharing or product reviews with a purchase link attached, the publisher
> must conspicuously label the content as an advertisement.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation, Internet Advertising Measures (互联网广告管理办法), Order 72, Article 9, in force May 2023. https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10506/202306/content_6885261.html

On Xiaohongshu that declaration runs through Pugongying, the platform's own
creator marketplace. It has a price.

> Pugongying charges 10% of the deal in standard mode and 20% in the premium
> mode that adds platform promotion.
> Source: Niaoge Biji (鸟哥笔记), October 2022. https://www.niaogebiji.com/article-482538-1.html

Beauty carries a paperwork rule most categories do not.

> For cross-border trade on Xiaohongshu, imported cosmetics require the
> import cosmetics filing, and brand authorization chains are capped at three
> levels, or two for beauty.
> Source: Xiaohongshu merchant qualification requirements (小红书资质入驻要求), platform documentation, confirmed September 2026. https://picasso-static.xiaohongshu.com/test/8b3bc7324ca06fceea379177f9eed1fa/小红书资质入驻要求.pdf

Two levels, for beauty specifically. Say your product reaches China through a
distributor, who sells to a trading company, who sells to the store. That's
three. The store application fails, and no amount of creator spend fixes it.
Check the paperwork chain before anyone books a creator.

## How many KOC posts a launch actually needs

Nobody publishes a benchmark for this. The platform doesn't release one, and
anyone quoting a single national number is guessing.

The number comes out of search coverage. Pick the Chinese phrases a buyer
types before choosing. Count how many notes it takes for yours to hold the
first screen on each. Multiply. That's the seeding plan, and it's arithmetic
rather than a rule of thumb.

| Launch size | Search terms to own | Notes per term | Where the plan lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| One hero product, one city | 3 to 5 | 4 to 6 | Roughly 12 to 30 notes |
| Full range, national | 10 to 15 | 4 to 6 | Roughly 40 to 90 notes |
| Category claim, sustained | 20 or more | 6 or more | 120 and up, continuing |

Treat that as planning arithmetic, not a benchmark. The inputs are yours: your
terms, how contested each one is, your budget. Two brands in the same
subcategory can land three times apart and both be right.

Creator fees move the total more than volume does.

> Nano KOC accounts with 1,000 to 5,000 followers run about 20 to 60 US
> dollars per post, while micro KOL work under 1 million followers runs about
> 10,000 to 35,000 US dollars per campaign.
> Source: Long Advisory, June 2026. https://longadvisory.eu/en/kol-vs-koc-marketing-in-china-which-delivers-better-roi/

## Douyin live commerce: when it pays and when it does not

Douyin (抖音) sells beauty at volume. It also moves the legal risk onto the
person doing the talking.

> A seller promoting goods through internet livestream, where this amounts to
> commercial advertising, takes on the duties of an advertiser. Livestream
> room operators and livestream marketing staff take on the duties of an
> advertising operator and publisher when commissioned to do so.
> Source: Internet Advertising Measures, Order 72, Article 19, in force May 2023. https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10506/202306/content_6885261.html

That matters more in beauty than anywhere else, because the words that sell
on camera are the words Article 17 of the Advertising Law forbids. A host who
says a serum treats acne has just made your brand the advertiser behind an
illegal claim.

Verification is cheap. Getting the script right is not.

> Douyin enterprise verification costs 600 yuan for the first year and 120
> yuan a year to renew, with review in about two working days.
> Source: Ocean Engine (巨量引擎), Douyin enterprise account FAQ, May 2022. https://www.oceanengine.com/faq/douyin-qyh-zmrzn.html

Live commerce pays for beauty brands with three things already in place: a
store that can take the order, enough search coverage that viewers who leave
can find them again, and a script a lawyer has read. Miss one and a
livestream becomes an expensive way to hand traffic to a competitor.

One case does justify going to Douyin first: color cosmetics that demonstrate
well on camera, at a price a viewer decides on in ninety seconds. Those sell
before they ever build a search footprint. Skincare almost never behaves that
way. Our [Douyin platform page](/platforms/douyin/) covers the format in more
detail.
